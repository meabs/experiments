# Accounts service FMEA

Assessment date: 2026-11-04  
Scope: the Spring Boot accounts API, its scheduled payment work, persistence, security boundary, and the four
integrations on which it depends. The assessment uses the repository implementation, tests, `README.md`, and the
knowledge bundle in `okf/`.

This is a current-state FMEA. Scores are on a 1–10 scale: severity (S) is 1 = negligible and 10 = customer funds,
regulatory, or tenant-confidentiality impact; occurrence (O) is 1 = rare and 10 = frequent/continuously present; and
detection (D) is 1 = almost certain detection before customer impact and 10 = unlikely to be detected before impact.
RPN = S × O × D. The scores are relative prioritisation aids, not estimates of incident probability.

## Failure modes

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers, dependent systems, and teams | Detection / current controls | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F1 | An accepted payment is not represented locally, or is later sent again | The scheme can accept an instruction and then time out. `PaymentDispatcher` does not catch `SchemeTimeoutException`; the transaction rolls back, although the scheme reference may already have been accepted. | Customer sees an error or no payment record while the payee may be paid. A retry can create a duplicate payment; finance reconciliation and complaints handling lose the authoritative local trail. | Scheme timeout logs/HTTP errors; no instruction-status query, durable outbox, or alert for accepted-but-unknown instructions. | 10 | 4 | 8 | **320** | Catch timeouts as `SUBMITTED/UNKNOWN`, persist before/around the call, query scheme status by the same `P{id}` reference, reconcile unknowns, and make retry/resubmission use the same reference. Add an integration test for timeout-after-acceptance. |
| F2 | An unscreened payment is sent | The fraud client exists but `PaymentService` never calls it. This violates FRD-7; the required fail-closed behavior is therefore absent. | A fraudulent or sanctioned payment can reach the scheme; customers, the fraud team, compliance, and the bank carry financial, regulatory, and reputational exposure. | There is no screening decision in the payment state or audit trail. Existing fraud fakes test behavior that is not wired into the production flow. | 10 | 10 | 7 | **700** | Release-blocking: call screening before scheme submission, persist `ACCEPT`, `REFER` as `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, and `REJECT` as opaque `FRAUD`; treat timeout/error as hold. Alert on any payment sent without a decision and test all three decisions plus unavailable behavior. |
| F3 | A ledger posting is duplicated or its result is misreported | A ledger timeout may mean “booked”. The dispatcher creates a random `ledgerReference` on each dispatch; an operational retry with a new reference can debit twice. | Customer can be debited twice; the scheme may already have paid once. Finance's 07:00 reference reconciliation breaks and operations must investigate exceptions. | `POSTING_FAILED` and a warning log exist; the daily exception report is expected operationally, but no retry workflow or idempotency assertion is implemented. | 10 | 5 | 6 | **300** | Generate and persist `ledgerReference` before the first post, reuse it for every retry, query/reconcile ledger status where possible, and expose an auditable repair action. Alert on aged `POSTING_FAILED` items and test timeout-before/after-booking. |
| F4 | A customer is told a payment was sent when ledger posting failed | After a ledger timeout the payment is marked `POSTING_FAILED`, but `notifySent` still sends `PAYMENT_SENT`. | Customer receives a false confirmation; support and complaints teams must correct the record, while the ledger and service views disagree. | Payment status is visible if queried; notification delivery is not correlated with status in metrics. | 8 | 5 | 5 | **200** | Notify `PAYMENT_FAILED` for posting failure (or explicitly “pending”), never `PAYMENT_SENT` unless the state is `COMPLETED`; add notification/status contract tests. |
| F5 | The 06:00 queue dispatches a payment twice | `QueuedPaymentJob` has no distributed lock or claim/lease. Multiple service instances, overlapping runs, or a retry can dispatch the same `QUEUED` row concurrently; each dispatch generates a new ledger reference. | Same payment can be submitted twice and debited twice. Customers, scheme settlement, finance reconciliation, and operations all receive conflicting records. | Scheme reference `P{id}` is safe for 30 days, but the ledger reference is not; no job-run, claim, or duplicate-dispatch metric exists. | 10 | 4 | 7 | **280** | Atomically claim rows with a lease/status transition, use a distributed scheduler lock or a single worker, keep stable scheme and ledger idempotency keys, and alert on duplicate attempts and stale `QUEUED` rows. |
| F6 | Concurrent payment requests overspend an account or bypass its daily limit | Balance and `paidToday` are checked on a copied entity without locking, reservation, idempotency key, or database constraint. Two requests can pass before either debit is committed. | Customer can spend more than available or the daily limit; the local balance, ledger, and scheme diverge. Finance and support need manual correction. | Reconciliation may reveal the break after the fact; there is no concurrency metric or invariant check. | 9 | 5 | 7 | **315** | Serialize per account (pessimistic lock or optimistic version with retry), reserve funds atomically, enforce a server-side idempotency key for client retries, and test parallel requests. |
| F7 | A payment is queued for a date the scheme rejects | `WorkingDays` excludes weekends only and does not know UK bank holidays. The scheme rejects a holiday instruction with `DT01`. | Payment is delayed or rejected unexpectedly; customer promise, scheme operations, and support communications are wrong. | A scheme rejection is stored as `REJECTED/DT01`; no calendar freshness check or alert identifies the systemic cause. | 7 | 5 | 5 | **175** | Use a maintained UK bank-holiday calendar (with a fail-safe for stale data), calculate the next scheme working day, and alert on holiday-related `DT01` spikes. |
| F8 | The customer-facing balance is stale or incorrect | The balance is a copy overwritten from the ledger at 02:00, while daytime debits are local. The refresh job is not present in this repository and closure must not rely on the copy. | App/online banking shows the wrong balance; payment decisions can be wrong. Customers may be told they have funds they do not have, and support/finance see inconsistent books. | Ledger reconciliation and the next 02:00 refresh can reveal drift; no freshness timestamp, age alert, or mismatch endpoint is exposed. | 9 | 6 | 6 | **324** | Make ledger balance refresh durable and observable, store freshness/source metadata, fail or degrade safely when stale, reconcile after every payment, and use a live ledger check for all exact-money decisions. |
| F9 | An account is closed without the required approval or with funds remaining | The repository implements creating and listing closure requests, but no approval/decision endpoint or close operation. There is also no ledger zero-balance check in the closure flow. | Closure can be stuck indefinitely, or a future implementation could strand customer funds if it trusts the copied balance. Approvers, operations, complaints, and regulatory teams lack a complete audit trail. | Pending requests are listable; there is no SLA alert, approval completion metric, or invariant that a closed account had a zero ledger balance. | 10 | 6 | 9 | **540** | Release-blocking: implement maker-checker approval, prevent self-approval, query the ledger immediately before closing, enforce zero balance and no in-flight payments, audit every decision, and alert on aged pending requests. |
| F10 | Monthly statements or the regulatory return are missing or wrong | Statement generation from ledger closing balances and the 5th-working-day regulatory workflow are not implemented in this service/repository. | Customers receive incomplete statements; Finance/regulatory reporting submits an incorrect or late return; complaints evidence is incomplete. | Statement rows can be queried, but there is no generation job, completeness check, deadline alert, or ledger-to-statement reconciliation. | 9 | 6 | 9 | **486** | Assign an owned statement/return job, generate from an immutable ledger extract, reconcile account population and totals, alert before the 5th working day, and retain a rerunnable audit trail. |
| F11 | Tenant or role isolation fails and data is disclosed or changed | The model relies on claims and application-level `AccessPolicy`; a bad identity claim, future endpoint, query, or regression can omit a tenant predicate. Staff-only fields also require endpoint-by-endpoint filtering. | One bank's customer/account data or internal notes/risk data can be exposed to another tenant or customer; this is a reportable confidentiality incident and undermines every dependent team. | 401/403/404 conventions and tenant checks exist; there are no production authorization audit events, cross-tenant canary tests, or query-level tenant constraints. | 10 | 3 | 8 | **240** | Add mandatory tenant-scoped repositories/row-level defense, validate issuer and tenant claims, centralize response DTO filtering, log authorization decisions without sensitive data, and add exhaustive cross-tenant/role tests to the release gate. |
| F12 | Sensitive bank details are returned or logged beyond their intended audience | `PaymentView` returns payee sort code and account number on payment reads. Notification code correctly limits its own message to the last four digits, but API responses and logs have no equivalent minimisation policy. | A customer or staff client can receive data it does not need; downstream app logs, traces, screenshots, or support exports increase data-protection exposure. | Code review can spot the fields; there is no response schema scan, DLP alert, or access audit for these values. | 8 | 4 | 8 | **256** | Minimise payment DTOs (mask or omit account details), classify fields, redact structured logs/traces, add DLP/schema tests, and review existing consumers before changing the contract. |
| F13 | Service data disappears or resets after restart/failover | Production configuration uses an in-memory H2 database (`jdbc:h2:mem:accounts;DB_CLOSE_DELAY=-1`) with `create-drop` schema and fixture initialization. | Accounts, payments, cases, closure records, statements, and audit evidence are lost or revert to fixtures. Customers and all dependent teams lose the system of record view. | Startup/health may be green; there is no persistence durability, backup/restore test, or data-loss alarm. | 10 | 7 | 3 | **210** | Release-blocking: use a durable, highly available production database with migrations, backups, point-in-time recovery, restore drills, and startup guards that fail if a non-production H2 profile is used. |
| F14 | A database or external dependency outage becomes broad API failure | Calls have synchronous timeouts, little isolation, and the exception handler does not provide a general dependency-failure contract. Scheme timeout is not caught; a notification failure is caught, but other integration errors can surface as 5xx. | Payment requests fail or become ambiguous; account/support reads and writes may be unavailable. App, back-office, fraud, finance, and operations queues back up. | Basic request errors and warning logs; no dependency health, latency/error-rate SLOs, circuit breakers, bulkheads, or queue-depth alerts are configured. | 8 | 5 | 6 | **240** | Add dependency health/readiness checks, metrics and tracing by dependency/reference, circuit breakers/bulkheads, bounded retries only for idempotent calls, graceful read-only/deferred behavior, and an incident runbook. |
| F15 | Notification is missing, duplicated, or contains the wrong payment state | Notifications are best effort, have no retry or outbox, and the dispatcher sends `PAYMENT_SENT` even for `POSTING_FAILED`. | Customer may not know a payment is queued, under review, failed, or completed; support receives avoidable contacts. A false message can cause a customer to act on incorrect information. | A warning is logged when sending throws; no durable delivery record, retry queue, deduplication, or customer-notification reconciliation exists. | 6 | 5 | 6 | **180** | Use an outbox keyed by payment/state, a notification worker with bounded retry and deduplication, state-specific templates, and delivery/failure dashboards. Preserve the rule that notifications never contain full account numbers. |
| F16 | Fraud holds are not reviewed within four hours | Even after fraud wiring, `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` has no production workflow, review callback, deadline queue, or escalation in this repository. | Legitimate payments are delayed; suspicious payments may remain unresolved. Fraud team workload and customer support complaints increase, and the FRD-7 control cannot be evidenced. | The intended status is documented, but there is no held-payment inbox, age metric, or four-hour alert. | 8 | 6 | 8 | **384** | Persist the screening decision and hold deadline, publish to the fraud team's tool, receive an auditable decision callback, alert at four hours, and reconcile held items with no owner/status. |

## Priority and release recommendation

The highest current RPNs are F2 (fraud bypass, 700), F9 (incomplete closure control, 540), F10 (statement/regulatory
gap, 486), F16 (unmanaged fraud holds, 384), F8 (stale balance, 324), F6 (concurrent overspend, 315), and F1
(scheme timeout ambiguity, 320). F2 is an always-present control failure rather than a merely hypothetical outage.

Before the next release, the minimum go/no-go gates should be:

1. Wire fraud screening and fail closed; make the fraud hold/review SLA observable (F2, F16).
2. Make scheme and ledger operations durable and idempotent across timeouts, retries, and multiple workers; serialize
   balance/limit decisions (F1, F3, F5, F6).
3. Remove in-memory persistence from any production profile and prove backup/restore (F13).
4. Complete maker-checker closure with a live ledger zero-balance check, and establish statement/regulatory ownership
   and deadline monitoring (F9, F10).
5. Add tenant-isolation, sensitive-response, dependency, and payment-state contract tests plus metrics and alerts so
   the controls can be detected before customer impact (F11, F12, F14, F15).

The existing offline test suite passes, but it primarily proves the current API behavior with fakes; it does not prove
the production fraud wiring, multi-instance scheduling, durable storage, timeout reconciliation, or operational alerts
identified above.
