# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its scheduled payment worker, the
customer-facing account data, and the scheme, fraud, ledger and notification
integrations. It is based on the code and the repository knowledge bundle as of
2026-11-04. The scores describe the current release candidate, not the residual
risk after the recommended actions are implemented.

Scores use the conventional FMEA direction:

- **Severity (S):** 1 = negligible inconvenience; 10 = material financial loss,
  customer harm, regulatory breach or loss of financial control.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 = exceptional; 10 = expected or frequent under normal
  operation.
- **Detection (D):** 1 = almost certain to be detected immediately by an
  automated control; 10 = unlikely to be detected before customer or downstream
  impact.

`RPN = S × O × D`. RPN is a prioritisation aid; any severity 9–10 item requires
  explicit release acceptance even when its RPN is relatively low.

| FMEA-01 | Customer/account/payment data disappears or reverts after restart | Production datasource is H2 in-memory (`DB_CLOSE_DELAY=-1`) with startup fixtures; there is no durable database or recovery path in the service | Accounts, transactions, notes, cases and payment history can vanish; payment status shown to a customer can disagree with what was sent | Complaints and regulatory reporting lose records; finance reconciliation cannot join scheme/ledger activity to a payment; operations cannot work exceptions | Database restart/health checks may be green while data is gone; only reconciliation, support queries or customer reports expose it | 10 | 3 | 9 | 270 | Block production release until a durable HA database, migrations, backups, restore tests and startup/readiness checks are in place. | FMEA-02 | Payment is sent without fraud screening | `PaymentService.create` dispatches directly; the `FraudScreeningClient` exists but is not called, despite FRD-7 requiring screening before every payment | A fraudulent or sanctioned payment may be sent; a legitimate payment may also bypass the required hold/reject decision | Fraud team has no review item or screening result; compliance, finance and operations face an audit and remediation issue | There is no per-payment screening result or alert; downstream fraud/settlement review may find it after the fact | 10 | 6 | 9 | 540 | Wire screening before scheme submission. | FMEA-03 | Scheme accepts a payment but the service reports an error and loses its local record | Scheme timeout may mean accepted; `SchemeTimeoutException` is not caught and the surrounding transaction rolls back. | FMEA-04 | Ledger posting is booked twice or remains unresolved | Ledger timeout is ambiguous; recovery is manual, and an automatic/manual retry could create a new `ledgerReference`. | FMEA-05 | Payment creation is duplicated on client retry | No idempotency key or uniqueness constraint covers a customer request; a timeout after persistence or a mobile retry creates another payment | Customer can send the same instruction twice and may be debited twice | Scheme receives separate references (`P` + different IDs); finance gets multiple settlements and support must unwind the duplicate | Duplicate customer references/amounts may be noticed by fraud or reconciliation, but there is no deterministic request-level signal | 9 | 5 | 8 | 360 | Require an idempotency key, scope it to tenant/customer, persist it with a unique constraint, and return the original payment for repeats. | FMEA-06 | Concurrent payments overspend or exceed the daily limit | Balance and `paidToday` are read before debit with no account lock, atomic conditional update or serialisation; two requests can pass the same checks | Customer can spend more than the available balance or bank limit; later ledger synchronisation may produce an unexpected balance | Core ledger/reconciliation sees an overdrawn or limit-breaching set of postings; support and fraud teams must investigate | Daily ledger balance overwrite and reconciliation may reveal it after both payments were sent; no local contention metric exists | 9 | 5 | 8 | 360 | Lock the account row or use an atomic reservation/conditional debit, enforce the daily total transactionally, and add concurrency tests plus an alert for negative/reserved balance. |
| FMEA-07 | Queued payment is missed, dispatched twice, or dispatched on a bank holiday | Job runs only at 06:00 Monday–Friday; there is no durable claim/lock, retry/dead-letter handling or job-run monitoring; `WorkingDays` does not know bank holidays | Payment is late, or in a duplicate case is sent twice; the customer receives an incorrect status or timing | Scheme may reject a bank-holiday instruction with `DT01`; operations must find stale `QUEUED` items; finance sees delayed or duplicate settlement | The daily exceptions report can find overdue queue items, but it is described as an operational check rather than an automated alert; duplicate dispatch is found by reconciliation | 9 | 6 | 8 | 432 | Use a durable scheduler/queue with lease/claim semantics and idempotent dispatch, model UK bank holidays, retry safely, and alert on missed runs and overdue queue items. Notify customers of final outcome. |
| FMEA-08 | Fraud-screening outage blocks all new payments without a controlled customer outcome | Screening correctly fails closed after 2 seconds, but there is no persisted pending/hold path in the payment creation flow and no queue/back-pressure or service-level alert described | Legitimate payments fail or time out rather than becoming reviewable; customers cannot tell whether to retry safely | Fraud team receives no `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` item; contact centre volume rises and operations cannot distinguish unavailable screening from rejected payments | Client errors/latency can be logged, but payment-level state and customer communication are absent; impact is detected through API error rate | 7 | 5 | 6 | 210 | Persist a `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`/screening-pending state on timeout, expose a safe status, alert on screening latency/error rate, and define capacity/recovery behaviour with the fraud team. |
| FMEA-09 | Nightly ledger balance refresh is missing, late or wrong | The balance is a copy overwritten by the ledger at 02:00, but the refresh mechanism and freshness/quality controls are outside this service description | App shows stale or incorrect available balance; payments may be refused or accepted using the wrong copy | Account closure can strand funds if it relies on the copy; support, finance and regulatory outputs use inconsistent balances | Customers, ledger reconciliation or the next refresh may expose drift; no freshness timestamp or data-quality alert is specified | 9 | 4 | 8 | 288 | Make the import durable and replayable, store source timestamp/version, alert on missing/stale/imbalanced loads, and require a live ledger check for closure and other exact-balance decisions. |
| FMEA-10 | Cross-tenant or unauthorised data/action is exposed | Regression in JWT claim mapping, URL rules, `AccessPolicy`, or a new endpoint bypasses resource checks; 404/403 conventions are security-sensitive | A customer or staff member can view another tenant’s account/payment or initiate an unauthorised payment | Tenant bank confidentiality and regulatory obligations are breached; security, compliance and support teams must contain the incident | API tests cover representative cases, but production detection depends on audit logs and anomaly monitoring; no explicit access-denial/audit signal is shown | 10 | 3 | 8 | 240 | Add tenant/subject authorisation tests for every endpoint, negative contract tests and security review; emit immutable audit events and alert on cross-tenant denial anomalies. |
| FMEA-11 | Authentication or signing-key/configuration failure makes the API unavailable or insecure | Identity issuer/key rotation, public-key configuration, clock skew, integration URL or timeout configuration is wrong; the service has a static public-key location and no documented rotation/readiness control | Customers and staff receive 401/5xx and cannot access accounts, or—if validation is weakened during recovery—unauthorised access is possible | Mobile/online banking and back office fail; support and incident teams have no account channel during the outage | Health endpoint may remain reachable; 401 rate, startup failures and token-validation logs should reveal the issue, but no alerting is defined | 9 | 3 | 7 | 189 | Implement managed key rotation and configuration validation, clock-skew policy, safe startup/readiness probes, and alerts for 401 spikes and dependency/configuration failures. |
| FMEA-12 | Notification failure hides a completed, failed or held payment | Notification delivery is best effort, failures are only logged, and there are no retries or delivery status | Customer does not receive confirmation or failure/review information and may retry or contact support unnecessarily | Contact centre and complaints workload increases; customer communication evidence is incomplete even though payment/finance processing continues | Application logs contain a warning, but there is no durable outbox, delivery metric or alert for notification backlog/failure | 6 | 6 | 7 | 252 | Use an outbox/event record committed with the payment, retry with provider idempotency, track delivery status, and alert on age and volume of undelivered notifications. Keep last-four-only payload validation. |
| FMEA-13 | Closure approval closes an account with funds or never completes | Closure request flow only records a pending request in the shown code; the required approver/ledger zero-balance decision is not represented, and the local balance is not authoritative | Closure may be delayed, or funds can be stranded if a later implementation trusts the copied balance | Operations must recover funds manually; complaints and regulatory controls lose evidence of approver and balance check | Pending-request reports show workflow backlog, but no control detects closure without a ledger confirmation or stale requests | 9 | 3 | 8 | 216 | Complete the approval workflow with maker/checker separation, a live ledger zero-balance check, immutable decision/audit records, and alerts for overdue or inconsistent closure requests. |
| FMEA-14 | Statements or supporting records are incomplete or inconsistent | Statements are built from monthly ledger closing balances, but durable storage, cut/overlap rules, import completeness and generation monitoring are not defined; restart can also remove records | Customer receives a wrong/missing statement and may be unable to prove transaction history | Regulatory return due by the fifth working day can be wrong; complaints team lacks a complete evidence trail | Monthly generation/reconciliation should detect it, but no explicit completeness control or alert is specified | 9 | 3 | 8 | 216 | Persist immutable statement versions, reconcile statement totals to ledger, monitor generation/completeness before the fifth working day, and test month-end plus restart/recovery scenarios. |
| FMEA-15 | Operational failure is not noticed within the service window | No explicit SLO metrics, dependency dashboards, queue-age alerts, exception ageing, synthetic journey or on-call escalation is present in the repository | Customers experience prolonged stale balances, failed payments or unavailable account access before anyone responds | Finance, fraud, complaints and operations miss their 07:00/4-hour/monthly obligations | Logs and the public health endpoint are insufficient to prove customer-path health or business processing health | 8 | 6 | 9 | 432 | Define and instrument 99.9% availability plus business SLIs: payment state age, queue age, screening latency, ledger exceptions, balance freshness, reconciliation breaks and notification delivery; page with runbooks and ownership. |

## Release priorities

The highest-priority release blockers are FMEA-02 (mandatory fraud screening),
FMEA-01 (durable persistence), FMEA-07 (safe queued dispatch), FMEA-03 and
FMEA-05 (ambiguous/duplicate payment submission), and FMEA-15 (operational
detection). FMEA-04 and FMEA-06 should be closed before enabling meaningful
payment volume. The remaining items need named owners, acceptance criteria and
explicit residual-risk sign-off from Operations, Finance, Fraud, Compliance and
the Accounts squad.

The minimum go-live evidence should include failure-injection tests for scheme
timeouts before and after acceptance, ledger timeouts before and after booking,
fraud unavailability, worker restart during dispatch, duplicate client retries,
concurrent payments, bank holidays, database restore, key rotation and
cross-tenant access attempts.
