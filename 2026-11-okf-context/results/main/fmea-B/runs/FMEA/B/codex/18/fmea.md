# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its scheduled payment dispatch, persistence, security boundary, and
the scheme, fraud, ledger and notification integrations. It is based on the implementation and the operational facts in
`okf/architecture.md`, `okf/context.md` and `okf/system.md`, as reviewed on 4 November 2026.

Scores use the usual FMEA convention: severity (S) is the impact if the failure occurs, occurrence (O) is the expected
likelihood in the current design, and detection (D) is the likelihood that the failure escapes existing controls (10 is
hardest to detect). RPN = S × O × D. The scores are relative prioritisation aids, not probabilities. The highest risks
should be addressed before release even where their observed occurrence is currently low.

| # | Failure mode | Cause / trigger | Effect on customers, dependent systems and teams | Detection / current control | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | Payment is sent without fraud screening | `FraudScreeningClient` exists but `PaymentService` never calls it. The payment goes directly from validation to `PaymentDispatcher`. | A prohibited or suspicious payment can be sent, violating FRD-7 and exposing the bank to fraud, customer loss and regulatory action. Fraud operations do not receive a review item. | No runtime control or payment-level screening result. Detect through code review, fraud audit and reconciliation of payments against screening decisions. | 10 | 10 | 9 | **900** | Make screening a mandatory state transition before dispatch. Persist the decision and screening timestamp; map `ACCEPT` to dispatch, `REFER`/timeout/error to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, and `REJECT` to `REJECTED` without revealing the reason to the customer. Add a release-blocking test and metric for any sent payment without an ACCEPT. |
| 2 | A scheme-accepted payment has no durable local record | The scheme call times out after acceptance. `SchemeTimeoutException` is not handled, so the transaction rolls back even though the scheme reference (`P` + payment id) may already be accepted. | The customer sees an error and may retry; the scheme can pay once or twice while accounts-service has no reliable status. Finance reconciliation sees a scheme settlement with no matching payment; complaints and operations cannot explain it. | Scheme settlement report and 07:00 reconciliation can reveal an unmatched instruction, but there is no status-query/recovery path. | 10 | 5 | 8 | **400** | Treat timeout as `SUBMITTED`/`UNKNOWN`, commit the payment and reconcile asynchronously. Query `GET /v2/instructions/{reference}` before any retry; always reuse the same scheme reference and add alerts for unresolved instructions. |
| 3 | Customer is debited twice during ledger recovery | A ledger timeout may have booked. On a later dispatch/repost, `PaymentDispatcher` creates a new random `ledgerReference` rather than reusing the payment's original reference. | Core ledger can receive two debits for one scheme payment. Customer balance and finance books are wrong; 07:00 reconciliation breaks and operations must refund or manually correct funds. | `POSTING_FAILED` log and daily exceptions report identify candidates, but duplicate detection depends on finance reconciliation and is after-the-fact. | 10 | 4 | 8 | **320** | Generate and persist `ledgerReference` before the first post, never regenerate it, and use it for every retry. Add a recovery worker/runbook that queries or reposts idempotently and alerts on scheme-settled/ledger-unmatched payments. |
| 4 | Scheme payment is sent but ledger posting fails, while customer is told it was sent | Ledger timeout is caught and status becomes `POSTING_FAILED`, but `notifySent` still sends `PAYMENT_SENT`; the customer-facing balance copy is debited without a completed ledger booking. | Customer receives a misleading confirmation and may spend against an inconsistent view. Finance sees a scheme/ledger break; operations must repair the posting. | `POSTING_FAILED` status, warning log and daily exceptions report exist; notification content and alerting are not tied to the failed state. | 9 | 5 | 5 | **225** | Use a distinct “sent, posting pending” customer message or suppress success notification until ledger outcome is known. Make `POSTING_FAILED` durable, visible to operations, retryable with the same reference, and alert on age/SLA. |
| 5 | Same-day payment is accepted more than once | No idempotency key is accepted from the caller and no database uniqueness/locking protects concurrent requests. Two requests can both pass balance and daily-limit checks before either debit is committed. | Customer can send duplicate instructions or exceed funds/daily limit; scheme, ledger and finance receive multiple payments. Support handles duplicate-payment complaints. | Reconciliation and customer reports may reveal duplicates; there is no request deduplication or concurrency alarm. | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Require an idempotency key, persist it per tenant/customer and return the original result. Add account-level locking or an atomic reservation for balance and daily-limit checks, plus unique constraints and concurrency tests. |
| 6 | Service balance is stale or becomes inconsistent with the ledger | `accounts.balance` is only a copy, overwritten by the ledger at 02:00; intra-day debit is local. Failures or lag in the overwrite are not surfaced. | Customers see a wrong balance; payment validation may reject valid payments or allow overspending. Closure with funds stranded is especially severe if the zero check is made against the copy. Finance, support and operations get conflicting figures. | 02:00 ledger feed and 07:00 reconciliation are implicit controls; no freshness, variance or failed-import alert is defined. | 9 | 6 | 7 | **378** | Monitor ledger-balance import freshness/completeness and per-account variance. Check the ledger directly for closure and other exact-money decisions; expose balance freshness and fail safely when the copy is stale. |
| 7 | A payment queued for a bank holiday is rejected or delayed | `WorkingDays` excludes weekends only. The service queues for a weekday that can be a bank holiday; the scheme rejects it with `DT01`. | Payment is not sent when promised and may end `REJECTED`; customers, support and complaints teams see an avoidable failure. Operations must resubmit it. | Scheme rejection reason `DT01` and queued-past-date exceptions can identify it, but there is no bank-holiday calendar or proactive alert. | 7 | 6 | 5 | **210** | Use a versioned UK bank-holiday calendar shared with the scheme, calculate the next scheme working day, and test cut-off/weekend/holiday boundaries. Alert on any `DT01` from a supposedly queued payment. |
| 8 | Due queued payments are not dispatched | The 06:00 Monday-Friday scheduler is disabled, crashes, is not running in the deployed profile, or fails part-way through the list. There is no explicit retry/lease or completion checkpoint. | Payments remain `QUEUED` after their execution date; customers miss expected payment timing, and operations/fraud workflows accumulate backlog. | Operations are told to use a daily report for queued payments past date, but no implementation-level metric or alert is evident. | 8 | 4 | 7 | **224** | Emit due-count, success, failure, duration and oldest-queued-age metrics. Alert on missed 06:00 runs and queue age; use durable work claiming, retry with bounded backoff, and an operator replay command. |
| 9 | Fraud referral is lost or treated as a rejection | The intended `REFER`/unavailable-to-hold flow is not implemented; `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` has no API workflow or in-service review completion path. | Legitimate customers can be blocked indefinitely or receive the wrong outcome; fraud staff cannot reliably find or complete the four-hour review. | A status query can show `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` only if it was set; no age/SLA metric or reconciliation with the fraud tool is present. | 8 | 6 | 8 | **384** | Implement a durable review queue with decision correlation, four-hour ageing alerts, explicit approve/reject transitions, audit trail and customer notification. Never interpret `REFER` as `REJECTED`. |
| 10 | Sensitive account or tenant data is exposed | Payment responses include full payee sort code/account number; access relies on claims and resource checks, while admin and support paths are security-sensitive. A future endpoint or misconfigured claim could bypass the intended tenant boundary. | Privacy breach, account takeover assistance and regulatory reporting; affected customer, security, support and compliance teams respond. | Spring Security returns 401/403 and `getVisible` returns indistinguishable 404s. Tests cover API access, but there is no stated production audit/alert for cross-tenant access or sensitive-field leakage. | 10 | 3 | 8 | **240** | Minimise payment response fields (mask account numbers where not required), add tenant/role/scope deny-by-default tests and property-based cross-tenant tests, audit access decisions, and alert on anomalous 403/404/access patterns. Keep internal notes, risk scores and full account details out of customer views. |
| 11 | Account closure is requested but cannot be safely completed | The repository contains request creation and pending-listing, but no approval/closure operation; the documented ledger zero-balance check is not implemented in this service path. `ClosureRequest` also uses `Instant.now()` rather than the injected UK clock. | Customers cannot complete closure; approvers and operations have stale work. If closure is later implemented against the copy, funds could be stranded. Regulatory/complaints records may show an unresolved request. | Pending requests are listable; there is no SLA/age alert, approval audit control, or ledger-zero verification. | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Implement a separate approver decision endpoint with two-person enforcement, ledger balance verification, transactional/audited state transitions and pending-age alerts. Inject the clock into closure and case timestamps. Do not mark an account closed on the local balance copy alone. |
| 12 | Notifications fail or contain an unsafe message | Notification is best effort and failures are only logged, with no retry or delivery status. A template/configuration change could violate the last-four-digits-only rule. | Customer may not know a payment was queued, sent or under review; support receives avoidable contacts. A full account number/sort code in a notification is a reportable data-protection breach. | Warning logs and notification-provider telemetry; no durable outbox, delivery reconciliation or automated payload redaction test. | 7 | 6 | 6 | **252** | Use an outbox linked to the committed payment state with retry/dead-letter handling. Add contract tests that reject full account/sort-code fields and monitor notification lag/failure rate. Keep notification failure independent of money movement. |
| 13 | API becomes unavailable or loses data | H2/in-memory configuration is suitable for fixtures but not durable production operation; database, JVM, network, identity key, or deployment failure can make reads/writes unavailable. There is no visible readiness/DB dependency design in the repository. | Mobile/online banking and back office cannot show accounts or create payments; scheduled work may be missed. Customers retry, increasing duplicate risk; support, finance and operations lose timely records. | Permitted `/actuator/health` endpoint and platform monitoring are expected, but no SLO/error-rate, dependency, backup/restore or scheduler health evidence is defined here. | 9 | 4 | 6 | **216** | Use a durable HA database and tested backups/restore, readiness checks for required dependencies, graceful degradation for reads, deployment smoke tests and the 99.9% availability/error-budget dashboards described by the service objective. |
| 14 | Authentication or authorisation is misinterpreted | Token role/scope claims, tenant claim, signing key rotation, or clock validation is misconfigured. Method and URL rules differ, or a new endpoint is added without `@PreAuthorize`. | Unauthorised data access or rejected legitimate access across customers/tenants; security incident and customer/support disruption. | JWT signature validation and API tests provide baseline controls; no production key-rotation, claim-shape, endpoint inventory or access audit is shown. | 10 | 3 | 7 | **210** | Pin and test issuer/audience/expiry/key rotation, fail closed on missing tenant/roles/scopes, maintain an endpoint authorisation matrix, run negative cross-tenant tests in CI, and audit privileged access. |
| 15 | Statements or complaint records are missing/incomplete | Statement entities/repositories and account/case records exist, but no statement-serving or generation workflow is exposed in the inspected API; direct use of system time also risks inconsistent timestamps. | Monthly regulatory return due by the fifth working day can be wrong or late. Complaints team cannot evidence the customer history within the eight-week response window; finance/regulatory teams remediate manually. | Regulatory and complaint reconciliations are downstream controls; no statement completeness/period-close job or report is evident. | 9 | 4 | 8 | **288** | Implement/operate a ledger-driven statement close with completeness checks, immutable period outputs, rerun/reconciliation controls and alerts before the fifth working day. Add record-retention and complaint-history integrity checks. |

## Release priorities

1. Block release until every payment is fraud-screened and the fail-closed `REFER`/timeout behavior is durable (row 1).
2. Make scheme and ledger uncertainty recoverable and idempotent, with reconciliation alerts and no customer success message
   for a posting that is not complete (rows 2–4).
3. Add payment idempotency/concurrency protection and balance freshness controls (rows 5–6).
4. Make the queued-payment, fraud-review and notification workflows observable and replayable (rows 8–9 and 12).
5. Close the ledger-verified account-closure, statement, and access/privacy control gaps before relying on the service for
   regulatory or high-risk operational processes (rows 10–11 and 14–15).

The first production dashboards should at minimum show payment counts by status and age, sent-without-screening,
scheme/ledger unmatched references, ledger timeout age, duplicate/idempotency conflicts, balance-import freshness,
queued-past-date count, fraud-review age, notification failures, API dependency latency/error rate, and statement/closure
backlogs.
