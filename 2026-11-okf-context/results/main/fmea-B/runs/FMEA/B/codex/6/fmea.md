# Accounts service FMEA

Review date: 2026-11-04  
Scope: the Spring Boot accounts API, its persistence and scheduled work, and the
scheme, fraud, ledger and notification integrations. The service is the customer's
view of accounts; the core ledger remains the system of record for money.

This is an operational FMEA based on the implementation and the repository
knowledge bundle (`okf/context.md`, `okf/system.md`, and `okf/architecture.md`).
The scores are deliberately risk-oriented and should be recalibrated with incident
and monitoring data after release.

## Scoring

- **Severity (S):** 1 = negligible customer/team impact; 10 = major financial,
  regulatory, security or widespread customer harm.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 = exceptional; 10 = expected or frequent.
- **Detection (D):** 1 = detected automatically and promptly; 10 = unlikely to be
  detected before customer, finance, fraud or regulatory impact.
- **RPN:** `S × O × D`. Detection is scored in the usual FMEA direction: a higher
  number means poorer detectability.

## Failure modes

| ID | Failure mode | Cause | Effect on customers and dependent systems/teams | Detection | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F-01 | A payment is sent without fraud screening | `PaymentService.create` saves and dispatches directly; the existing `FraudScreeningClient` is never called. | A payment that should be rejected or referred can reach the scheme. Customers may suffer fraud; the fraud team loses the four-hour review queue; compliance and complaints teams face a policy breach under FRD-7. | Audit payment records against fraud-screening events; alert on any scheme submission without an `ACCEPT` decision. At present there is no preventive or complete in-service detection. | 10 | 10 | 9 | **900** | Wire screening before dispatch with a two-second timeout and fail-closed handling. Persist the decision/event and use `REFER` as `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`; only `ACCEPT` may submit. Add an invariant test and a reconciliation alert. |
| F-02 | A scheme timeout loses or duplicates a payment | `SchemeTimeoutException` is not caught. The scheme may have accepted the instruction, while the transaction rolls back the local record; a customer retry can send another payment. | Customer sees an error despite possible payment; the payee may be paid once or twice. Finance reconciliation sees a scheme settlement with no local payment, or a duplicate; complaints and operations must investigate. | Compare scheme settlement to local `schemeId`/reference at the 07:00 reconciliation; customer reports are likely before detection. No status-query use exists. | 10 | 4 | 8 | **320** | Catch timeout, retain the payment in an explicit `SUBMISSION_UNKNOWN`/recovery state, and query `GET /v2/instructions/{reference}`. Reconcile before allowing resend; always reuse `P{id}` and alert aged unknowns. |
| F-03 | A ledger timeout creates money/status divergence or a double debit | A five-second ledger timeout may mean booking succeeded. The dispatcher creates a fresh UUID ledger reference during dispatch, and there is no automatic status query; unsafe future retries could use a new reference. | Scheme payment is sent while the customer balance copy and payment status say `POSTING_FAILED`; or a retry debits the ledger twice. Finance gets an exception/reconciliation break and operations must remediate. | Existing `POSTING_FAILED` rows and the 07:00 ledger-versus-scheme reconciliation. Detection is after the event and depends on the daily report. | 10 | 5 | 6 | **300** | Generate and persist `ledgerReference` before the call, make it immutable, and expose a controlled idempotent repost/status-check workflow. Alert on aged `POSTING_FAILED`, and verify ledger outcome before retry. |
| F-04 | A queued payment is submitted on an invalid scheme date | `WorkingDays` handles weekdays but not bank holidays; the scheduled job runs only Monday-Friday. | Payment is rejected with `DT01`, arrives later than promised, or remains queued through a holiday. Customers miss a due date; operations and support handle avoidable exceptions; finance sees incomplete settlement. | Monitor `QUEUED` payments past `executionDate`; inspect scheme rejection reason `DT01`. The existing report is required but no in-code alert is present. | 7 | 6 | 6 | **252** | Use a bank-holiday calendar owned by payments, calculate the next scheme working day, and schedule/re-run by due date rather than weekday alone. Add holiday boundary tests and an aged-queue alert. |
| F-05 | The 06:00 queue job does not run, partially runs, or runs concurrently | Process outage, scheduler misconfiguration, deployment at run time, database failure, or multiple service instances without a claim/lock. `runDue` loops over all due rows with no per-payment lease or recovery marker. | Customers wait past the promised execution date; a retry or second instance can submit the same queued payment twice. Scheme, finance and operations see late or duplicate instructions. | Alert on `QUEUED` rows past date and compare daily submissions with due queue count. Duplicate detection relies on scheme reference/reconciliation. | 8 | 5 | 7 | **280** | Make dispatch idempotent on payment state/reference, claim rows with a durable lease or database lock, record attempts, and provide a replayable operator command. Alert on job heartbeat, duration, failures and aged queue depth. |
| F-06 | Concurrent requests bypass balance or daily-limit checks | `PaymentService` reads the balance copy and sums payments before dispatch; there is no account lock, atomic reservation, or database uniqueness/idempotency constraint. | Two accepted requests can overdraw the account or exceed the daily limit. Customers see an incorrect balance; ledger and finance reconciliation diverge; support handles disputes. | Reconcile account copy to ledger and monitor negative/over-limit balances; these are post-event signals. | 9 | 5 | 7 | **315** | Add request idempotency, pessimistic/optimistic account concurrency control, and an atomic available-balance/daily-limit reservation. Test parallel submissions and reconcile reservations with final outcomes. |
| F-07 | Customer retries create duplicate payments | There is no idempotency key for the API request; each POST creates a new payment id and a new scheme reference (`P` + id). | A timeout, mobile retry or client double-tap can pay the same payee twice. The customer is debited twice and finance must investigate a reconciliation break. | Repeated payee/amount/reference heuristics and customer complaints; scheme reference uniqueness cannot detect two different payment ids. | 9 | 6 | 8 | **432** | Require and persist a client idempotency key scoped to tenant/account; return the original result for a repeat. Keep the customer reference as a display field, not the deduplication mechanism. |
| F-08 | The service balance copy is stale or wrong | Balances are overwritten from the ledger at 02:00, while daytime payment dispatch debits the local copy; the refresh path is external to this repository and there is no freshness/version field or visible stale state. | App and online banking show wrong available funds; valid payments may be refused or excess payments accepted. Closure must not rely on this copy or it can strand funds. Support and finance lose trust in the displayed balance. | Compare `accounts.balance` with ledger snapshots and alert when the 02:00 refresh is missing, stale or materially different. | 9 | 5 | 7 | **315** | Track ledger snapshot timestamp/version, fail or mark balances stale when refresh is absent, and use an authoritative ledger check for payment availability and all closure decisions. |
| F-09 | Account closure is requested/approved without an enforceable zero-ledger-balance check | The repository has closure request creation and listing, but no approval/execution endpoint; the documented requirement to check the ledger is not represented in the closure flow. | An account can remain open indefinitely, or a future approval implementation may close an account with funds. Customers' money can be stranded; operations and complaints teams need manual remediation. | Review pending closure age and reconcile closed accounts against ledger balances; no automatic control currently enforces this. | 10 | 3 | 9 | **270** | Implement a two-person approval/execution workflow that queries the ledger immediately before close, rejects non-zero or unknown balances, prevents new payments during finalization, and emits an auditable event. |
| F-10 | Fraud screening is unavailable and payment handling fails open or strands work | The client defines fail-closed semantics, but payments do not call it. A future integration may mishandle `FraudScreeningUnavailableException`, and there is no persisted hold/review queue in the payment flow. | An unavailable fraud service could allow unscreened payments or leave customers without a recoverable status. Fraud analysts cannot meet the four-hour review target; support receives ambiguous failures. | Alert on fraud client errors/timeouts and on submissions without a decision; measure age/count of `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`. Current code has no such payment-level metric. | 10 | 4 | 8 | **320** | Treat timeout/error as `REFER`, persist `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, send `PAYMENT_UNDER_REVIEW`, and provide fraud-team work queue, SLA timers and resolution transitions. Add contract tests for timeout, `REFER`, and `REJECT`. |
| F-11 | A notification failure is mistaken for a payment failure, or a notification leaks sensitive data | Notification is best effort and caught by the dispatcher, but there is no retry/dead-letter path. Template construction is local and must remain last-four-only. | Customer may not learn that a payment was sent, causing duplicate retries and support contacts. A regression that includes a full account number/sort code becomes a reportable data-protection incident. | Monitor notification failure logs and delivery acknowledgements; scan payloads/templates for account-number patterns and alert on failed delivery backlog. Current logging is only a warning. | 7 | 5 | 7 | **245** | Use an outbox with durable, retryable, deduplicated notifications; keep payment outcome independent. Enforce schema-level redaction and automated tests preventing full account number/sort code in payloads and logs. |
| F-12 | Cross-tenant or staff-only data is disclosed | A token claim, role mapping, repository query or future endpoint omits the tenant/resource check. Sensitive fields include account numbers, internal notes/comments and risk scores. | A customer's financial or fraud information reaches another tenant or an unauthorized customer. The bank faces a serious privacy/security incident, regulatory reporting and loss of trust. | 401/403/404 API tests, authorization audit logs, cross-tenant synthetic probes, and DLP scanning. Existing tests cover several access cases but not every data-bearing resource or token-claim anomaly. | 10 | 3 | 8 | **240** | Enforce tenant predicates at repository/service boundaries, use deny-by-default DTOs, add negative tests for every endpoint and role/scope combination, and alert on cross-tenant access attempts. Never return internal fields in customer DTOs. |
| F-13 | Monthly statements or regulatory data are missing/incorrect | Statements are persisted as data, but generation from ledger closing balances and the regulatory-return workflow are not implemented in this service. A missed/failed ledger snapshot or incorrect balance copy can also feed wrong data. | Customers lack a complete statement; the return due by the fifth working day can be wrong or late. Finance, regulatory reporting and complaints teams must reconstruct records manually. | Reconcile statement coverage for every account/period to ledger balances; report missing periods and variance before the fifth working day. | 9 | 4 | 8 | **288** | Implement an idempotent month-end statement job sourced from ledger closing balances, with completeness/variance gates, immutable artifacts, rerun support and an on-call deadline alert. |
| F-14 | Database or application outage makes the API unavailable or loses in-flight work | H2/in-memory configuration is suitable for tests but has no demonstrated production persistence/backup/HA configuration; a transaction spanning remote calls can roll back after an external side effect. | Customers cannot view balances or payments, queued work may be missed, and in-flight scheme/ledger outcomes become uncertain. Back office, fraud, finance, operations and complaints workflows stop. | Health endpoint, dependency probes, uptime/latency/error-rate monitoring, durable queue age, backups and restore tests. The repository exposes only a basic permitted health route. | 9 | 4 | 6 | **216** | Use durable production DB with HA, backups and tested restore; keep external side effects out of fragile request transactions via an outbox/state machine; add readiness checks, SLO alerts and replay/reconciliation tooling. |
| F-15 | Time-zone or clock error changes cutoff, due date, daily-limit window or audit time | Payment cutoff and queueing use the injected UK clock, but other entities use `Instant.now()` directly and scheduled work depends on host/runtime timing. Configuration or DST handling can still drift. | Payment is sent same-day when it should queue, delayed, counted in the wrong day, or recorded with an inconsistent audit timestamp. Customers and finance see disputes around deadlines. | Synthetic tests around 15:30, midnight and DST; compare host time/zone and event timestamps; alert on late/early queue executions. | 7 | 4 | 6 | **168** | Inject one authoritative `Clock`/time service everywhere, store UTC instants plus explicit UK business dates, and test DST, midnight, cutoff and bank-holiday transitions in CI and production canaries. |

## Priority actions before release

The highest-risk release blockers are F-01, F-07 and F-02: a payment must not reach
the scheme without a persisted fraud decision, a customer retry must not create a
new payment, and a scheme timeout must leave a recoverable unknown state. F-03 and
F-06 are next because they can create ledger divergence or overdraw a customer's
account. F-04/F-05 and F-13 should be scheduled with explicit operational owners
and alerts before relying on the service for due-date payments and regulatory
reporting.

## Required operating signals

At minimum, page or ticket on: scheme/fraud/ledger error rates and latency;
payments in `SUBMISSION_UNKNOWN`, `POSTING_FAILED`, `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, or
`QUEUED` past date; missing 02:00 balance refresh; queue-job heartbeat failure;
ledger-versus-scheme mismatches; statement coverage/variance failures; and
notification outbox backlog. Every alert should include tenant, payment/account
identifier, state, external reference and a safe correlation id, never full
account or sort-code data.
