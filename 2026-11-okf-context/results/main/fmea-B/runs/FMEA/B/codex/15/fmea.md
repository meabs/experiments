# Accounts-service FMEA

Operational review date: 4 November 2026  
Scope: the accounts-service API, its scheduled payment processing, persistence, security boundary and four external integrations. The core ledger remains the system of record for money; this service is the system of record for the customer's view.

## Scoring

Scores use the conventional 1–10 scale: Severity (S) is the impact if the failure occurs, Occurrence (O) is the expected likelihood without the recommended action, and Detection (D) is how unlikely the existing controls are to detect it before customer or downstream impact (10 = least detectable). `RPN = S × O × D`; the highest values should be treated first. Scores are relative prioritisation aids, not probabilities.

| FMEA-01 | Payment is sent without fraud screening | `PaymentService` never calls the available fraud client; FRD-7 is therefore not enforced | A fraudulent payment can leave the account; the customer may lose money and face delayed recovery | Fraud team has no hold/review record; compliance and incident teams must investigate an avoidable control breach | Code review and reconciliation can reveal the gap; no runtime control or metric currently proves every payment was screened | 10 | 8 | 9 | 720 | Wire screening before scheme submission. Fail closed on timeout/error, map `REFER` to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, persist the decision and correlation ID, and alert on any unscreened attempt. Add contract and negative-path tests. |
| FMEA-02 | Accepted scheme payment is lost locally or later duplicated | A scheme timeout is not caught; the transaction rolls back even though the scheme may have accepted. A later retry with a new reference can pay twice | App shows an error or no payment record; recipient may be paid once or twice and the balance/status can be wrong | Finance reconciliation sees an unmatched settlement or duplicate; support and operations cannot reliably trace the payment | Scheme reference is deterministic (`P` + payment id), and daily reconciliation is expected, but there is no status-query/recovery path | 10 | 6 | 9 | 540 | Catch `SchemeTimeoutException`, retain an indeterminate/submission state, query the scheme by the same reference, and reconcile before retrying. Never create a new scheme reference for a retry. Alert on indeterminate payments. |
| FMEA-03 | Ledger is posted twice or scheme is sent without a durable ledger outcome | Ledger timeout may mean “booked”; retrying with a new `ledgerReference` duplicates the debit. The dispatcher generates the reference late and does not provide an automated recovery workflow | Customer can be debited twice, or sees a sent payment with an apparently failed status | Finance's 07:00 settlement match breaks; operations must manually repost `POSTING_FAILED` items; complaints and support get conflicting answers | `POSTING_FAILED` status and daily exceptions report; ledger idempotency works for the same reference for 24 hours | 10 | 5 | 8 | 400 | Generate and persist `ledgerReference` before posting, reuse it for every retry, make recovery/query idempotent, and alert on `POSTING_FAILED` age and settlement mismatches. Test timeout-before/after-booking cases. |
| FMEA-04 | Balance copy permits an overpayment or presents a false balance | Balance is stale between the 02:00 ledger refreshes; concurrent payment requests are not shown to use locking/atomic reservation | Payment may be accepted when funds are no longer available, or a valid payment may be rejected; displayed balance is misleading | Ledger becomes the source of a negative/incorrect outcome; finance and support investigate discrepancies | Amount is checked against the local balance and the ledger overwrites the copy at 02:00; no freshness, concurrency or reservation check is visible | 9 | 7 | 7 | 441 | Add a monitored ledger balance import with freshness/version, reserve funds atomically per account, and perform an authoritative ledger check for boundary/high-risk payments. Alert when refresh is late or balances diverge. |
| FMEA-05 | Queued payment is sent on a non-settlement day or late | `WorkingDays` excludes weekends only; it has no UK bank-holiday calendar. The 06:00 job is hard-coded to Monday–Friday | Payment is delayed, rejected with `DT01`, or appears queued beyond its promise | Scheme rejects the instruction; operations and support handle avoidable exceptions and customers may miss bills | Scheme rejection status/reason code; no calendar validation or SLA alert | 8 | 6 | 6 | 288 | Use a versioned UK bank-holiday/settlement calendar shared with the scheme, calculate the next settlement day, and monitor queued payments past `executionDate`. |
| FMEA-06 | Due queue is not dispatched, is dispatched twice, or job failure leaves items stranded | Scheduler disabled, application down at 06:00, process crash part-way through the loop, or multiple instances run without claiming/locking rows | Payment remains `QUEUED`, is late, or is submitted twice | Operations' past-due report grows; scheme and finance see missing/duplicate references; support receives inconsistent status | `QueuedPaymentJob` scans due rows, and `QUEUED` past-date items are an operational exception; no durable claim, retry, or job health metric is shown | 8 | 6 | 7 | 336 | Implement a durable outbox/claim-and-lock pattern, retry with backoff, idempotent dispatch, and a job heartbeat plus alerts for missed runs, age, and backlog. |
| FMEA-07 | Payment status and customer notification disagree | Notification is best effort with no retry; it can fail after scheme submission or ledger timeout, and the same “sent” template is used for a posting failure | Customer does not know a payment was sent, or is told it was sent while ledger status is `POSTING_FAILED` | Support and complaints teams lack reliable customer evidence; notification operations cannot replay a failed message | Notification exceptions are logged; no durable notification event, delivery status, or alert is specified | 7 | 6 | 8 | 336 | Emit an outbox event after the payment state is committed, retry safely, expose delivery status, and use distinct templates for completed, posting-failed, queued and under-review states. Never include a full account number. |
| FMEA-08 | Closure proceeds with money still in the account or never completes | The closure request flow only creates/lists requests; no approval/execution path is present here, and the stated zero-balance check must use the ledger rather than the copy | Funds can be stranded, or the customer waits indefinitely for closure | Approvers and operations have no complete workflow; complaints and regulatory servicing are affected | Pending requests are visible to approvers; account status and ledger zero-balance enforcement are not implemented in the reviewed code | 10 | 4 | 8 | 320 | Implement an explicit approver transition with segregation of duties, authoritative ledger zero-balance check, transaction/payment freeze, idempotent close operation, and aging alerts for pending requests. |
| FMEA-09 | Monthly statement is missing or wrong | Statements are read from persistence, but no statement cut/import job is present in this service; ledger closing balances or the 1st-of-month run can be unavailable | Customer sees an incomplete/wrong statement and may dispute the balance | Regulatory return due by the 5th working day can be wrong; finance and complaints teams lack authoritative records | Existing statement rows and API tests provide fixture coverage only; no completeness/reconciliation or deadline alert is visible | 9 | 4 | 9 | 324 | Define ownership and implement a repeatable statement cut from ledger closing balances, with period completeness, totals reconciliation, immutable/versioned output, rerun controls, and a 5th-working-day alert. |
| FMEA-10 | Cross-tenant or unauthorised data/action is exposed | Token claims, role mappings, resource checks, support assignments or endpoint rules are misconfigured; payment views also return payee account details to any caller who can view the payment | Personal, account and payment data disclosure; unauthorised payment or support action | Tenant bank, security, privacy and incident teams face a reportable breach; customer trust and regulatory standing are harmed | JWT validation, tenant checks, 404 concealment, scopes and method security; tests cover several tenant/role cases but not all endpoint combinations | 10 | 3 | 8 | 240 | Add deny-by-default integration tests for every endpoint/role/tenant combination, enforce tenant on payment/resource queries, review minimum data in `PaymentView`, and add audit logging and access anomaly alerts. |
| FMEA-11 | Authentication or authorisation fails open or causes broad outage | Identity key rotation/public-key configuration, malformed/missing tenant or role claims, or an overly broad security rule is deployed incorrectly | Customers and staff receive 401/403 and cannot use accounts, or an attacker gains access if a rule fails open | Mobile/online channels, back office and support operations are unavailable; security incident response may be required | Resource-server JWT validation, stateless sessions, `/actuator/health` public and `/api/**` authenticated; no key-rotation drill or auth availability SLO is shown | 10 | 3 | 7 | 210 | Establish key-rotation and claim-contract tests, fail closed for missing tenant/subject, monitor 401/403 rates by endpoint and tenant, and keep a tested break-glass/support procedure. |
| FMEA-12 | Service or database outage returns unavailable/stale account data | Application failure, H2/non-durable deployment characteristics, database connectivity/lock exhaustion, or dependency saturation | App and online banking cannot show balances, transactions or payment status; writes may fail | Back office, fraud review, finance and complaints work is delayed; availability target (99.9%, 07:00–23:00) is missed | Health endpoint and ordinary request errors; no visible readiness, dependency health, backup/restore, or latency/error SLO instrumentation | 9 | 4 | 6 | 216 | Use a durable production database, readiness/liveness and dependency health checks, backups and restore drills, capacity limits, dashboards and alerts for availability/latency/error budgets. |
| FMEA-13 | Customer submits duplicate payments | No idempotency key is required at the API boundary; app retry/double-click can create separate payment IDs and therefore separate scheme references | Customer pays twice and may exceed the intended daily limit or available funds | Scheme settlement and ledger reconciliation break; support and finance need manual reversal/investigation | Per-payment scheme reference protects retries of the same ID, but does not deduplicate multiple create requests; daily-limit check is not an atomic request idempotency control | 9 | 6 | 7 | 378 | Require and persist a client idempotency key scoped to tenant/account, atomically return the original result, and add duplicate-intent detection and reconciliation alerts. |
| FMEA-14 | Business or audit records are silently incomplete/changed | Transaction rollback around external calls, partial state transitions, missing audit events, or uncontrolled direct data changes | Customer cannot prove what happened; case, payment and complaint history is incomplete | Complaints team cannot meet the 8-week response reliably; fraud, finance and regulators lose an audit trail | JPA transactions and status fields; no append-only audit trail or completeness report is visible | 8 | 4 | 8 | 256 | Add immutable audit events for authentication-sensitive reads, payment/closure transitions and operator actions; reconcile payment, scheme and ledger lifecycles and retain correlation IDs. |
| FMEA-15 | Sensitive account/payment information is logged or returned too broadly | Exceptions and request/response logging may include integration payloads; `PaymentView` exposes payee sort code/account number to every authorised payment viewer | Data-protection exposure and phishing risk, even without a cross-tenant breach | Privacy/security teams handle an incident; notification policy is violated if full source-account data is later reused in templates | Notification code explicitly sends only last four digits, but API response and logging redaction are not centrally enforced | 8 | 4 | 7 | 224 | Minimise/mask account identifiers in API views and logs, add structured redaction tests, prohibit payload logging, and run a privacy review of retention and access. |

## Immediate release gates

The release should not be considered operationally safe for payment production until FMEA-01 (fraud screening), FMEA-02 (scheme timeout recovery), FMEA-03 (ledger idempotent recovery), FMEA-04 (balance concurrency/freshness), and FMEA-13 (client idempotency) have either been fixed or explicitly risk-accepted by the accountable bank control owners. FMEA-05 through FMEA-09 require named operational owners, dashboards and tested runbooks before launch.

The recommended minimum alert set is: any unscreened payment; indeterminate scheme outcome; `POSTING_FAILED` age/count; ledger/scheme reconciliation breaks; stale ledger balance import; queued payments past date; missed/failed 06:00 job; notification outbox backlog; pending closures past SLA; statement period incompleteness; and abnormal 401/403, 5xx, latency or cross-tenant access-denial patterns.
