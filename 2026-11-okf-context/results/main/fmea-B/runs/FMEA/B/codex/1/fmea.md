# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the current implementation and the operational behaviour described in `okf/`. It covers the API,
the payment path, scheduled work, persistence, integrations, security boundaries, and the records consumed by finance,
fraud, regulatory reporting, complaints, and operations.

Scores use the conventional 1–10 scale: severity (S) is the impact if the mode occurs, occurrence (O) is the expected
likelihood under current controls, and detection (D) is how unlikely the failure is to be detected before customer or
downstream impact (10 = hardest to detect). RPN = S × O × D. The scores are comparative engineering judgements, not
incident-rate estimates. Prioritise the highest RPNs, and treat any severity 10 item as release-blocking until an owner
accepts the residual risk.

## Failure-mode analysis

| FM-01 | A payment is sent without fraud screening | `PaymentService` does not call the existing `FraudScreeningClient`; FRD-7 requires screening before every payment | Unscreened fraud or sanctions exposure; customer may lose funds; fraud team and compliance cannot evidence the required control | No screening call, hold, or alert exists; discoverable only by code review or a downstream fraud incident | 10 | 10 | 10 | **1000** | Wire screening before scheme submission. | FM-02 | A scheme timeout loses the local payment record while the scheme accepted it | `SchemeTimeoutException` is not caught; the transaction rolls back, although the scheme may have accepted the instruction | Customer sees an error and may retry; a payment can leave the account without a local status or reference. | FM-03 | A ledger timeout produces an ambiguous debit and an exception workload | The ledger may book before its 5-second timeout; the service marks `POSTING_FAILED`, but there is no built-in status check or repost workflow | Payment may be sent but appear failed; operations must manually repost; a new reference can debit twice. | FM-07 | Queued payments remain queued or are dispatched late/duplicated | The 06:00 scheduler can be down, misconfigured, or run twice; the job has no claim/lease and only runs Monday–Friday | Customer misses the promised payment date; fraud/operations queues grow; scheme settlement and support expectations are wrong | Operations can inspect queued payments past their date, but there is no job heartbeat, run result, or duplicate-dispatch alert | 8 | 4 | 7 | **224** | Add scheduler heartbeat, run metrics, a durable outbox/claim lease, and replay-safe dispatch. | FM-08 | Account closes with money remaining, or approved closure never changes account state | Closure request creation exists, but approval/rejection processing and the required ledger zero-balance check are absent from the API | Funds can be stranded in a closed account, or an account remains open indefinitely; approvers, operations, complaints, and regulatory records are affected | Manual review of pending requests and ledger reconciliation; no invariant prevents closure without a ledger check | 10 | 3 | 9 | **270** | Implement a separate approver decision endpoint with tenant/role checks, re-read the ledger balance at decision time, require zero, make the state transition auditable and atomic, and alert on stale pending requests. |

| FM-11 | A customer or support user sees staff-only material | A new projection/controller can return internal notes, internal case comments, or risk scores without the explicit staff filter; the data model stores both public and internal records | Sensitive fraud, hardship, or operational notes are exposed to customers; complaints and data-protection teams must investigate | Unit/API tests for current endpoints; no response-schema contract test that forbids internal fields or production DLP alert | 9 | 3 | 8 | **216** | Use separate customer/staff DTOs and repository queries, deny-by-default field mapping, add JSON contract tests for internal fields and full account numbers, and scan logs/notifications for sensitive data. |
| FM-12 | Notifications fail or contain incorrect payment information | Notification delivery is best effort with no retry; template/configuration failure or outage prevents customer notice. | FM-13 | External 5xx or malformed responses become generic API failures | HTTP clients translate only selected errors (for example, scheme timeout and 4xx); other integration errors are not consistently mapped or retried | Customers get 5xx/ambiguous outcomes; local transaction rollback can hide whether money moved; dependent teams see spikes without actionable state | HTTP/server metrics and logs; no integration outcome taxonomy or uncertainty dashboard | 8 | 5 | 8 | **320** | Define per-integration error taxonomy and state machine, catch and persist uncertain outcomes, use bounded retries only where idempotent, add circuit breakers/back-pressure, and expose correlation/reference IDs for operations. |

## Release priorities

1. Block release until FM-01 is fixed and evidenced: every payment must have a fraud decision before scheme submission.
2. Fix FM-02, FM-03, and FM-04 before enabling higher payment volume; these are the direct duplicate/lost-money risks.
3. Implement FM-09 and FM-08 before the next reporting/closure cycle, with ledger-backed controls and audit trails.
4. Add the operational controls in FM-05–FM-07 and FM-13: durable state, idempotent replay, heartbeats, overdue queues,
   and reference-based reconciliation.
5. Treat FM-10–FM-12 as security/privacy release gates: retain tenant and staff-boundary regression tests and add
   production detection rather than relying on code review alone.

## Required evidence before sign-off

- Payment integration tests cover fraud `ACCEPT`, `REFER`, `REJECT`, timeout/error, scheme accepted-before-timeout,
  ledger booked-before-timeout, notification outage, replay, and concurrent duplicate requests.
- Daily dashboards show payment state counts and age, scheme/ledger reconciliation breaks, overdue queued payments,
  `POSTING_FAILED`, fraud holds older than four hours, scheduler heartbeat, and notification delivery.
- Monthly controls prove one statement per account and period, complete ledger sourcing, and a reconciled regulatory
  return input before the fifth working day.
- Security tests prove 401/403/404 semantics, tenant isolation, staff-only field exclusion, and no full account number
  or sort code in logs, responses, or notifications.
