# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its scheduled payment worker, persistence, security boundary, and the
scheme, fraud, ledger, and notification integrations. It is based on the implementation and the operational facts in
`okf/`, as at 4 November 2026. The scores are qualitative and should be recalibrated against production incident and
volume data before release.

Scores are on a 1–10 scale: severity (S) measures customer, money, regulatory, or data impact; occurrence (O) measures
likelihood under normal operation; detection (D) measures how unlikely the failure is to be detected promptly (10 is
hardest to detect). `RPN = S × O × D`. A high RPN is a prioritisation signal, not a probability estimate.

| F8 | Scheme timeout hides an accepted payment | Scheme timeout is not caught by `PaymentDispatcher`; the transaction rolls back, although the scheme may have accepted the unique `P{id}` instruction. | Customer receives an error and may retry; the payment can still leave the account, or the local record is absent. | Scheme settlement cannot be matched; finance investigates an exception; complaints and operations lack a payment reference. | Query scheme status for every timeout; reconcile scheme settlement against local payments and alert on orphan scheme references. | 10 | 4 | 9 | 360 | Catch timeout, persist an `UNKNOWN`/reconciliation state before returning, query by the same scheme reference, and make retry/status recovery idempotent. | F9 | Payment is submitted but ledger posting is absent or duplicated | Ledger timeout may be after booking. | F10 | Payment queue runs on a bank holiday or does not run | `WorkingDays` excludes weekends only; bank holidays are not modeled. | F14 | Customer is not notified, or notification contains sensitive data | Notifications are best effort and not retried; current code sends only last four digits, but template/config changes could violate that rule. | Customer may think a payment was not sent, miss a review/failure, or suffer a data-protection breach. | Support and complaints load increases; a full account number in a message is reportable. | Notification success/failure metrics, outbox age, template contract tests, DLP scanning, and alert on full account/sort-code patterns in payloads/logs. | 7 | 5 | 6 | 210 | Use an outbox linked to the durable payment state with safe retries/deduplication; enforce an allow-listed template payload and automated redaction/DLP tests. |

## Release priorities

The release should not proceed without addressing F7 (unscreened payments), F8 (ambiguous scheme outcomes), F9/F11
(money movement idempotency and duplicate payment), and F4/F5 (durable and fresh account data). F6 and F10 are the next
operational controls because they create stranded funds or missed promised dates. The remaining actions should be tracked
with measurable owners, alert thresholds, and evidence in the operational readiness review.

## Evidence and test baseline

The repository test suite uses MockMvc, signed test tokens, fake integrations, and a mutable UK clock. Running the
mandated `mvn -o -q test` completed successfully on 4 November 2026. The passing suite is useful coverage of current API
behavior, including ledger timeout simulation, but it does not demonstrate production readiness for the unresolved
risks above: in particular, it cannot prove fraud screening is wired, scheme timeout recovery is durable, bank holidays
are handled, or concurrent/idempotent payment submission is safe.
