# Accounts service operational FMEA

**Review date:** 4 November 2026  
**Scope:** customer and staff API, payment dispatch and queueing, data persistence, security boundaries, and the
integrations described in `okf/`. This is an operational review of the repository as it stands, including known gaps;
it is not an assertion that every listed failure has already occurred.

The scores use the conventional 1–10 FMEA scale: **S** (severity) is 1 = negligible, 10 = customer-money,
regulatory, security, or prolonged service impact; **O** (occurrence) is 1 = rare, 10 = expected/frequent under normal
operation; **D** (detection) is 1 = almost certain detection before impact, 10 = unlikely to be detected before impact.
The risk priority number is `RPN = S × O × D`. Scores are relative and should be recalibrated with production
incident and monitoring data. RPNs of 200 or more should be treated as release blockers; 100–199 require an owned
mitigation and due date.

## Failure modes

| F-01 | Payment is sent without fraud screening | `FraudScreeningClient` exists, but `PaymentService` calls `PaymentDispatcher` directly. FRD-7 therefore is not enforced. | A fraudulent payment can be sent; the customer and bank may suffer an unrecoverable loss. | Fraud team has no review item; financial-crime controls and audit evidence are bypassed. | Code review can see the missing call, but there is no runtime control or metric proving every payment was screened. | 10 | 8 | 8 | **640** | Make screening a mandatory state transition before dispatch. Fail closed on error/2-second timeout, persist the decision and correlation id, map `REFER` to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, and alert on any non-screened dispatch. |
| F-02 | Fraud `REFER`/unavailable is treated as reject, accept, or lost | The required hold semantics are not implemented in the payment flow; the unavailable exception is currently unhandled by `PaymentService`. | A legitimate payment may be rejected or a customer may receive a 5xx with no reliable status; accepting it would violate policy. | Fraud review SLA (four hours) is missed or the team cannot find the payment; support sees inconsistent status. | 5xx logs and customer complaints; no dashboard for screening latency, decisions, or held-payment age. | 9 | 7 | 7 | **441** | Add explicit `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` persistence, customer `PAYMENT_UNDER_REVIEW` notification, fraud inbox/review callback or controlled release path, age/SLA alerts, and contract tests for ACCEPT/REFER/REJECT/timeout. |
| F-03 | Scheme accepts a payment but the service loses the local record | A scheme timeout may mean accepted. `SchemeTimeoutException` is not caught; the enclosing transaction rolls back the payment row and account changes. | Customer sees an error and may retry; the payee may still be paid, causing duplicate payment or an untraceable debit. | Reconciliation cannot match the scheme instruction to a local payment; operations and complaints must investigate manually. | Scheme timeout logs/external settlement mismatch; there is no status query or durable outbox. | 10 | 4 | 9 | **360** | Persist `RECEIVED` before calling the scheme, catch timeout, query `/v2/instructions/{reference}`, and reconcile unknown outcomes. Use `P` + payment id for every retry and add a durable outbox/state machine. |
| F-04 | Ledger posting is duplicated or left unresolved after timeout | A timeout can mean the ledger booked the debit. The code generates `ledgerReference` only immediately before posting and marks `POSTING_FAILED`; an operational retry could generate a new reference. | The customer may be debited twice, or the payment may be sent but the balance/ledger status remains wrong. | Finance's 07:00 reference reconciliation breaks; operations must repost from exceptions and complaints may be raised. | `POSTING_FAILED` is available to the daily exceptions report; the ambiguous booking itself is not automatically verified. | 10 | 5 | 5 | **250** | Generate and persist `ledgerReference` before the first attempt, query/repost idempotently with that same reference, make the exception workflow explicit, and alert on age/count of `POSTING_FAILED`. |
| F-05 | Payment submitted on a bank holiday is rejected or mishandled | `WorkingDays` knows weekdays only; bank holidays are not modeled. The scheme rejects an invalid date with `DT01`. | A payment expected on the next working day is rejected or delayed, with an incorrect status/message. | Scheme rejects accumulate; operations and support handle avoidable exceptions; customer promise dates are wrong. | `DT01` responses and queued-past-date report, but only after the payment has reached the scheme. | 8 | 6 | 6 | **288** | Use a versioned UK bank-holiday calendar or scheme business-day API, test calendar boundaries, and alert on any `DT01` for a supposedly queued payment. |
| F-06 | Queued payment is not dispatched on time, or is dispatched twice | The 06:00 in-process scheduler has no durable job/lock/claim, no misfire recovery, and no explicit bank-holiday handling. | F-07 | Concurrent requests overspend an account or breach the daily limit | Balance and `paidToday` are read before the payment row and account debit; there is no visible row lock, optimistic version, or database-enforced invariant. | Multiple payments can be accepted against the same funds or daily limit; a later ledger correction may surprise the customer. | Scheme may receive payments finance must unwind; reconciliation and fraud limits become unreliable. | Ledger balance refresh at 02:00 may reveal the discrepancy; no admission-control or invariant metric is described. | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Atomically reserve funds and daily-limit capacity (locking/versioning or a ledger authorization), enforce a unique client idempotency key, and load-test parallel submissions. |
| F-08 | Client retry creates a second payment | There is no idempotency key on the create API; the scheme reference is generated from a new payment id, so retrying after a lost response creates a new scheme instruction. | A customer can pay twice while believing the first request failed. | Finance reconciliation sees two valid scheme/ledger references; support and complaints must arrange recovery. | Duplicate payee/amount patterns may be found after the fact; no retry correlation or duplicate-payment alert exists. | 10 | 6 | 8 | **480** | Require and persist an idempotency key scoped to tenant/customer, return the original result for repeats, and retain request correlation through scheme and ledger references. |
| F-09 | Customer-facing balance is stale or wrong | The balance is a copy overwritten from the ledger at 02:00. ## Release priorities

The first release gate should address F-01, F-02, F-03, F-08, F-07, and F-16: they can cause fraud, duplicate payment,
untraceable external settlement, or broad outage. F-04, F-05, F-06, F-09, F-10, F-11, F-13, and F-15 need named
owners and dates before production approval because they can create reconciliation, regulatory, or customer-money
breaks even when the API remains reachable.

At minimum, the operating model should provide these daily controls:

- scheme-versus-ledger reconciliation by payment reference at 07:00, with an owned queue for ambiguous outcomes;
- alerts for `POSTING_FAILED`, stale `QUEUED`, held-fraud payments approaching four hours, failed ledger refresh, and
  pending closures/statements approaching their deadlines;
- payment and authorization audit trails that retain tenant, caller, idempotency key, screening result, scheme
  reference, and ledger reference without logging full account numbers;
- tested backup/restore, dependency-failure, timeout-after-acceptance, duplicate-retry, bank-holiday, and concurrent
  payment scenarios.
