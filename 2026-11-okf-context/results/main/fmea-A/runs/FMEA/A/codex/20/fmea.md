# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its persistence, authentication and
authorization boundary, scheduled payment dispatch, and the scheme, fraud, ledger and
notification integrations described in `README.md` and `application.yml`. It is an
operational FMEA for the next release, not a substitute for a threat model, a capacity
test, or a reconciliation design.

Scores use the conventional 1–10 scale:

- **Severity (S):** 1 = negligible inconvenience; 10 = customer funds, regulatory,
  confidentiality, or multi-tenant impact.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 = rare with strong controls; 10 = frequent or readily
  triggered in normal operation.
- **Detection (D):** 1 = almost certain rapid detection; 10 = unlikely to be detected
  before customer or downstream impact. The RPN is `S × O × D`; higher is more urgent.

The scores are relative release-triage scores. They should be recalibrated against
production incident, latency, and reconciliation data after launch.

## Failure modes

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers and dependent systems/teams | Detection / control evidence | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F-01 | Payment accepted by the scheme but the API returns an error or the payment is lost | `SchemeTimeoutException` is not handled by `PaymentDispatcher`. A timeout can occur after the gateway accepted the instruction; the surrounding transaction can roll back the locally saved `RECEIVED` payment. | Customer may retry and create a duplicate payment. The scheme has an instruction that operations cannot find or reconcile. Support receives an unexplained debit/duplicate complaint. | Alert on 5xx and scheme timeout rate; compare scheme instructions with local payments by the idempotency reference. Current code has a stable `P{id}` reference but no recovery workflow. | 10 | 4 | 8 | **320** | Persist an outbox/payment before calling the scheme, handle unknown outcomes as `SUBMISSION_UNKNOWN`, use the stable reference for idempotent retry/query, and build a scheme-to-service reconciliation job. Add an integration test for a post-acceptance timeout. |
| F-02 | Ledger posting is uncertain after scheme submission | Ledger timeout is converted to `POSTING_FAILED`, but there is no retry or reconciliation path. The ledger may have booked the posting despite the timeout. | Funds can be sent through the scheme while the account/ledger state is wrong. Customers see a failed-looking payment; finance and operations must manually investigate. A retry can double-post without idempotency. | Alert on `POSTING_FAILED`, reconcile scheme receipts and ledger postings using `ledgerReference`, and expose an operations queue. Current tests verify the status but not recovery. | 10 | 4 | 7 | **280** | Make ledger posting idempotent on a durable reference, retry with backoff, distinguish timeout-before-booking from unknown outcome, and block/resolve payments through a reconciliation state machine before allowing another attempt. |
| F-03 | Local balance is debited despite an incomplete payment | `account.debit()` happens after scheme submission and before ledger success; it is persisted even when the ledger call times out and the payment becomes `POSTING_FAILED`. | Balance and transaction history can disagree with the core ledger. Customers may be denied later payments and support/finance may see a phantom debit. | Periodic balance-to-ledger reconciliation and alert on `POSTING_FAILED` with balance deltas. There is no transaction row for the debit and no explicit compensating action. | 9 | 5 | 7 | **315** | Establish one source of truth for balance, or record a pending/held debit and finalize it only after ledger confirmation. Add immutable transaction entries and automated compensation/reconciliation. |
| F-04 | Duplicate payment submitted | Concurrent requests can both read the same balance and daily total; there is no request idempotency key, account lock/version, or unique business constraint. A client retry after an ambiguous timeout can submit again. | Customer can be overcharged or exceed the daily limit; scheme, ledger, notifications and support cases contain duplicate records. | Detect repeated payee/amount/reference windows and daily-limit anomalies; monitor duplicate scheme references. Current `P{id}` prevents duplicate scheme instruction IDs only after a second local payment has already been created. | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Require an idempotency key with a unique constraint and return the original result. Serialize account debit/limit decisions using optimistic locking or a database lock; test concurrent requests and retry-after-timeout behavior. |
| F-05 | Queued payments remain queued or are dispatched more than once | The 06:00 scheduled job has no durable claim/lease, retry policy, dead-letter handling, or job health metric. A crashed instance, overlapping instances, or a failure part-way through the loop can leave work ambiguous. | Payments miss their execution date or are sent twice; customers and the scheme see late/duplicate payments. Operations has no reliable backlog view. | Alert on queued age, due-but-not-submitted count, job duration/failure, and per-payment state age. The job currently selects all due rows and calls the dispatcher directly. | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Use a claim/lease (`PROCESSING` plus owner/time) or a queue, make dispatch idempotent, retry safely, record attempts/errors, and provide a replay/dead-letter runbook. |
| F-06 | Fraud screening is unavailable or bypassed | `HttpFraudScreeningClient` exists and has a timeout, but no production payment path calls `FraudScreeningClient`; there is no fail-closed decision in `PaymentDispatcher`. | Suspicious payments can reach the scheme without screening. Fraud, compliance and incident-response teams lose a control and may face financial and regulatory exposure. | Compare payment volume with fraud-screening decision volume; alert when the ratio is not one-to-one. Add a release test that a payment cannot dispatch without `ACCEPT`. | 10 | 3 | 9 | **270** | Integrate screening before scheme submission, persist the decision and correlation ID, fail closed or hold for review on `REFER`/unavailability, and alert on missing decisions. |
| F-07 | Notification failure or slow notification blocks the payment request | Notifications have no configured connect/read timeout. `notifySent` catches runtime errors, but the blocking HTTP call can tie up request threads until the client/network default fails. | Payment completion is delayed or the API times out even though the payment may have completed. Customers miss confirmation; support sees inconsistent UI state. | Track request latency, thread-pool saturation, notification error/latency and payment-to-notification lag. Current logging is only a warning and has no metric or correlation ID. | 6 | 6 | 8 | **288** | Make notifications asynchronous via an outbox, configure bounded timeouts, retry with backoff and deduplicate by payment ID. Treat notification as non-critical to payment completion. |
| F-08 | Non-2xx responses from external services become generic 5xx failures | Scheme/ledger/fraud clients only consistently translate selected exceptions; HTTP server errors and malformed success bodies can escape as runtime exceptions. | Customers get opaque errors and may retry. Payments can be left in an ambiguous state; dependent teams cannot distinguish rejection, outage, and bad integration data. | Monitor 5xx by endpoint and dependency, structured error logs with payment/reference IDs, and contract tests for 4xx/5xx/malformed payloads. | 8 | 5 | 6 | **240** | Define explicit error mapping for each dependency, validate response schemas, preserve payment state on every failure, and expose dependency/error reason metrics without leaking sensitive data. |
| F-09 | Service restart loses all account and payment state | `application.yml` configures `jdbc:h2:mem:accounts;DB_CLOSE_DELAY=-1` and Hibernate `create-drop`; the repository has fixture initialization rather than a production migration/persistence setup. | A restart or redeploy can erase balances, payments, cases and closure requests. Customers and every downstream team lose authoritative account history. | Startup data-integrity check, persistence smoke test across restart, and alert if the configured datasource is non-production. Current health endpoint is not backed by an actuator dependency. | 10 | 3 | 9 | **270** | Use a durable, managed database in deployed environments, migrations (Flyway/Liquibase), backups and restore drills. Fail startup when an in-memory datasource is used outside tests. |
| F-10 | Cross-tenant or staff-only data exposure | Authorization depends on token claims (`tenant`, `roles`, `scope`) and distributed resource checks. A claim-mapping or future endpoint omission could bypass the policy; staff-only entities are present even though not all have controllers. | Confidential customer, risk-score, account, or internal-note data is exposed to another tenant/customer. This is a severe regulatory and trust incident. | Automated matrix tests for every endpoint and role/tenant combination, audit logs for denied and privileged access, and security review of new controllers. Existing tests cover important account/customer cases but not a complete endpoint matrix. | 10 | 2 | 8 | **160** | Centralize tenant predicates, deny by default, add contract/security tests for every endpoint and object type, validate issuer/audience/key rotation, and emit immutable access audit events. |
| F-11 | Authentication/authorization fails closed for legitimate callers or accepts stale/invalid claims | Identity-service key rotation, malformed role/scope claims, or a mismatch between token and policy is not operationally surfaced. The public key is packaged on the classpath, and there is no visible issuer/audience configuration. | Customers cannot view/pay; support and admin teams cannot handle cases. If validation is weakened during troubleshooting, the same boundary can become a security incident. | Track 401/403 rates by endpoint/tenant/client, key-rotation drills, token validation tests, and identity-service dependency monitoring. | 8 | 4 | 5 | **160** | Configure issuer/audience and managed key rotation, document token contract, alert on changes in 401/403 baselines, and test key rollover before release. |
| F-12 | Database unavailable, slow, or exhausted | All API reads/writes use JPA; there are no explicit pool, query, retry, or circuit-breaker settings visible in the repository. Lists and payment daily-limit checks can grow with data. | API latency and error rates rise; payments/cases/closure requests fail. Thread and connection exhaustion can cascade to every tenant and the on-call team. | Database connectivity, pool utilization, query latency, error rate, JVM/thread metrics, and endpoint SLO alerts. Add load tests for account lists, transactions, and payment creation. | 9 | 5 | 5 | **225** | Use managed DB HA/backups, bounded connection pools/timeouts, indexes and pagination, slow-query monitoring, and an overload policy. Keep payment state transitions retryable and idempotent. |
| F-13 | Closure workflow is stranded or creates an invalid state | The repository/model supports approval fields, but the exposed controller only creates requests and lists pending requests; there is no approval/rejection endpoint that changes account status. `ClosureRequest` also uses `Instant.now()` rather than the injected clock. | Customers cannot close accounts; approvers and support teams retain an unprocessed queue. If a separate unreviewed path is added, account and closure state may diverge. | Metric for pending closure age/count, workflow end-to-end test, and reconciliation of `APPROVED` requests to account `CLOSED` status. | 7 | 6 | 8 | **336** | Complete the approve/reject transition with tenant, approver and segregation-of-duties checks; atomically update request and account, use the service clock, and alert on aged pending requests. |
| F-14 | Operational incident is not detected or correlated quickly | Logging is only `WARN` at the root, there is no metrics/tracing dependency or visible actuator implementation, and external calls do not consistently carry correlation/payment references. | Outages, stuck payments, security anomalies and data mismatches persist longer. Customers, finance, fraud, support and on-call teams cannot establish impact quickly. | Synthetic probes for authenticated and payment journeys, logs searched by payment/reference ID, dependency dashboards, and explicit SLO/error-budget alerts. | 8 | 6 | 8 | **384** | Add Micrometer/Actuator or the platform equivalent, structured logs and trace propagation, dependency and business metrics (state age, queue depth, reconciliation breaks), dashboards, alerts and runbooks. |

## Release priorities

The highest initial RPNs are F-14 (384), F-04/F-05 (360), F-13 (336), F-01
(320), and F-03 (315). For a banking release, the numerical ranking should not
override severity: F-01, F-02, F-03, F-06, and F-09 are release blockers until
there is a tested recovery/reconciliation story and durable persistence. F-10 is
also a release blocker if the service is exposed to real tenant data.

The minimum evidence to collect before release is:

1. A failure-injection test matrix covering scheme timeout before/after acceptance,
   ledger timeout before/after booking, notification outage, database restart, and
   scheduler crash/retry.
2. A reconciliation report proving that every local payment has a matching scheme
   outcome and ledger posting, with no unexplained balance delta.
3. Load and concurrency tests for payment creation, daily limits, account reads and
   the queued-payment worker.
4. Security tests covering every endpoint across customer, support, approver, admin,
   wrong-tenant and missing-scope identities.
5. Live dashboards and runbooks for payment state age, queued backlog, posting
   failures, fraud decisions, dependency health, 401/403/5xx rates, and database
   health.
