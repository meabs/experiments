# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its H2/JPA persistence, JWT-based
multi-tenant authorization, payment and closure workflows, scheduled payment job,
and the scheme, fraud, ledger and notification integrations described in
`README.md` and `application.yml`. It is an operational risk review of the
implementation as supplied, not a claim that every recommendation is already
required by the product specification.

Scores are on a 1–10 scale:

- **Severity (S):** 1 is negligible; 10 is customer-money loss, a material
  confidentiality/integrity breach, or a prolonged service-wide outage.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 is unlikely under normal operation; 10 is expected or
  frequent without a change.
- **Detection (D):** 1 is almost certain to be detected promptly by an existing
  control; 10 is likely to remain undetected until a customer, reconciliation or
  dependent team reports it.
- **RPN = S × O × D.** RPN is a prioritisation aid, not a substitute for treating
  any high-severity security or money-movement risk. The scores assume production
  traffic and real external integrations; the repository's API tests use fakes.

## FMEA register

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers | Effect on dependent systems and teams | Detection / existing control | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |

| F1 | Payment is accepted by the scheme but the service returns an error or loses the local record | Scheme timeout after acceptance; `SchemeTimeoutException` is not recovered, and the surrounding transaction may roll back | Payment may leave the account without a visible payment record, or the customer may retry and see a duplicate/rejection; uncertainty over whether money moved | Operations and finance cannot reliably reconcile scheme instruction to the service; support must investigate manually | Scheme timeout/error logs and scheme reconciliation, but no durable ambiguous state or alert is created | 10 | 5 | 8 | **400** | Use an outbox/state machine with a durable idempotency key (`P{id}`), persist `SUBMISSION_UNKNOWN`, reconcile/query the scheme, and make retries safe. Alert on aged unknowns. |
| F2 | Scheme payment is submitted but ledger posting fails or is applied after the timeout | `PaymentDispatcher` submits first, then posts; a ledger timeout produces `POSTING_FAILED`; there is no retry/reconciliation job | Customer balance can be wrong in this service, and payment status may not reflect the ledger; available funds and future payments may be incorrect | Core ledger, finance and operations must resolve a cross-system break; duplicate recovery is only safe if the ledger honours the reference | `POSTING_FAILED` status and a warning log exist, and the ledger reference is stored; no metric, alert or automated repair is present | 10 | 5 | 7 | **350** | Make ledger posting idempotent and retryable by a stable reference; persist an explicit reconciliation state, run a repair/reconciliation worker, and alert on age/count. Reconcile local balance to the ledger rather than treating local debit as authoritative. |
| F3 | Payment is sent twice | Client retry after a timeout, scheduled job overlap, or manual replay; the API has no request idempotency key and no locking/claiming of queued rows | Duplicate transfer or confusing duplicate rejection; the customer may be charged or may have to contact support | Scheme and ledger receive duplicate attempts; operations and fraud teams handle disputes and reversals | Scheme test fake rejects duplicate `P{id}`, but production behaviour is an integration contract; no duplicate-attempt metric or idempotency response is exposed | 10 | 5 | 7 | **350** | Require an idempotency key, enforce a unique tenant/key constraint, atomically claim queued work, use a stable payment reference across all retries, and return the existing payment for a replay. |
| F4 | Cross-tenant or unauthorised account/customer data is returned or changed | A future endpoint/query omits the tenant predicate, trusts a token claim without issuer/audience/key-rotation validation, or bypasses `AccessPolicy`; this is especially risky as the service grows | Confidential account, transaction, customer or staff data disclosure; unauthorised payment or case activity | Regulatory incident; identity, support, security and tenant teams lose trust in the API and may need tenant-wide containment | Current controls include JWT signature validation, method security, tenant checks and 404 masking; tests cover representative access paths, but there is no stated issuer/audience configuration or systematic negative/tenant fuzz testing | 10 | 3 | 8 | **240** | Configure and verify issuer/audience/key rotation; centralise tenant-scoped repository methods; add matrix tests for every endpoint (tenant, role, scope, owner) and security monitoring for denied and anomalous access. |
| F5 | Fraud screening is bypassed | Fraud client and decisions exist, but no payment path calls `FraudScreeningClient`; `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` is unused | Fraudulent payment can be sent without screening, increasing loss and customer harm | Fraud team receives no review work or decision trail; scheme/ledger and incident teams absorb downstream consequences | Code review can find the unused integration; there is no runtime signal that payments were screened | 10 | 7 | 10 | **700** | Put screening before scheme submission, fail closed on unavailable/invalid results, persist decision and model/version, implement `REFER` review and `REJECT` handling, and alert on any payment without a screening record. |
| F6 | Overspending bypasses balance or daily-limit checks | Concurrent payment requests read the same balance and paid-today total; entities have no optimistic/pessimistic locking or atomic reservation | Customer can initiate payments beyond balance or daily limit; later payments fail or the account becomes overdrawn | Ledger/scheme reconciliation breaks; support and finance handle rejects, reversals and complaints | Per-request checks and API tests cover sequential cases only; no concurrency metric or invariant check is evident | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Add version/row locking or an atomic funds reservation, make daily-limit accounting transactional, and reconcile balance plus outstanding payment exposure continuously. |
| F7 | Queued payments are delayed, skipped, or repeatedly attempted | Single in-process `@Scheduled` job at 06:00; no distributed lock, durable work claim, retry/backoff, dead-letter state, or holiday calendar; an instance outage at run time loses the trigger | Payment misses its execution date or remains unclear; customer cash-flow and scheduled obligations are affected | Scheme receives late bursts; support and operations manually replay jobs and explain missed cutoffs | `QUEUED` status and execution date are queryable, but there is no job health/lag metric or alert and no bank-holiday handling (only Monday–Friday) | 8 | 6 | 8 | **384** | Move dispatch to a durable queue/worker with leasing, retries and dead letters; expose queue age and run outcome metrics; use a business-holiday calendar and an explicit missed-run recovery process. |
| F8 | Account balance is debited locally even though the ledger did not book the debit | `account.debit()` happens before ledger call; on timeout the entity is still saved with `POSTING_FAILED`, while local balance remains changed | Customer sees an unavailable balance despite no core-ledger debit, or receives inconsistent balances across channels | Ledger, statements and reconciliation disagree; finance must decide whether to restore, post or reverse the local amount | `POSTING_FAILED` and ledger reference provide a starting point, but no invariant or automated reconciliation is implemented | 9 | 5 | 7 | **315** | Do not treat the local balance as settled until an idempotent ledger acknowledgement; model reserved/posted funds separately, and repair all `POSTING_FAILED` records from ledger truth. |
| F9 | Notifications are lost or payment responses become unreliable because notification delivery is synchronous | Notification call has no configured timeout/retry/queue; exceptions are swallowed after logging, so delivery is silently lost (and a slow dependency holds the request) | Payment may complete without confirmation; customers miss important payment information | Notification team cannot distinguish lost messages from service behaviour; support sees “I was not told” cases | Warning log only; no notification outbox, delivery status, latency metric or alert | 6 | 6 | 9 | **324** | Write an outbox event in the same transaction, deliver asynchronously with bounded timeouts/retries and provider idempotency, and track sent/failed/dead-letter status. |
| F10 | Service or database is unavailable, or data disappears on restart | The configured datasource is in-memory H2 with `create-drop`; no production database/backup/HA/readiness configuration is present in the repository; unbounded request work can also exhaust instances | Accounts, payments, cases and closure requests are unavailable or lost; customers cannot see balances or initiate support/payment actions | Identity, scheme, ledger, notification and support teams receive a flood of failures; recovery and auditability are compromised | Spring startup/request errors may be visible in logs; `/actuator/health` is permitted but no actuator dependency or health implementation is present | 10 | 3 | 9 | **270** | Use a managed durable database with migrations, backups and restore drills; configure connection pools/timeouts, readiness/liveness probes and graceful degradation; add capacity/load tests and alerts. |
| F11 | Authentication or authorisation configuration is unavailable or misinterpreted | Identity key rotation/issuer changes are not represented in configuration; malformed/missing claims can produce denied access or unsafe assumptions in new code | Legitimate customers and staff receive 401/403, or a claims mapping defect grants the wrong role/scope | Identity and support teams get an outage; tenant isolation and audit obligations are at risk if the defect is permissive | Signed JWT validation, stateless sessions, scopes and roles are enforced; current config uses a bundled public key, with no visible key-refresh or auth failure dashboard | 9 | 4 | 7 | **252** | Use issuer/JWK discovery with controlled rotation and validation, contract-test claims from the identity service, alert on 401/403 spikes by tenant/client, and maintain a break-glass operational procedure. |
| F12 | Internal notes, risk scores or internal case comments leak to customers | A new response mapper returns staff-only fields, or an endpoint fetches comments without filtering `internal`; the model stores sensitive data beside customer-visible data | Privacy, safety and reputational harm; customers may see fraud, hardship or dispute information | Security, compliance and support teams must investigate and notify affected tenants/customers | README documents the rule, but the supplied API does not expose note/comment controllers and there is no response-level redaction test suite | 9 | 3 | 8 | **216** | Separate staff/customer DTOs and repository queries; deny by default; add automated contract tests asserting internal fields never appear for each customer endpoint, plus access logging and DLP monitoring. |
| F13 | Closure request remains pending or closure state is inconsistent | The API creates and lists requests, but the supplied `ClosureController` has no approve/reject endpoint and no transaction that changes `Account.status` | Customer cannot complete closure and may believe the account is closed when it is still open; new activity may continue | Approvers and support have an inbox but no completion path; operations must change data manually, weakening dual-control auditability | Pending list and duplicate-pending conflict are visible; no SLA/age alert and no end-to-end approval test exists | 7 | 6 | 8 | **336** | Implement an authenticated, tenant-scoped approve/reject workflow with separation of requester/approver, row locking, account-state checks, audit timestamps/reason, and alerts for aged pending requests. |
| F14 | Incorrect payment date/cutoff causes premature or late execution | Business calendar is only weekday logic, clock/time-zone configuration can drift, and the cutoff comparison has no explicit boundary/holiday policy | Payment executes on the wrong day or misses a contractual cutoff; customer may incur fees or miss an obligation | Scheme receives invalid timing; support, payments operations and complaints teams handle avoidable incidents | `Clock` is injectable and tests cover one cutoff/weekend path; no production clock, holiday, or cutoff-boundary monitoring is evident | 7 | 5 | 7 | **245** | Centralise a timezone-aware business calendar, define cutoff inclusivity and holidays, monitor clock drift, and test boundary/holiday/year-end cases. |
| F15 | Downstream errors surface as generic 500s or are not actionable | HTTP clients handle only selected exception types; notification has no timeout; `ApiExceptionHandler` does not map integration failures and there is no correlation/metrics instrumentation | Customers see failed or hanging requests without a safe retry instruction; payment outcome may be ambiguous | Dependent teams cannot quickly identify which integration or payment is affected; incident triage is slower | Stack traces/warnings may be in logs, but no correlation ID, integration-specific counters, latency/SLOs or alert rules are present | 7 | 6 | 8 | **336** | Add consistent timeouts, bounded retries only for idempotent operations, typed safe 502/503 responses, correlation/payment IDs, structured logs, metrics/traces and SLO alerts. |

## Priorities

Immediate release blockers are **F5** (payments bypass fraud), **F1–F3/F8**
(ambiguous or inconsistent money movement), and **F6** (concurrent limit/balance
failure). These can create financial loss even when the API is technically
available. Next, make the payment and notification workflows durable and
observable (**F7, F9, F13, F15**), then harden persistence, identity and data
isolation (**F4, F10–F12, F14**).

Before release, add failure-injection tests for scheme and ledger timeouts both
before and after acceptance, concurrent payment tests, duplicate HTTP retries,
job restart/overlap, identity key rotation, tenant-matrix access, and database
restore. Reconcile a representative set of payment records against scheme and
ledger test environments and retain the resulting evidence with the release.
