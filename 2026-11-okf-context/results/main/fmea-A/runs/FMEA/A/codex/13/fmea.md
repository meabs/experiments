# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot API, its H2/JPA persistence, authentication and tenant authorization, payment
dispatch, scheduled payment processing, account closure and support-case workflows, and the configured scheme, fraud,
ledger and notification integrations. It is based on the repository implementation and `application.yml`, not on
production telemetry or infrastructure controls that are not present here.

Scores use the usual 1–10 FMEA scale: Severity (S) is the impact if the failure occurs, Occurrence (O) is the expected
likelihood in operation (1 = rare, 10 = frequent), and Detection (D) is the likelihood that the failure will escape
detection before customer or downstream impact (1 = almost certain to detect, 10 = unlikely to detect). RPN is
`S × O × D`; higher values should be addressed first. RPN is a prioritisation aid, not a substitute for treating any
severity-9/10 security or money-movement risk as release-blocking.

## FMEA table

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers and dependent systems/teams | Detection | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F-01 | A payment is submitted twice | `POST /payments` has no idempotency key or unique business reference. Client retry after a timeout can create a second `Payment` and send a second scheme instruction. | Customer may be debited twice and the payee may receive two transfers. Ledger, reconciliation, fraud and support teams must unwind duplicate money movement. | Reconcile payment IDs, scheme references and ledger references; alert on repeated customer/reference/amount tuples. | 10 | 5 | 8 | **400** | Require an idempotency key, persist it with a unique constraint, and make scheme and ledger operations idempotent on a stable payment reference. Return the original result for safe retries. |
| F-02 | Scheme accepted a payment but the API reports failure or loses the record | The scheme call is an external side effect inside the database transaction. A timeout may happen after the gateway accepts the instruction; an exception can roll back the local payment row. | Customer retries and sends a duplicate; the scheme has an orphan instruction with no local status. Operations cannot reliably tell whether money moved. | Scheme-to-service reconciliation by `P{id}`; gateway timeout/unknown-outcome metrics and an exception queue. | 10 | 4 | 8 | **320** | Use an outbox/state machine with `UNKNOWN` status, query the scheme by idempotency reference before retrying, and provide a reconciliation job and operator workflow. |
| F-03 | Scheme payment completed but ledger posting is missing or inconsistent | `PaymentDispatcher` submits to the scheme, debits the local `Account`, then posts to the ledger. A ledger timeout produces `POSTING_FAILED`; there is no automatic retry or durable posting queue. A timeout can also occur after the ledger booked the posting. | Transfer can leave the bank while the core ledger or account balance disagrees. Customers see an incorrect balance/status; finance, ledger and incident teams must reconcile manually. | Compare scheme receipts, `POSTING_FAILED` rows, account balances and ledger postings; alert on any non-zero ageing `POSTING_FAILED` count. | 10 | 4 | 7 | **280** | Make ledger posting idempotent using a stable reference, persist an outbox before dispatch, retry with backoff, and block/hold completion until reconciliation resolves the outcome. |
| F-04 | Concurrent payments bypass balance or daily-limit checks | Requests independently read the same balance and `paidToday` total; there is no row lock, version check, or atomic reservation. | Two payments can exceed available funds or the daily limit. The account can become overdrawn and ledger/customer balances diverge. | Alert on negative balances, daily-limit breaches and reconciliation differences; concurrency/load tests. | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Atomically reserve funds and daily allowance, or use optimistic/pessimistic locking with a retry on conflict. Add a database constraint and concurrent integration tests. |
| F-05 | Queued payment is delayed, lost, or dispatched more than once | The scheduler is a single polling loop at 06:00 with no claim/lease, retry state, misfire recovery, or distributed lock. Multiple service instances can process the same `QUEUED` row. | Customer payment is late or duplicated; scheme, ledger and support teams receive inconsistent status and settlement timing. | Dashboard for queued items past execution date, scheduler heartbeat, duplicate scheme-reference alerts, and job duration/failure alerts. | 9 | 4 | 8 | **288** | Use a durable work queue or claimed rows with leases and an idempotent dispatcher; retry failed items, record attempts, and add a recovery command for missed runs. |
| F-06 | Fraud screening is bypassed entirely | `FraudScreeningClient` and its timeout are configured, but no production payment path invokes it. | Fraudulent or high-risk payments can reach the scheme. Customers, the bank, fraud operations and loss/recovery teams bear direct financial and regulatory impact. | Compare every payment against fraud-screening decisions; alert when payment volume exists without a corresponding screening event. | 10 | 6 | 10 | **600** | Make screening a mandatory pre-dispatch state transition. Fail closed on `REJECT` and unavailable/unknown decisions (with an explicit reviewed override), persist the decision, and test the integration in the payment API tests. |
| F-07 | Payment rejection or integration error is returned as the wrong outcome | Scheme handling maps client errors but not all server/status failures; ledger handling only converts `ResourceAccessException`. Other HTTP errors can escape as generic 500s, and transaction rollback may hide the payment state. | Customers cannot tell whether to retry; a retry can duplicate an accepted external operation. Support receives opaque failures and downstream systems lack a dependable status. | Track 4xx/5xx/timeout rates by integration and correlate every request to a persisted payment state; alert on unclassified exceptions. | 8 | 5 | 6 | **240** | Normalize all integration outcomes, persist `UNKNOWN` for ambiguous results, use bounded retries only with idempotency, and expose a stable customer-safe status/error contract. |
| F-08 | Customer is not told that a payment was sent | Notification failure is caught and only logged at WARN; there is no outbox or retry. The notification client also has no configured timeout. | Payment succeeds but the customer misses confirmation, increasing duplicate submissions and support contacts. Notification and support teams cannot distinguish delivery failure from payment failure. | Notification success/failure metrics, delivery acknowledgements, and alerting on unsent/outbox age. | 6 | 5 | 8 | **240** | Publish notifications through a durable outbox after payment state is committed, add connect/read timeouts, retry with backoff and deduplicate by payment ID. |
| F-09 | Customer or staff data crosses tenant or role boundaries | A future endpoint can omit the repeated tenant/resource check; the model has several IDs that are not foreign-key constrained, increasing the impact of bad associations. Security relies on claims and application code. | Cross-bank exposure of balances, transactions, PII, risk scores or internal notes; unauthorized payment or regulatory breach. Admins, security, legal and affected-bank teams must respond. | Automated cross-tenant negative tests for every endpoint, authorization audit logs, canary access alerts, and periodic access review. | 10 | 2 | 8 | **160** | Centralize authorization checks, require tenant-scoped repository methods, add database foreign keys/tenant consistency constraints, and test every role/scope combination including forged tenant claims. |
| F-10 | Authenticated callers can create workflow records without the documented write scope | Closure-request and case-creation endpoints are authenticated but do not require `accounts:write`, although the README says data changes need that scope. | A compromised or over-broad token can create closure requests or support cases, causing workflow noise and potentially delaying account operations. Support and approver teams must triage abuse. | Authorization contract tests that omit the scope; audit rate/actor monitoring for unusual request creation. | 6 | 5 | 6 | **180** | Add explicit scope checks to every mutating endpoint, keep role/resource checks separate, and include a policy matrix test for missing/extra scopes. |
| F-11 | The account database is lost or reset on restart/deploy | `application.yml` uses an in-memory H2 database with `DB_CLOSE_DELAY=-1`, `ddl-auto: create-drop`, and fixture initialization. This is unsuitable as the production persistence profile. | Accounts, balances, payments, cases and closure state disappear or revert to fixtures. Customers and every dependent team lose authoritative state; recovery may be impossible without an external backup. | Startup configuration scan, health checks, persistence smoke test across restart, and backup/restore drills. | 10 | 3 | 7 | **210** | Separate test and production profiles; use the managed durable database, migrations, backups, restore verification, and a startup guard that refuses an in-memory/create-drop database outside tests. |
| F-12 | Closure requests cannot be completed | The repository contains request creation and pending-list APIs, but no approve/reject endpoint or service that changes the account to `CLOSED`. | Customers and approvers can raise and see requests but accounts remain open. Closure operations, compliance and support teams must use an out-of-band process, risking stale access and payments after a requested closure. | Ageing pending-request report and an end-to-end test from request through approval and closed-account payment rejection. | 8 | 8 | 4 | **256** | Implement a separately authorized approve/reject workflow with maker-checker enforcement, atomic account-state transition, audit trail, and alerts for overdue pending requests. |
| F-13 | Cut-off and execution date are wrong | Cut-off uses `isAfter`, so exactly 15:30 is treated as same-day dispatch; `WorkingDays` excludes weekends but not UK bank holidays. The clock is UK-local but integration business calendars are not consulted. | Payments may miss scheme cut-off or execute on a non-settlement holiday. Customers and treasury/settlement teams see unexpected dates and may incur late-payment consequences. | Boundary tests at 15:29:59/15:30/15:30:01, bank-holiday calendar tests, and compare requested versus scheme execution dates. | 7 | 5 | 6 | **210** | Define the cut-off inclusively, use a versioned bank-holiday/business-calendar service, and persist the calculation inputs and chosen execution date for audit. |
| F-14 | Service or integration outage is not detected quickly | Only a public health URL is permitted; no application metrics, integration health/readiness checks, request correlation, payment backlog metric, or alert configuration is present. Root logging is WARN. | Customers experience unexplained errors or stale payment states. On-call, fraud, ledger, settlement and support teams learn from complaints or reconciliation rather than an alert. | Synthetic authenticated journeys, dependency SLO dashboards, logs with correlation IDs, and alerts on error rate, latency, queue age and reconciliation gaps. | 8 | 6 | 9 | **432** | Add Micrometer metrics/tracing, structured audit logs with secret-safe correlation IDs, readiness checks appropriate to dependency policy, and actionable SLO alerts/runbooks. |
| F-15 | Sensitive information is logged or returned beyond its intended audience | Payment views return full payee sort code/account number; exception/logging paths can include integration exception details. Staff-only data depends on each future controller applying filtering. | Exposure of payment and PII data increases fraud risk and breach scope; security, privacy and support teams must investigate. | API schema/data-classification tests, log redaction scans, DLP monitoring and access-log review. | 8 | 3 | 7 | **168** | Minimize payment responses (mask account numbers), use stable customer-safe errors, redact exception bodies, and enforce DTO-level role tests for internal notes/risk data. |

## Release priorities

Before release, treat F-06, F-01–F-05 and F-14 as priority controls. In particular, money movement should not be
released without mandatory fraud screening, idempotent external operations, durable recovery for ambiguous outcomes,
and concurrency protection. F-11 is a deployment blocker if this configuration is used outside tests. F-12 and F-10
should be resolved or explicitly accepted by the product and control owners because they create operational and access
control gaps even when the happy-path API tests pass.

The existing test suite covers normal API authorization and several scheme/ledger fake scenarios, including a ledger
timeout. It should be extended with the failure-oriented cases above: retries after ambiguous timeouts, concurrent
payments, scheduler restart/multi-instance behavior, mandatory fraud decisions, cross-tenant negative cases, scope
requirements on all mutations, and persistence/backup recovery.
