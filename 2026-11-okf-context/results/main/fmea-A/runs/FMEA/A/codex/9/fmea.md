# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and assumptions

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API and its payment, account, customer, case and closure workflows, including
the configured integrations with the payment scheme, fraud screening, ledger and notification services. It is based on
the implementation in this repository and the configuration in `src/main/resources/application.yml`. The scores are a
release-readiness assessment, not measured production rates.

Scores use the usual FMEA scale: severity (S) is the impact if the failure occurs, occurrence (O) is the expected
likelihood under normal operation, and detection (D) is how unlikely the failure is to be detected before customer or
downstream impact. Each is 1 (low/good) to 10 (high/bad). The risk priority number is `RPN = S × O × D`; higher RPNs
should be addressed first. Detection assumes the monitoring currently evident in the repository; adding the recommended
controls should reduce D.

## Failure-mode analysis

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers | Effect on dependent systems and teams | Detection | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F1 | Account, payment and support data disappears or reverts after a restart | The configured datasource is `jdbc:h2:mem:accounts;DB_CLOSE_DELAY=-1` and JPA uses `create-drop`; no production persistence or migration configuration is present | Balances, payment history, cases and closure requests can vanish; customers may see incorrect balances or be unable to prove a payment | Ledger, scheme and support records no longer reconcile with this service; operations face a broad recovery incident | Restart/restore test, database persistence alerts and reconciliation against the ledger; none are apparent in the application | 10 | 6 | 8 | 480 | Make the production database explicit and durable, use versioned migrations, backups and restore drills; fail startup for an unsafe in-memory profile; reconcile all external payment state after recovery |
| F2 | Payment is accepted by the scheme but the API returns an error and no durable local payment state exists | `PaymentDispatcher` does not catch `SchemeTimeoutException`; the scheme can accept before a timeout, while the surrounding transaction can roll back | Customer retries or believes payment failed; the same payment may be delayed, duplicated or become difficult to trace | Scheme has an instruction that accounts-service cannot reliably correlate; support and finance must investigate manually | Compare scheme instructions with local payments, alert on uncaught 5xx/timeouts and expose an incident/reconciliation queue | 10 | 5 | 8 | 400 | Persist a payment and stable idempotency/reference before submission; use an explicit `UNKNOWN`/`SUBMISSION_PENDING` state, query the scheme by reference, and make retry/recovery deliberate and auditable |
| F3 | Payment is sent to the scheme but ledger posting fails or times out | Scheme submission and ledger posting are separate calls; a timeout may occur before or after the ledger commits | Payment may leave the account while the customer sees `POSTING_FAILED`, or the displayed balance may be wrong | Ledger and local balance can diverge; finance and operations need manual repair; retries risk double posting | Reconcile scheme receipts, ledger postings and `PaymentStatus.POSTING_FAILED`; alert on aged failed postings | 10 | 5 | 7 | 350 | Use an outbox/workflow with durable retries, idempotent ledger references and a reconciliation worker; model `UNKNOWN` separately from confirmed failure and never infer commit status from a timeout |
| F4 | Same account is overspent or the daily limit is exceeded | `PaymentService` checks balance and `paidToday` before save/dispatch, with no row lock, optimistic version or idempotency key | Multiple concurrent requests can each pass validation; customer loses more than the balance/limit permits | Scheme and ledger receive excess instructions; fraud, complaints and manual reversal workload increase | Concurrent-payment tests, negative-balance/limit alerts and daily reconciliation; current API has no explicit signal | 9 | 5 | 8 | 360 | Add idempotency keys, atomic reservation/debit and database locking/versioning; enforce limits in the authoritative ledger as a second control |
| F5 | Duplicate queued payment is dispatched, or a queued batch stops part-way through | `QueuedPaymentJob` has no distributed lock; each replica can run at 06:00, and one uncaught integration error can abort the loop | Payment may be delayed, incorrectly marked rejected after a duplicate response, or have confusing status | Duplicate scheme calls and inconsistent local status; later due payments may remain queued; operations must replay jobs | Queue age, due-versus-dispatched metrics, job duration/failure alerts and scheme duplicate reports | 9 | 4 | 8 | 288 | Use a durable job/outbox with leasing or a scheduler lock, per-payment exception isolation, retry/backoff and a terminal/manual-review state; make duplicate handling idempotent |
| F6 | Payments bypass fraud screening | Fraud integration types and test fakes exist, but no production payment code calls `FraudScreeningClient` | Suspicious payments can be submitted without screening or review, causing financial loss and customer harm | Fraud team receives no decision/event; scheme and ledger process transactions that should have been held or rejected | Compare payment volume with fraud-screening volume and audit every payment decision; currently this is a silent gap | 10 | 4 | 10 | 400 | Put screening before scheme submission; persist `ACCEPT`/`REFER`/`REJECT`, fail closed or explicitly hold when unavailable, and alert on any payment without a decision |
| F7 | Payment cutoff or execution date is wrong | `WorkingDays` excludes weekends but not bank holidays; cutoff and clock/time-zone assumptions are not externally validated | Payment arrives earlier or later than promised, especially on UK holidays or clock/DST changes | Scheme receives instructions on an invalid business day; contact centre and payment operations handle complaints | Contract tests around DST, holidays and cutoff boundaries; compare execution dates with scheme acknowledgements | 7 | 5 | 6 | 210 | Use a versioned bank-holiday calendar and one explicit business-time service; publish the chosen execution date and alert on scheme date mismatches |
| F8 | Account balance and transaction/statement history disagree with the ledger | `PaymentDispatcher` debits the local `Account`, but does not create a `Transaction` or update `Statement`; the ledger is a separate system | Customer sees an incomplete or contradictory balance/history and may make decisions using stale data | Core ledger, statements and this API cannot be reconciled from one workflow; support and finance need corrections | Scheduled balance/transaction/ledger reconciliation, invariant checks and customer complaint trends; no such job is present | 9 | 6 | 8 | 432 | Define the ledger as source of truth, consume/post authoritative ledger events, derive balances/history consistently, and block release until reconciliation and repair tooling exist |
| F9 | Customer is told nothing about a completed or failed payment | Notification failure is caught and only logged at WARN; there is no durable notification outbox or retry | Customer may not know a payment was sent and may submit it again or contact support | Notification service has no guaranteed delivery record; support cannot distinguish delayed notification from a payment issue | Delivery receipts, notification backlog and payment-to-notification reconciliation; currently only logs are available | 6 | 6 | 8 | 288 | Write notifications to an outbox in the payment transaction, retry with idempotent message keys, and expose delivery status without blocking payment completion |
| F10 | Cross-tenant or unauthorized data/action is exposed | Security depends on JWT `tenant`/`roles` claims and repeated service-level checks; data relations have no visible foreign keys/tenant constraints, and `ownsAccount` looks up the customer by ID without also checking its tenant | Disclosure of balances, transactions, contact details or internal risk data; unauthorized payment/closure actions are possible if data or token claims are wrong | Breach of bank-tenant isolation, regulatory incident, identity/security response and loss of trust | Authorization matrix tests, tenant-fuzz tests, audit logs and alerts for cross-tenant access; no audit trail is visible | 10 | 3 | 8 | 240 | Validate issuer/audience/key rotation and token claims, enforce tenant-scoped queries and database constraints, centralize authorization, add negative tests for every endpoint and immutable security audit events |
| F11 | Identity or signing-key change causes all requests to fail, or invalid claims are accepted | Resource server uses a static classpath public key; issuer/audience validation and key rotation behavior are not configured in the repository | Customers and staff receive widespread 401/403 responses, or a bad trust configuration permits unauthorized access | All API consumers fail simultaneously; identity and security teams must coordinate emergency rollout | 401-rate, token validation and key-expiry alerts; health check does not validate authentication dependencies | 10 | 3 | 7 | 210 | Configure issuer/audience and managed JWKS/key rotation with overlap, test rotation and clock skew, and monitor authentication failure rate by cause |
| F12 | Closure request remains pending forever or an account is never actually closed | The API creates requests and lists pending requests, but no approval/decision endpoint or service is implemented; no workflow worker is present | Customer cannot complete account closure and may continue to be charged or treated as active | Approvers and back-office teams have an inbox but no completion path; account status and closure records diverge | Ageing pending-request report and end-to-end closure test; current health checks do not cover business completion | 7 | 7 | 9 | 441 | Implement an authorized, auditable approve/reject transition with separation of duties, close the account atomically with required checks, and alert on SLA-aged pending requests |
| F13 | API becomes unavailable or latency spikes under database/integration failure | External calls are synchronous; no resilience policy is visible, the notification client has no timeout, and there is no connection-pool/back-pressure configuration | Reads, payment creation and support workflows time out or return opaque 5xx errors | Upstream clients retry and amplify load; scheme/ledger calls may be uncertain; on-call lacks a controlled degradation mode | p95/p99 latency, timeout/5xx rates, dependency health and saturation dashboards; only WARN root logging is configured | 8 | 6 | 7 | 336 | Set bounded timeouts for every client, add circuit breakers/bulkheads and retry only idempotent operations, define degraded responses, and instrument traces/metrics with correlation IDs |
| F14 | Operational monitoring reports healthy while the service or a dependency is broken | Security permits `/actuator/health`, but `spring-boot-starter-actuator` is not a dependency; there are no visible metrics, probes, dashboards or structured audit events | Outage and stuck payments are discovered through customer complaints rather than promptly | Dependent services keep sending traffic; on-call response and recovery time increase | Probe the deployed endpoint and verify dependency checks; alert on missing telemetry and stale queue/reconciliation data | 8 | 6 | 9 | 432 | Add Actuator and separate liveness/readiness, dependency health and business probes; export metrics/traces/logs, alert on SLOs, queue age, reconciliation breaks and payment state age |
| F15 | Sensitive internal data is exposed through a future or accidental read path | Staff-only entities (risk score, internal notes/comments) exist, while resource filtering is implemented manually and there is no visible endpoint-level data classification test | Customers could see fraud, hardship or risk information, creating privacy and conduct harm | Regulatory reporting, customer remediation and support process exposure | DLP/access-log review, response-schema contract tests and security penetration tests; no production audit evidence is configured | 9 | 3 | 8 | 216 | Define DTO allow-lists and field classification, query only permitted fields, add customer-versus-staff contract tests, and log/audit privileged access |
| F16 | Malformed or unexpected dependency response becomes an unhandled 5xx | Clients cast unvalidated maps and only translate selected exception types; `ApiExceptionHandler` has no general integration/5xx policy | Customer receives an opaque error and may retry an uncertain payment | Retries can amplify duplicate submissions; support lacks a stable reason and correlation ID | Contract/schema tests, dependency error-rate dashboards and sampled error logs | 7 | 5 | 7 | 245 | Use typed, schema-validated clients, map errors to explicit domain states, include safe correlation IDs, and test malformed, 4xx, 5xx and timeout responses |

## Release priorities

The highest-priority release blockers are F1 (durable storage), F2/F3/F4 (payment state and money integrity), F6
(fraud bypass), F12 (unfinished closure workflow), F8 (ledger/history reconciliation) and F14 (observability). Before
release, demonstrate recovery from scheme and ledger timeouts—including the case where the remote system committed—plus
concurrent-payment, scheduler failover, key-rotation and database-restore tests. The service should not be considered
operationally ready until every payment has a durable audit trail and can be reconciled across accounts-service, scheme
and ledger.

## Evidence reviewed

- `src/main/resources/application.yml`: in-memory H2, integration timeouts, static JWT public key and logging level.
- `payment/PaymentService`, `payment/PaymentDispatcher` and `payment/QueuedPaymentJob`: validation, orchestration and
  scheduled dispatch behavior.
- `integration/scheme`, `integration/ledger`, `integration/fraud` and `integration/notify`: client error handling and
  available-but-unused fraud integration.
- `security/SecurityConfig`, `security/AccessPolicy` and `security/JwtClaimsConverter`: authentication and tenant
  authorization model.
- `closure/ClosureController`: request and pending-list paths, with no decision transition.
- `README.md` and the API tests: stated conventions and tested behavior.
