# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its H2/JPA persistence, authentication and tenant authorization, the
payment dispatcher and scheduled queue, and the scheme, fraud, ledger and notification integrations. It is based on the
current repository implementation and configuration, not on controls that may exist elsewhere in the platform.

Scores use the usual 1–10 FMEA scale:

- **Severity (S):** 1 = negligible inconvenience, 10 = loss of funds, material confidentiality/integrity breach, or
  prolonged service outage.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 = rare or strongly prevented, 10 = expected frequently or whenever the triggering condition
  occurs.
- **Detection (D):** 1 = almost certain immediate detection, 10 = unlikely to be detected before customer or
  downstream impact.
- **RPN = S × O × D.** Detection is scored in the usual FMEA direction: a higher number means poorer detection.

The RPN is a prioritisation aid, not a statement of accepted risk. Any entry with S >= 9 should be treated as a release
gate even if its RPN is moderate. Scores assume production traffic and the external services named in `application.yml`.

## Failure modes

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers and dependants | Detection | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F1 | Payment is sent twice | No client idempotency key or unique business reference; a client/gateway retry can create two `Payment` rows and two scheme submissions. | Customer may be debited twice; scheme, ledger, support and reconciliation teams see duplicate instructions. | Compare payment creation rate with scheme instructions and reconcile by a durable idempotency key; alert on duplicate references. | 10 | 5 | 8 | 400 | Require an idempotency key, enforce a tenant-scoped uniqueness constraint, persist an outbound instruction key, and make scheme/ledger calls idempotent. Add concurrency and retry tests. |
| F2 | Scheme accepts a payment but the API reports an error or loses the record | Scheme timeout/connection failure is thrown without an unknown-outcome state; the transaction can roll back after the scheme has accepted the instruction. | Customer retries or contacts support; an instruction may be duplicated or remain untracked, and operations cannot reliably prove final state. | Trace each request to a scheme reference and reconcile scheme acknowledgements against local payments; alert on unknown outcomes. | 10 | 4 | 8 | 320 | Treat timeouts as `SUBMISSION_UNKNOWN`, use the stable payment reference for status inquiry/reconciliation, and never blindly retry an ambiguous submission. |
| F3 | Ledger and scheme become inconsistent | Scheme submission succeeds, then ledger posting times out or fails. The code records `POSTING_FAILED` but has no retry, compensation, or reconciliation job. | Payment may leave the bank while the customer balance is not debited (or remains operationally unclear); ledger, finance and support teams must manually repair it. | Alert on every `POSTING_FAILED`; daily compare scheme-completed instructions, ledger postings and local status. | 10 | 5 | 7 | 350 | Add an outbox/reconciliation workflow, idempotent ledger references, bounded retries with backoff, and an explicit hold/repair process. Do not expose a “completed” balance effect before ledger confirmation. |
| F4 | Fraud control is bypassed | `FraudScreeningClient`, decisions and `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` exist, but `PaymentDispatcher` never invokes fraud screening. | High-risk or fraudulent payments can be sent; fraud operations and the bank’s financial-crime controls are bypassed. | Monitor fraud-service request volume against payment volume; sample scheme submissions and alert when screening coverage is below 100%. | 10 | 8 | 9 | 720 | Make screening a mandatory step before scheme submission; fail closed on unavailable/`REFER`, persist the decision and reason, and add a release test proving every payment is screened. |
| F5 | Queued payment is not executed on time | Scheduling is process-local; a stopped instance, clock/time-zone issue, deployment at 06:00, or a missed scheduler run leaves `QUEUED` payments behind. | Customer payment misses its execution date; scheme, customer communications and support SLAs are affected. | Gauge due queued payments and age; alert when any due item remains queued beyond a defined threshold. | 8 | 5 | 6 | 240 | Use a durable worker/queue or externally monitored scheduler, make runs resumable, expose queue lag, and provide a controlled replay operation. |
| F6 | Queued payment is dispatched more than once | Multiple service instances can run `QueuedPaymentJob` concurrently; there is no claim/lock or status transition before dispatch. | Duplicate scheme instructions and possible duplicate ledger postings; downstream reconciliation and customers are impacted. | Correlate scheduler runs, local payment IDs, scheme references and ledger references; alert on multiple attempts. | 10 | 4 | 8 | 320 | Atomically claim rows with a lease/version, use one idempotency key per payment, and make dispatch safe to retry. Test multi-instance execution. |
| F7 | Balance or daily limit is bypassed under concurrent requests | Funds and limit are checked before dispatch without a database lock/version check; concurrent requests can observe the same balance and spend allowance. | Overspend, negative/incorrect available balance, and inaccurate customer statements; ledger and fraud teams must investigate. | Reconcile account balance and payment totals; alert on negative balances, limit breaches and optimistic-lock conflicts (currently none). | 10 | 5 | 8 | 400 | Reserve funds atomically in the ledger or under a row lock/optimistic version; calculate limits from authoritative booked/reserved state and add load tests. |
| F8 | Notification is missing or delayed | Notification errors are caught and only logged; there is no durable outbox, retry or delivery status. | Payment may complete without the customer being informed; support receives avoidable disputes and notification SLAs are missed. | Compare completed payments with notification requests/delivery receipts; alert on log count and outbox age. | 6 | 6 | 7 | 252 | Publish notifications through a transactional outbox with retries, deduplication and delivery metrics. Keep notification failure separate from payment completion. |
| F9 | Notification calls exhaust request threads | `HttpNotificationClient` has no connect/read timeout. A slow notification service can block dispatcher/API threads. | Payment requests become slow or unavailable, cascading to customers and dependent channels. | Thread-pool saturation, request latency, timeout and connection-pool metrics; synthetic notification dependency checks. | 8 | 5 | 7 | 280 | Configure strict timeouts and a circuit breaker; move notification delivery off the request transaction and bound connection/thread pools. |
| F10 | Cross-tenant or over-privileged data access | Authorization depends on token claims and distributed checks; a bad token claim, future endpoint, repository query, or access-policy regression can omit tenant/resource filtering. | Confidential customer, account, transaction, risk-score or staff-note data can be exposed or changed; tenant banks and compliance teams are affected. | Automated cross-tenant negative tests on every endpoint; audit access by tenant/user; alert on unusual tenant-to-tenant access. | 10 | 3 | 8 | 240 | Centralise tenant scoping, derive tenant/roles only from validated issuer claims (including issuer/audience and key rotation), add authorization integration tests and security review gates. |
| F11 | Staff-only data leaks through a new or insufficiently filtered endpoint | Internal notes/comments and risk data are stored alongside customer resources; the current security model requires every future controller to implement the filtering correctly. | Customer privacy breach and regulatory exposure; support/admin operations lose trust in the API. | Schema/API contract tests that assert customer responses contain no internal fields; DLP scanning and access-log review. | 9 | 4 | 8 | 288 | Use separate staff/customer DTOs and service methods, deny-by-default endpoint rules, field-level contract tests, and security review for every endpoint. |
| F12 | Authentication or authorization fails open or becomes unavailable | JWT public key rotation/configuration error, identity-service/token outage, or missing issuer/audience validation. | Legitimate customers are locked out, or (if validation is weakened during recovery) unauthorized callers gain access. | Authentication failure rate by reason, key-expiry/rotation alerts, startup configuration checks and synthetic 401/403 tests. | 10 | 3 | 6 | 180 | Validate issuer, audience, expiry and key rotation; fail closed; support overlapping keys and monitor 401/403 rates separately from application errors. |
| F13 | Account/payment data is lost on restart or instance replacement | Production configuration uses H2 in-memory storage (`jdbc:h2:mem`, `create-drop`) and fixture SQL initialization. | Accounts, payments, cases and audit history disappear or revert to fixtures; customers and all downstream teams are severely affected. | Startup data-integrity checks, row-count/sequence anomaly alerts, persistence smoke test across restart, and backup-restore drills. | 10 | 3 | 9 | 270 | Use a managed durable database in production, migrations instead of `create-drop`/fixture initialization, backups, point-in-time recovery and restore testing. |
| F14 | Database outage or connection-pool exhaustion takes down the API | Database failure, slow queries, unbounded list reads, or pool exhaustion. | Account balances, transactions, payments, cases and closure requests are unavailable; dependent channels receive 5xx/timeouts. | Database health, pool utilisation, query latency/errors, API latency/5xx and readiness probes. | 9 | 4 | 4 | 144 | Add production DB health/readiness checks, pool limits/timeouts, query/index review, bounded pagination, and tested failover/runbooks. |
| F15 | External dependency errors are misclassified or become unhandled 500s | HTTP clients catch only selected exception types (for example ledger only catches `ResourceAccessException`); no shared error taxonomy, retry policy or circuit breaker exists. | Customers see unstable failures with no clear status; operations cannot distinguish retryable, rejected and unknown outcomes. | Per-dependency HTTP status/latency/error metrics, distributed tracing, structured error logs and synthetic probes. | 8 | 6 | 6 | 288 | Standardise client error handling, timeouts, retry budgets and circuit breakers; map stable customer-safe errors and preserve correlation IDs. |
| F16 | Closure request workflow stalls or violates four-eyes control | The repository exposes request creation and pending-listing, but no approve/reject endpoint or account-closing transition is implemented; duplicate requests can also race. | Customers cannot close eligible accounts; approvers and operations have an unresolvable backlog, while closure state may not match customer expectations. | Alert on pending-request age/count; end-to-end test from request through approval and account status; reconciliation of approved requests to account status. | 7 | 7 | 8 | 392 | Implement an explicit approve/reject transaction with approver separation, state-transition rules, row locking/unique pending constraint, audit trail and SLA alerts. |
| F17 | Operational incident is not detected or diagnosed quickly | No actuator dependency/metrics endpoint is configured, root logging is WARN, and important failures are only log messages; no correlation IDs or business metrics are visible in the code. | Outages, stuck payments, ledger drift and fraud-screening bypass persist until customers report them; incident response and dependent teams are delayed. | Current detection is mainly logs and manual inspection; verify health, metrics, traces, alert delivery and runbooks in staging. | 9 | 7 | 9 | 567 | Add authenticated operational endpoints, RED/business metrics (payment states, screening coverage, queue lag, reconciliation drift), structured correlated logs, traces, dashboards and paging alerts. |
| F18 | Sensitive payment data is exposed in logs or traces during troubleshooting | External/client exceptions are logged with stack traces and request tracing may capture payloads; no explicit redaction policy is present. | Payee account details or customer data may enter log systems, increasing privacy and incident scope. | Secret/PII scanning of logs and traces, log-retention review, and sampled payload inspection. | 8 | 4 | 8 | 256 | Redact account numbers/payee data, use structured safe error fields, restrict and retain logs minimally, and test observability output for PII. |

## Release priorities

The release should not proceed without addressing or explicitly accepting F4 (fraud bypass), F1/F7 (duplicate and
concurrent-spend controls), F3 (scheme/ledger reconciliation), F17 (ability to detect incidents), and F13 (durable
production persistence). F2 and F6 require an idempotency and unknown-outcome design before increasing traffic. The
remaining actions should be tracked with owners, target dates and measurable alert/runbook acceptance criteria.

## Evidence reviewed

- `README.md` and `src/main/resources/application.yml` for service boundary, security model, dependencies and timeouts.
- `PaymentService`, `PaymentDispatcher`, `QueuedPaymentJob` and integration clients for payment state transitions and
  failure handling.
- `SecurityConfig`, `JwtClaimsConverter`, `CurrentUserProvider` and `AccessPolicy` for authentication and tenant/resource
  authorization.
- Account, case, closure and customer controllers/services, persistence entities/repositories, fixtures and API tests.
