# Accounts service FMEA

Assessment date: 2026-11-04  
Scope: the Spring Boot accounts API, its H2/JPA data store, payment scheduler, security boundary, and the scheme, fraud, ledger, and notification integrations. This is a design and code-review FMEA, not a production incident-frequency analysis.

## Scoring

Each score is on a 1–10 scale. Severity (S) is the impact if the failure reaches a customer or dependent system; occurrence (O) is the expected likelihood with the current design; detection (D) is the likelihood that the failure will escape detection before causing impact (10 = difficult to detect). The risk priority number is `RPN = S × O × D`; higher values should be addressed first.

- Severity: 1 = negligible inconvenience, 5 = material service degradation or manual work, 10 = financial loss, material data/security breach, or regulatory harm.
- Occurrence: 1 = exceptional, 5 = plausible during normal operation, 10 = expected/recurrent without a change.
- Detection: 1 = prevented or detected immediately by an automated control, 5 = detectable through routine review, 10 = no reliable current signal.

## Failure-mode analysis

| FMEA-01 | Persistent account, payment, case, or closure data is lost on restart | `application.yml` uses an in-memory H2 database and `ddl-auto: create-drop`; fixtures are loaded at startup | Balances, payment history, cases, and closure requests revert or disappear; customers can see stale or missing information | Ledger/scheme records can no longer be reconciled to the service; support and operations lose their work and audit trail | Startup logs may show schema recreation, but there is no durable-store or data-loss alert; API tests use the same ephemeral setup | 10 | 8 | 9 | **720** | Use a production durable database with migrations and backups; make `create-drop` and fixture loading test-only; add restore/backup drills, startup safety checks, and reconciliation against the ledger. |
| FMEA-02 | Payment is accepted by the scheme but the API reports an error or loses the payment record | A scheme timeout or unhandled gateway error occurs after the remote side accepts the request; the local transaction rolls back, and there is no durable outbox/idempotency recovery | Customer may retry and create a duplicate payment, or cannot establish whether money was sent | Scheme and service states diverge; operations must investigate manually and may need a payment recall | Scheme timeout becomes an exception; only application logs and customer retry symptoms expose the ambiguous result | 10 | 5 | 9 | **450** | Persist the instruction before dispatch; use a stable idempotency key and scheme status inquiry; implement an outbox/worker with retry and reconciliation, and return an explicit pending state for unknown outcomes. |
| FMEA-03 | Scheme submission succeeds but ledger posting fails; account and payment state become misleading | `PaymentDispatcher` submits first, debits the local account, then catches only `LedgerTimeoutException`; it records `POSTING_FAILED` but does not retry or reverse; notification is still sent | Customer may be told the payment was sent while the ledger is not booked; balance and statement can be wrong | Core ledger lacks a posting; downstream reporting/reconciliation and finance teams see an unbalanced transaction; the payment is not picked up by the queued job | `POSTING_FAILED` and a WARN log exist, and a test covers a timeout, but no alert, retry queue, or reconciliation job is present | 10 | 6 | 8 | **480** | Make ledger posting idempotent using `ledgerReference`; add durable retry/dead-letter handling and reconciliation; do not send `PAYMENT_SENT` until ledger confirmation, and define compensation for a scheme-submitted/ledger-failed instruction. |
| FMEA-04 | Fraud screening is bypassed entirely | `FraudScreeningClient` and `Decision` exist, but `PaymentService`/`PaymentDispatcher` never calls them; `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` is unused | Fraudulent payments can be sent without screening, increasing loss and customer harm | Fraud team receives no screening event or review work; compliance evidence is incomplete | Unit/API tests provide a fake fraud service, but no production-path assertion or metric proves screening occurred | 10 | 7 | 10 | **700** | Put screening in the payment state machine before scheme submission; fail closed on unavailable screening, persist `ACCEPT/REFER/REJECT`, route `REFER` to review, and add a test/metric asserting one decision per payment. |
| FMEA-05 | A queued payment is not dispatched on its execution date | Scheduler is not enabled in `AccountsApplication`; the job has no retry/claim/lease, and one uncaught failure can stop the batch or leave a row queued | Payment is delayed beyond the promised working day with no useful customer explanation | Scheme receives late instructions; support and operations must find and replay stuck payments | There is a scheduled method and a unit/API-style test invokes it directly, but no scheduler health, queue-age metric, or alert | 8 | 5 | 9 | **360** | Explicitly enable and monitor scheduling; use a transactional outbox/queue with leases, retries, dead-letter status, and idempotent dispatch; alert on due-payment age and job failure. |
| FMEA-06 | Concurrent payment requests bypass balance or daily-limit checks | Checks and account debit are not protected by a lock/version constraint; two transactions can read the same balance and paid-today total | Customer can exceed available funds or the daily limit; later payments may fail or create an overdraft | Ledger and account-service balances diverge; fraud, finance, and support teams handle avoidable disputes | No `@Version`, locking query, database constraint, or concurrency test; ordinary conflict responses do not reveal this race | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Enforce atomic reservation/debit and daily-limit accounting in the database (optimistic/pessimistic locking or serialized account commands); add high-concurrency tests and reconciliation alerts. |
| FMEA-07 | Duplicate payment is dispatched | Client retry, worker retry, or operator replay occurs after an unknown response; scheme idempotency is not explicitly configured and `P` + local generated ID is not durable across a rolled-back transaction | Customer may pay twice and needs a refund/recall | Scheme and ledger can contain duplicate instructions; operations and finance investigate a payment dispute | Payment IDs and scheme IDs are stored after success, but no uniqueness/idempotency constraint or duplicate detector exists | 10 | 5 | 8 | **400** | Require an idempotency key on create, enforce uniqueness per customer/account, propagate it to scheme and ledger, and make every worker retry safe. |
| FMEA-08 | Notification failure or slowness delays payment requests and loses customer communication | Notification client has no configured connect/read timeout; it is called synchronously; failures are only logged and not queued | API response can hang/fail after the money movement, or customer does not receive confirmation | Notification service/backlog and API thread pool are coupled; support sees “sent” records without a corresponding message | WARN log in `notifySent`; no delivery ID, queue, metric, timeout, or alert | 7 | 6 | 8 | **336** | Publish notifications asynchronously from an outbox after the payment state commits; add bounded timeouts, delivery status, retry/dead-letter handling, and alerts. |
| FMEA-09 | External integration errors become generic 5xx responses or unhandled worker failures | Scheme client maps only HTTP 4xx and resource-access timeouts; 5xx/invalid response cases escape; exception handler does not cover integration failures | Customer receives an opaque error and may retry; queued work may remain or be partially processed | On-call sees noisy framework errors rather than actionable dependency state; batch progress is uncertain | HTTP client timeouts exist for scheme/fraud/ledger, but no integration-specific metrics or error mapping | 6 | 6 | 7 | **252** | Handle 5xx, malformed responses, and all timeout classes explicitly; map to stable 502/503 or a persisted pending state; add retry policy with backoff and dependency-specific alerts. |
| FMEA-10 | Cross-tenant or over-privileged access exposes customer/account data | Tenant and roles are trusted from JWT claims; a token with incorrect/stale claims, or future code that skips `AccessPolicy`, can cross the boundary. | FMEA-11 | JWT acceptance or authorization configuration is unavailable or misconfigured | Local public-key-only validation depends on the correct key being deployed; no issuer/audience configuration is shown, and claim parsing assumes expected claim shapes | Legitimate users are locked out, or incorrectly shaped/over-privileged tokens cause inconsistent authorization | All API consumers fail or security incident response is required; identity-service and platform teams are paged | Spring Security returns 401/403, but there is no key-rotation test, authentication metric, or configuration startup validation | 9 | 4 | 7 | **252** | Configure issuer/audience and rotation-safe key retrieval or a managed trust store; validate required claims at authentication; monitor 401/403 rates and test rotation, expiry, malformed roles, and missing tenant. |
| FMEA-12 | Account/payment reads or writes fail because the database is unavailable or slow | All business operations use JPA synchronously; no pool, query, transaction, or circuit-breaker settings are shown | API timeouts or 5xx responses prevent balance checks, payments, support cases, and closure requests | Upstream channels retry and amplify load; support loses visibility; operations lack a clear dependency signal | Spring/application logs are the only apparent signal; no health endpoint dependency is configured (the permit rule exists but Actuator is not a dependency) | 8 | 6 | 7 | **336** | Run a supported HA database with connection-pool limits, migrations, query/index review, and backup/restore; add readiness/liveness and DB health probes, latency/error metrics, and load tests. |
| FMEA-13 | Internal sensitive data is returned or logged outside its intended audience | Staff-only fields exist in the model; broad entity access and exception/logging paths can accidentally expose them; payment payloads contain account details | Customers or logs may reveal risk scores, internal notes, or full account/payment identifiers | Privacy incident, increased social-engineering risk, and compliance investigation | Controllers intentionally omit some staff fields and use resource access checks; no centralized response allow-list, redaction, or log review control is evident | 9 | 4 | 8 | **288** | Use explicit DTOs for every audience, redact account numbers and payment payloads in logs, add structured audit logging, secret/PII scanning, and contract tests asserting forbidden fields never appear. |
| FMEA-14 | Closure request is duplicated or remains pending indefinitely | “Check then insert” is not protected by a unique pending constraint; there is no implementation shown for approval/decision or SLA escalation | Customer receives conflicting status or cannot close the account | Operations cannot complete lifecycle work; downstream account status and customer records may remain open | API returns 409 when an existing pending row is found, but concurrent requests can race; pending list is available only to approvers | 7 | 5 | 7 | **245** | Add a database uniqueness constraint for one pending request per account, implement an auditable approval transition, and alert/escalate requests past SLA. |
| FMEA-15 | API is unavailable or behaves differently after deployment | No production profile, migration strategy, readiness gate, or actuator dependency is shown; startup initializes fixture SQL and may recreate schema | All customers and staff lose access, or receive inconsistent behavior during rollout | Upstream services see failures; release/on-call teams have no safe rollback or traffic-drain signal | Maven tests cover API behavior with fakes; no deployment smoke, compatibility, or readiness evidence is in the repository | 8 | 4 | 7 | **224** | Add environment-specific configuration, Flyway/Liquibase migrations, health/readiness endpoints, backward-compatible rollout checks, smoke tests, dashboards, and a documented rollback/runbook. |

## Highest-priority actions

The largest current RPNs are not independent: durable storage and payment state integrity underpin reconciliation, duplicate prevention, and scheduled recovery. Before release, the recommended order is:

1. Replace the in-memory/create-drop data setup with a durable production database, migrations, backups, and restore testing (FMEA-01).
2. Put fraud screening and payment dispatch behind a persisted, idempotent state machine/outbox, with scheme and ledger reconciliation (FMEA-02–04 and FMEA-07).
3. Make balance/limit enforcement atomic and make the queued worker observable, retryable, and safe to replay (FMEA-05–06).
4. Decouple notifications and add timeouts, metrics, health/readiness, and dependency alerts (FMEA-08, FMEA-09, FMEA-12, FMEA-15).
5. Strengthen tenant/token controls and privacy regression tests before exposing the next release to multiple banks (FMEA-10, FMEA-11, FMEA-13).

The scores should be recalculated after these controls are implemented and after production incident/volume data is available; the current RPNs are comparative prioritization signals rather than probabilities.
