# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API and its in-process scheduled payment worker, based on the
implementation and configuration in this repository. It considers customer, support, bank-operations, ledger,
payment-scheme, fraud, notification, and identity-service dependencies. It is a design-time FMEA rather than a
production incident history; occurrence scores therefore reflect the likelihood of the condition in a normal release,
not a measured rate.

Scores use the conventional 1–10 scale:

- **Severity (S):** 1 = negligible inconvenience; 10 = unauthorised movement or loss of funds, material data breach,
  or prolonged inability to provide the service.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 = exceptional; 10 = expected frequently or on the normal path.
- **Detection (D):** 1 = almost certain to be detected immediately by an existing control; 10 = unlikely to be found
  before customer or dependent-system impact.
- **RPN:** `S × O × D`. Higher RPN means the item should be prioritised, but any severity-10 item should be treated as
  a release blocker even if its occurrence is low.

The code has no production metrics, distributed tracing, durable outbox/reconciliation process, or documented alert
rules. Where detection below says “add”, that is a recommended control rather than a capability currently present.

## Failure modes

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers | Effect on dependent systems and teams | Detection path | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F1 | A payment is accepted by the scheme but the API records no usable result. | `PaymentDispatcher` calls the scheme before the ledger and does not catch `SchemeTimeoutException`. A timeout may occur after the scheme accepted the instruction; the surrounding transaction can roll back the local payment row. | The customer sees an error or may retry, while the payee can receive the payment. A retry can create a duplicate or be rejected later as `DUPL`. | Scheme and local records diverge; operations must investigate an instruction with no local status. | Reconcile scheme instructions by the deterministic `P{id}` reference against local payments; alert on scheme instructions without a terminal local record. | 10 | 3 | 8 | **240** | Persist an outbox/payment attempt before the external call; use an idempotency key and a durable `SUBMITTED/UNKNOWN` state. Reconcile and retry only with the same scheme reference; return an explicit pending outcome for ambiguous timeouts. |
| F2 | A payment is sent to the scheme but is not correctly posted to the ledger. | The account is debited locally after scheme acceptance. A ledger timeout is converted to `POSTING_FAILED`, but there is no retry/reconciliation worker. Non-timeout HTTP failures are not caught and can roll back local state after the scheme side effect. | The payment may leave the account without a matching ledger entry, or the customer balance/status may disagree with the actual transfer. | Core ledger, scheme, finance, and operations records can diverge; manual repair is required. | Compare scheme receipts, `POSTING_FAILED` rows, local account balances, and ledger postings using `ledgerReference`; alert on nonzero mismatch and aged failures. | 10 | 4 | 8 | **320** | Make ledger posting idempotent on a stable reference, separate local balance from authoritative ledger state, retry with backoff, and add a reconciliation queue/runbook. Catch and classify all retryable HTTP failures. |
| F3 | Fraud screening is bypassed for every payment. | `FraudScreeningClient`, `Decision`, and `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` exist, but `PaymentService`/`PaymentDispatcher` never calls the fraud client. | Suspicious or fraudulent payments can be submitted without screening or review. | Fraud operations lose a control point; scheme and bank risk exposure increases and downstream fraud reporting is incomplete. | Compare payment volume with fraud-screening request volume; a zero screening-to-payment ratio is an immediate alert. Add test coverage that requires screening before scheme submission. | 10 | 10 | 10 | **1000** | Block scheme submission unless screening returns `ACCEPT`; persist `REFER` as `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` and `REJECT` as rejected. Define fail-closed behavior and a bounded timeout/queue for fraud-service outages. |
| F4 | Queued payments remain queued past their execution date. | `QueuedPaymentJob` has `@Scheduled`, but `@EnableScheduling` is not present and no other scheduling configuration is visible. The test invokes the job directly, so API tests do not prove the production trigger works. | Customers miss the promised next-working-day execution and may submit duplicate payments. | Scheme volume and ledger cash-flow timing are wrong; support and operations receive avoidable payment queries. | Alert on `QUEUED` payments with `executionDate < today`; expose job-run timestamp, duration, count, and failure metrics. Add a production-context test that verifies scheduling is enabled. | 8 | 8 | 9 | **576** | Enable and verify scheduling, or run the worker from a durable job platform. Make claiming/dispatching safe across multiple instances, and page on stale queued rows. |
| F5 | Concurrent requests bypass balance or daily-limit checks. | `paidToday()` and the account balance check are read before dispatch; there is no pessimistic/optimistic locking or atomic reservation. Two requests can both pass and debit the same account. | The customer can exceed the daily limit or spend more than the available balance; the local balance can become negative or differ from the ledger. | Ledger/scheme rejection and local acceptance can diverge; finance and support need corrections. | Monitor negative balances, daily-limit breaches, duplicate near-simultaneous payments, and rejected ledger postings; load-test concurrent payment creation. | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Lock the account or maintain an atomic payment reservation/available balance, enforce limits in the database transaction, and add concurrency tests. Use idempotency for client retries. |
| F6 | A notification failure delays or prevents the payment response. | `HttpNotificationClient` has no connect/read timeout. `notifySent` catches runtime failures, but a hung call can hold the request and database transaction until infrastructure timeouts. | Payment may complete but the customer receives no timely confirmation and may retry; a slow API degrades the whole customer experience. | Thread/connection pools can exhaust; support and notification teams lack a reliable delivery status. | Track request latency and notification latency/error rate separately; alert on p95/p99 latency and circuit-breaker opens. | 6 | 6 | 7 | **252** | Configure short timeouts, circuit breaking, and bounded retries. Publish notifications through an outbox/queue after the payment commits; make delivery observable and idempotent. |
| F7 | External integration responses cause an unclassified 5xx or incorrect state. | Scheme parsing assumes a non-null body and `instructionId`; fraud parsing assumes a valid `decision`; ledger only translates `ResourceAccessException`, not HTTP 4xx/5xx. | Customers get a generic server error, possibly after a payment-side effect, with no actionable status. | Dependent teams cannot distinguish rejection, outage, contract drift, or ambiguous acceptance; retry behavior becomes unsafe. | Contract tests against each integration; count and sample 5xx responses by dependency; alert on schema/parse exceptions and unknown status transitions. | 8 | 4 | 8 | **256** | Validate response schemas, map status classes explicitly, classify retryable versus terminal errors, and preserve an auditable attempt/status record before calling out. |
| F8 | A production restart loses the accounts database. | `spring.datasource.url` is `jdbc:h2:mem:accounts` and Hibernate is configured with `create-drop`; fixture SQL is loaded on startup. No persistent production profile or migration path is present. | Customer balances, payments, cases, notes, and closure requests disappear or revert to fixtures after restart. | Ledger and scheme retain external history while the API loses its reconciliation basis; severe incident response and data-restoration work follows. | Startup logs/config review; compare database record counts and payment IDs before and after restart; backup-restore and failover drills. | 10 | 3 | 10 | **300** | Make persistence an explicit environment-required dependency (production database with durable storage, backups, migrations, and restore tests). Fail startup if the production profile still selects in-memory H2 or fixture loading. |
| F9 | A valid token is accepted with the wrong issuer/audience or becomes unusable during key rotation. | JWT configuration uses only a classpath public key; no issuer/audience validation is configured, and rotation requires replacing the bundled key and redeploying. | Depending on identity configuration, callers may be rejected unexpectedly or a token from an unintended issuer could be trusted if it is signed by the accepted key. | Identity, tenant, and security operations face an emergency deployment or potentially cross-system trust incident. | Identity-provider canary tests for issuer, audience, expiry, and rotated keys; alert on 401 spikes and authentication failures by issuer/key ID. | 10 | 2 | 8 | **160** | Validate issuer, audience, expiry, and required claims; use the identity provider's JWKS with controlled rotation and cache behavior. Add negative security tests for missing/incorrect tenant, roles, and scopes. |
| F10 | A tenant or resource access rule regresses and exposes another tenant's data. | Tenant checks are distributed across controllers/services/repositories; some list queries load IDs then filter in memory. A future endpoint or query can omit one check, and there is no central database tenant guard. | Customer or support staff may see balances, personal data, cases, notes, or payments belonging to another bank. | Material confidentiality breach, regulatory reporting, tenant trust loss, and incident containment across support and security teams. | Automated cross-tenant API tests for every endpoint; audit access logs with user/tenant/resource IDs; alert on cross-tenant query/access anomalies. | 10 | 3 | 8 | **240** | Enforce tenant scoping in repository queries and service boundaries, centralize authorization policy, add mutation and negative tests for every role/scope combination, and perform an independent authorization review before release. |
| F11 | Closure requests accumulate without an account ever being closed. | The repository/model contain request states and the API lists pending requests, but there is no approval/rejection endpoint and no code that changes `Account.status` from `OPEN` to `CLOSED`. | Customers cannot complete closure; an account they believe is closed can continue to accept payments. | Approvers and operations have no completion workflow, and regulatory/customer-service SLAs can be missed. | Dashboard pending age/count by tenant; alert on aged pending requests and on payments to accounts with approved closure requests. | 7 | 7 | 9 | **441** | Implement the second-person decision workflow with tenant/role checks, atomic account-state transition, pending-request uniqueness, audit fields, and tests for payment/closure races. |
| F12 | The service becomes unavailable or data access degrades under load. | H2 in-memory storage is single-process; list endpoints return unpaged accounts, transactions, cases, and pending closures; no pool, query, cache, rate limit, or capacity configuration is visible. | Slow or failed account, transaction, and support-case requests; payment requests may time out and be retried. | Upstream channel/API callers see elevated latency and retry storms; operations lack capacity headroom. | HTTP saturation/error/latency metrics, JVM/DB pool metrics, slow-query logs, and load tests with production-sized tenants. | 8 | 5 | 7 | **280** | Move to a supported persistent database, add indexes and pagination, define pool/time budgets and rate limits, and load-test list and payment paths with failure injection. |
| F13 | Business-day execution occurs on a bank holiday or at an incorrect boundary. | `WorkingDays` excludes only Saturday/Sunday; scheme cutoff is a fixed local time and there is no holiday calendar or explicit clock/time-zone configuration for all timestamps. | A payment can be sent on a non-settlement day or delayed unexpectedly; the displayed execution promise is wrong. | Scheme rejects or reschedules instructions; operations and customer support must handle avoidable exceptions. | Compare execution dates with the scheme calendar; alert on scheme rejects due to date/cutoff and test DST, cutoff boundary, and holiday cases. | 6 | 6 | 7 | **252** | Use a versioned bank-holiday/calendar service, make timezone and cutoff rules explicit, inject the clock everywhere, and contract-test boundary/DST/holiday behavior. |
| F14 | Important failures are not detected or diagnosed quickly. | Health is permitted at `/actuator/health`, but no actuator dependency/configuration is present. Logging is root `WARN`; there are no metrics, traces, correlation IDs, audit events, or alerts in the repository. | Outages, stuck payments, missing notifications, and security anomalies last longer and customers receive inconsistent answers. | On-call, fraud, finance, and support teams lack evidence to reconcile or triage incidents. | Synthetic checks and log review may find only a subset; add explicit alerts for the conditions in this table. | 9 | 7 | 9 | **567** | Add real liveness/readiness and dependency health, Micrometer metrics, trace/correlation IDs, structured audit logs, dashboards, SLOs, and alerts for payment state age/mismatch, auth failures, and integration latency/error rates. |
| F15 | A malformed or unexpected request creates incorrect business data or a generic 500. | Validation covers payment field shape, but there is no visible global handler for all integration/runtime failures; case/closure timestamps use `Instant.now()` directly and persistence relies on implicit constraints. | Customers receive confusing errors, or repeated retries create duplicate cases/requests where the endpoint has no idempotency key. | Upstream clients cannot safely retry; support sees duplicate work and inconsistent timestamps. | Count 4xx/5xx by endpoint, validate database constraints, add property/fuzz tests and retry/idempotency tests. | 6 | 5 | 7 | **210** | Define a stable error contract, handle expected integration exceptions centrally, add idempotency keys for create operations, enforce database uniqueness/foreign keys, and use the configured clock consistently. |

## Priority actions before release

1. **Block release:** integrate fraud screening fail-closed (F3); make scheme/ledger payment transitions durable,
   idempotent, and reconcilable (F1/F2); move off in-memory `create-drop` storage (F8); and verify tenant authorization
   with negative tests (F10).
2. **Fix the payment path:** enable and monitor the queued-payment worker (F4), add account locking/reservations and
   client idempotency (F5), and bound notification calls (F6).
3. **Close the operational gap:** implement the closure decision flow (F11), add dependency-aware health/metrics,
   reconciliation dashboards and alerts (F14), and run failure-injection tests for scheme, fraud, ledger, notification,
   database restart, key rotation, DST, and bank holidays.

## Evidence reviewed

- `README.md` and `src/main/resources/application.yml` for the service contract, security model, integration timeouts,
  and data-store configuration.
- `PaymentService`, `PaymentDispatcher`, `QueuedPaymentJob`, `PaymentStatus`, and integration clients for payment
  sequencing, retries, timeout handling, and scheduling.
- `SecurityConfig`, `JwtClaimsConverter`, `CurrentUserProvider`, `AccessPolicy`, and all controllers for authentication,
  authorization, tenant isolation, and endpoint behavior.
- API tests and integration fakes for the tested happy paths and the explicitly modeled scheme/ledger timeout cases.
