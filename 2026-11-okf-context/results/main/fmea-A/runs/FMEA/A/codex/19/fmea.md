# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This FMEA covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its H2/JPA persistence layer, JWT
authorization, payment workflow, scheduled payment worker, closure workflow, and
the scheme, fraud, ledger, and notification integrations described in the
repository. It is based on the implementation and configuration in this
repository, not on production incident rates or infrastructure controls that
are not represented here.

Scores use the conventional 1–10 scale:

- **Severity (S):** 1 = negligible customer impact; 10 = severe financial,
  regulatory, confidentiality, or prolonged availability impact.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 = rare; 10 = frequent or highly likely without a
  control.
- **Detection (D):** 1 = almost certain to detect before impact; 10 = unlikely
  to detect promptly. The score is intentionally higher where the code has no
  metrics, alert, reconciliation, or explicit error handling.
- **RPN:** `S × O × D`. Treat high-severity rows as release blockers even when
  the RPN is moderate. As a working prioritization guide, RPN ≥ 200 is high,
  100–199 is medium, and <100 is lower priority.

## Failure modes

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers and dependent systems/teams | Detection | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F1 | Cross-tenant data or operation is authorized | A forged/misconfigured identity token has an incorrect `tenant` or role claim, or a future endpoint bypasses `AccessPolicy`; payment visibility also relies on account visibility rather than an explicit tenant predicate on the payment query | Confidentiality breach between banks; unauthorized account access or payment activity; regulatory incident; identity, security, compliance, and support teams must investigate and notify affected tenants | Cross-tenant authorization integration tests, security review, audit logs, and alerts on tenant-mismatch decisions; currently there is no visible audit/metric trail | 10 | 3 | 8 | 240 | Make issuer/audience/claim validation explicit and fail closed; centralize tenant-scoped repository queries; add negative tests for every endpoint and authorization decision; emit immutable audit events for access and payment actions |
| F2 | Staff-only information is exposed to a customer | A new DTO/controller returns internal account notes, case comments, or risk data without filtering `internal`; inconsistent resource-level checks; accidental logging of sensitive payloads | Exposure of fraud, hardship, dispute, or risk information; customer harm and breach response for support, security, and compliance teams | API contract tests using customer tokens; schema/data-loss-prevention scanning; review logs and DTOs for sensitive fields | 9 | 3 | 8 | 216 | Maintain separate customer/staff DTOs and repository methods; add tests asserting internal fields never appear; prohibit sensitive request/response logging and add redaction checks |
| F3 | Valid requests fail or all API traffic becomes unavailable | Service cannot start because configuration, signing key, database schema/initialization, or an integration property is invalid; database connection exhaustion or JVM/resource exhaustion; only a public health URL is configured and no management dependency is shown | Customers cannot view accounts, raise cases, pay, or submit closures; channels and support operations degrade; release/operations teams receive an outage | Startup/readiness probe, synthetic authenticated journeys, dependency and pool metrics, error rate/latency alerts; current code shows no readiness or resource metrics | 9 | 4 | 7 | 252 | Add production-grade external durable DB and migrations, readiness checks for required dependencies, liveness separately, connection/JVM/thread-pool metrics, and deployment smoke tests; do not use `ddl-auto=create-drop` outside tests |
| F4 | Account, transaction, or payment data is lost or reset | The configured datasource is in-memory H2 with `DB_CLOSE_DELAY=-1`; `ddl-auto=create-drop` recreates schema on restart | Balances, payment history, cases, closure requests, notes, and audit-relevant data disappear on restart; reconciliation, customer support, finance, and compliance teams lose records | Restart/restore test, row-count and checksum monitoring, database durability alerts, reconciliation against ledger; no durable persistence is represented in the configuration | 10 | 4 | 9 | 360 | Use a durable production database with versioned migrations, backups/PITR and tested restore; explicitly prevent the in-memory/create-drop profile from being deployed; reconcile local balances/payments with the ledger |
| F5 | Payment is accepted by the scheme but is missing locally or submitted twice | Scheme request times out after the gateway accepts it; the transaction rolls back local state, and a client retry creates a new payment/reference. There is no idempotency key or timeout reconciliation path | Duplicate transfer or an orphaned scheme instruction; customer may see an error and retry while money moves; scheme, operations, finance, and complaints teams must reconcile | Scheme reference reconciliation, duplicate-reference alerts, timeout/error dashboards, and customer-visible payment status; current code has timeout exceptions but no recovery workflow | 10 | 4 | 9 | 360 | Require an idempotency key and persist it before dispatch; use a durable outbox/state machine; query scheme status after timeout; make retries reference-idempotent and add reconciliation/alerting for accepted-but-unknown instructions |
| F6 | Ledger and local account balance diverge after a payment | The account is debited in the service before ledger posting; a ledger timeout is caught as `POSTING_FAILED`, but the ledger may have booked the posting after the timeout. There is no retry/reconciliation action in `PaymentDispatcher` | Customer balance can be wrong, or the customer may be charged twice if operations retry; finance and ledger teams cannot trust payment status without manual investigation | Compare `ledgerReference`, local debit, and ledger postings; alert on `POSTING_FAILED`, balance deltas, and aged unresolved payments; current logging is a single warning | 10 | 4 | 8 | 320 | Use ledger idempotency with a stable reference, query-before-retry, and an outbox/reconciliation worker; make status transitions explicit (`UNKNOWN`/`RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED`); never allow an operator retry to create a second debit |
| F7 | Concurrent payments bypass balance or daily limit | Two requests read the same balance and result set before either commits; `PaymentService` has no visible pessimistic/optimistic lock or atomic limit reservation | Overspend, daily-limit breach, negative local balance, scheme rejection, or inconsistent ledger postings; fraud, finance, and customer support receive avoidable disputes | Concurrent load tests, database constraint/lock telemetry, negative-balance and limit-breach alerts, daily reconciliation; ordinary API tests will not expose the race | 9 | 5 | 8 | 360 | Lock the account or atomically reserve available balance/limit in the database; add unique/idempotency constraints and concurrent tests; base limit calculation on a transactional reservation rather than a read-then-write scan |
| F8 | Fraud controls are bypassed or unavailable without fail-closed behavior | `FraudScreeningClient`, decisions, and properties exist, but `PaymentService`/`PaymentDispatcher` never calls the screening client; therefore no payment is screened and no `REFER`/`REJECT` path is wired | Fraudulent payments can be sent; fraud losses and regulatory exposure fall on the bank; fraud operations cannot rely on the accounts service | Search/code review currently reveals the missing call; production monitoring cannot detect a check that never occurs; add a screened-payment ratio and decision metrics | 10 | 6 | 10 | 600 | Integrate screening before scheme submission; persist decision and model/reference; fail closed on unavailable/invalid responses, route `REFER` to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, reject appropriately, and alert when screening coverage is below 100% |
| F9 | Queued payments are not executed, or are executed more than once | `@Scheduled` execution depends on one application instance and scheduler health; the job has no claim/lease or idempotent completion protocol; a crash can occur after scheme acceptance and before local save | Payments miss their execution date or are duplicated; customers miss bills; scheme, operations, and support teams handle aged queue and duplicate-payment cases | Gauge queued payments by due date, scheduler heartbeat, job duration/failure alerts, and scheme/local reconciliation; no scheduler health metric is shown | 8 | 5 | 8 | 320 | Move dispatch to a durable queue/outbox; use row claiming/locking and idempotent scheme/ledger references; expose due/aged queue metrics, last-success heartbeat, retry policy, and a dead-letter/reconciliation process |
| F10 | Scheme or other integration outage causes request storms and long latency | HTTP clients use synchronous calls and fixed timeouts (scheme 10s, fraud 2s, ledger 5s); no circuit breaker, bounded retry, bulkhead, or back-pressure is visible | Payment calls tie up threads and fail unpredictably; downstream outage spreads to the API; customers retry and increase load; platform and on-call teams face cascading failure | Per-dependency latency, timeout, saturation, and error-rate metrics; distributed traces and circuit state; current implementation only logs ledger/notification failures | 8 | 6 | 7 | 336 | Add bounded, jittered retries only for idempotent operations, circuit breakers, bulkheads, rate limits, and explicit customer-safe statuses; define dependency SLOs and alerts |
| F11 | Customer is told a payment failed or remains uninformed even though it completed | Notification call occurs after scheme/ledger processing and failures are swallowed with a warning; no durable notification event or retry exists | Payment may complete without confirmation, causing duplicate customer submissions and contact-centre load; notifications and support teams cannot prove delivery | Compare completed payments with notification delivery receipts; alert on notification failures and age; current code has only a warning log | 6 | 6 | 8 | 288 | Publish notification events through an outbox with retries and deduplication; expose delivery status separately from payment status; include a support reconciliation view |
| F12 | Payment, case, or closure request remains in an intermediate state | Unexpected runtime/database error occurs after an external side effect or before the local state transition; generic exceptions are not mapped to a stable operational status, and there is no recovery worker | Customers see 5xx or stale `RECEIVED`/`QUEUED`/`POSTING_FAILED` records; support and operations must manually determine whether money moved | Alert on 5xx and aged non-terminal statuses; trace requests with payment IDs; periodic state/reconciliation reports; current error handler covers only selected domain/validation exceptions | 8 | 5 | 8 | 320 | Define a persisted payment state machine, transactional outbox, retry/dead-letter handling, recovery commands, and an explicit `UNKNOWN` state; add global safe error mapping with correlation IDs |
| F13 | Payment or closure operation is submitted twice by a client | POST endpoints have no idempotency-key contract; network retry after a response timeout creates another payment/request. Closure has only a pending-state check, which is not a general request idempotency mechanism | Duplicate payments or confusing duplicate customer requests; customer, support, and finance teams handle reversals and complaints | Duplicate same-account/payee/amount heuristics, scheme duplicate responses, and idempotency-key dashboards; current API has no request key | 9 | 6 | 8 | 432 | Add required/optional idempotency keys with a uniqueness constraint and replayed response; define duplicate semantics for closure requests; test retries at the HTTP and worker boundaries |
| F14 | Closure request is created but never decided or account closure is not enforced | The visible controller creates and lists requests, but no approval/decision or account-status transition implementation is present in the inspected code; pending rows can age indefinitely | Customers cannot close accounts; closed-account policy may not be applied; approvers, operations, complaints, and regulatory-retention teams lose workflow control | Metric and alert for pending requests by age, approval-to-account-status reconciliation, and end-to-end closure synthetic test; current API tests can only verify creation/listing | 7 | 5 | 9 | 315 | Implement an authorized approve/reject transition with tenant and role checks, atomic account update, outstanding-payment/funds validation, audit event, and SLA alert for aged pending requests |
| F15 | Authorization is unintentionally weakened by token-claim handling | JWT converter maps roles/scopes but the code relies on token claims for identity and tenant; missing/incorrect claim validation or an issuer key/configuration mismatch can reject legitimate users or grant incorrect authorities | Either broad access breach or tenant-wide denial of service; identity platform, security, and customer-support teams are affected | Token validation tests for missing tenant, roles, scopes, expiry, issuer, and audience; security logs and 401/403 anomaly alerts; current tests use locally signed tokens | 10 | 3 | 8 | 240 | Validate issuer, audience, expiry, not-before, and required `sub`/tenant claims; use a managed key rotation/JWK configuration; monitor authorization-code spikes and perform contract tests with the identity service |
| F16 | Sensitive personal/financial data is leaked through operational telemetry | Payment and customer objects contain account numbers, addresses, email, and risk data; exception/request logging policy is not defined, while integration failures may include payload/context | Breach through logs/traces, wider internal access, retention and compliance exposure; security and privacy teams must contain and investigate | Secret/PII scanning of logs, access audit, retention reports, and red-team tests; current root logging is only set to WARN, not redaction | 9 | 3 | 8 | 216 | Define structured logging with field allowlists and redaction/tokenization; never log full account numbers or JWTs; restrict and audit log access and test representative exception paths |

## Release priorities

Before release, the highest-value controls are:

1. Wire fraud screening into the payment state machine and fail closed (F8).
2. Make scheme and ledger operations idempotent and recoverable, with an outbox,
   reconciliation, and explicit unknown states (F5, F6, F12).
3. Prevent concurrent limit/balance bypass and duplicate HTTP submissions (F7,
   F13).
4. Replace the in-memory/create-drop persistence configuration with durable,
   migrated, backed-up storage (F4).
5. Implement and monitor the closure approval transition and aged-work queues
   (F9, F14).
6. Add tenant/security regression tests, authenticated synthetic checks, and
   metrics/alerts for all non-terminal payment states and dependency failures
   (F1, F2, F3, F10, F15, F16).

The RPNs should be recalculated after production incident data, dependency SLOs,
deployment topology, and existing platform controls are confirmed.
