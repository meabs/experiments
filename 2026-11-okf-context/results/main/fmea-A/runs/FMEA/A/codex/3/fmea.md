# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its H2/JPA persistence model, bearer-token authorization, payment
workflow, scheduled queue worker, and the scheme, fraud, ledger, and notification integrations described in `README.md`
and `application.yml`. It is an operational release review, so it includes latent design failures that can occur even
when the current happy-path tests pass.

Scores are on a 1–10 scale. Severity (S) is the impact if the failure reaches a customer or dependent team/system;
occurrence (O) is the expected likelihood under normal production conditions; detection (D) is how difficult the
failure is to detect before material impact (10 = difficult to detect). The risk priority number is `RPN = S × O × D`.
Scores are relative within this service, not probabilities or regulatory classifications.

## Failure-mode analysis

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers and dependent systems/teams | Detection / control | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F1 | Payment is sent to the scheme but is recorded as failed or absent locally | Scheme accepts `P{id}`, then the request times out, the process dies, or the database transaction rolls back. There is no durable outbox, status callback, or reconciliation workflow. | Customer may see a 5xx or no payment record while the payee receives money; retries can send a duplicate. Ledger, customer support, finance, and scheme-operations teams cannot establish the authoritative state. | Compare scheme instructions with local payments by idempotency key; alert on scheme submissions without a terminal local status; trace each payment across systems. | 10 | 5 | 8 | 400 | Introduce an outbox/state machine with durable idempotency and scheme status lookup/callbacks; make ambiguous submissions explicitly `UNKNOWN` and reconcile before retrying. |
| F2 | Ledger posting is duplicated or disagrees with the scheme and account balance | `PaymentDispatcher` debits the account after scheme submission, catches only `LedgerTimeoutException`, and uses a random ledger reference. A timeout may occur after the ledger booked the posting; there is no automatic retry or reconciliation. | Double debit, missing ledger entry, or an account balance that disagrees with the core ledger. Customers see incorrect balances; finance and operations must perform manual repair. | Daily three-way reconciliation of payment, ledger-reference, and scheme data; alert on `POSTING_FAILED`, balance deltas, and ledger references not acknowledged. | 10 | 5 | 7 | 350 | Use a stable payment-derived ledger idempotency key, query ledger outcome after timeout, separate external calls from the request transaction, and provide replay/repair tooling. |
| F3 | Queued payment executes against insufficient funds or outside the intended limit | Queued creation checks current balance and today’s payments, but does not reserve funds; `QueuedPaymentJob` does not re-check balance, account status, or daily limit before dispatch. | Payment may overdraw or breach the bank limit after another payment has consumed the funds. Customers receive an unexpected debit; ledger, fraud, and support teams inherit an exception case. | Alert on negative balances, daily-limit breaches, and queued payments whose execution checks fail; reconcile queue execution against account policy. | 9 | 6 | 7 | 378 | Reserve queued amounts atomically (or re-authorize at dispatch), re-check status/balance/limit, and define a customer-visible outcome for insufficient funds. |
| F4 | Concurrent payments bypass balance or daily-limit checks | Reads and checks in `PaymentService` are not protected by an account lock/version check; two requests can validate the same balance and limit before either debit commits. | Multiple payments can be accepted beyond available funds or the bank’s limit. Customers and downstream ledger/scheme records diverge from the service’s policy. | Load test parallel payment requests; monitor negative balances and per-account daily totals over limit. | 9 | 5 | 7 | 315 | Add optimistic versioning or a database row lock around check-and-reserve/debit, with a concurrency test and database constraints where possible. |
| F5 | A ledger timeout leaves a payment marked `POSTING_FAILED` after money has moved | The code intentionally catches ledger timeout but cannot tell whether the ledger applied the request; it still debits the local account and notifies the customer. | Customer may be told or shown a failed payment while money has moved, or may retry and create a second instruction. Operations must manually determine whether to repair or reverse. | Page on every `POSTING_FAILED`; compare ledger acknowledgements by reference; do not allow blind customer retry while outcome is unknown. | 10 | 6 | 5 | 300 | Model `PENDING_LEDGER`/`UNKNOWN` separately from failure, query the ledger by idempotency reference, and make customer status and retry semantics explicit. |
| F6 | Scheme outage or 5xx causes an unhelpful 500 and leaves state ambiguous | `HttpSchemeClient` maps client errors to rejection and resource-access errors to timeout, but does not consistently map server errors; the external call is inside the request transaction. | Customer gets a generic failure and may retry; if the scheme processed the request before the error, duplicate payment risk follows. API clients and support teams lack a stable retry contract. | Synthetic payment probes, dependency error-rate/latency metrics, and alerts on 5xx/timeouts; inspect payments left in `RECEIVED`. | 9 | 6 | 6 | 324 | Normalize all dependency failures, use bounded retry only with idempotency, persist an intermediate state before calling out, and return a documented pending/unknown response. |
| F7 | Scheduled queued-payment worker does not run or stops part-way through | The worker is an in-process `@Scheduled` job with no distributed lock, durable job lease, misfire handling, or monitoring. Deployment, clock, node failure, or an exception can skip work; multiple nodes can race. | Payments miss their execution date or can be submitted twice. Customers and payment operations see delayed or duplicate transfers; support queues grow silently. | Gauge age/count of `QUEUED` payments; alert when due work remains after 06:00 or when job duration/failures exceed thresholds; audit scheme duplicate responses. | 9 | 5 | 8 | 360 | Move dispatch to a durable queue/worker with leases, retries, idempotency and dead-letter handling, or add clustered scheduling plus a due-work monitor and replay operation. |
| F8 | Payment is completed but customer notification is not delivered | Notification has no configured timeout/retry/dead-letter policy; errors are swallowed after a warning log in `notifySent`. | Customer lacks timely confirmation and may submit a duplicate or contact support; notification and customer-care teams cannot distinguish a payment failure from a messaging failure. | Metric and alert for notification failures, notification lag, and payment-to-notification completion ratio; retain delivery status. | 6 | 7 | 6 | 252 | Publish notifications through an outbox with retry/DLQ and idempotency; expose delivery state separately from payment state. |
| F9 | Fraud screening is bypassed for payments | `FraudScreeningClient` and properties exist, but `PaymentService`/`PaymentDispatcher` never invoke them. This is a functional control gap rather than a transient outage. | Suspicious payments can reach the scheme without screening; customers, the bank, fraud operations, and regulatory reporting incur financial and compliance exposure. | Compare payment volume with fraud-screening request volume; release test must prove every eligible payment has a decision; fraud team reconciliation. | 10 | 6 | 9 | 540 | Treat as a release blocker: integrate screening before scheme submission, fail closed or queue on unavailable screening, persist the decision, and add accept/review/reject tests and metrics. |
| F10 | Cross-tenant or over-broad data access | Authorization depends on claims (`tenant`, `roles`, `scope`) and application-level filters. A claim-converter, controller, repository query, or future endpoint regression could omit the tenant check; there are no database row-level safeguards. | Confidential balances, transactions, payment payee details, risk scores, or support data can be disclosed or changed across banks. Dependent banks, security, legal, and incident-response teams face a severe breach. | Automated negative authorization matrix for every endpoint and tenant; audit logs for tenant/user/resource; security tests with forged combinations of role, scope, and tenant claims. | 10 | 3 | 8 | 240 | Centralize tenant predicates, add integration tests and property-based authorization cases, log access decisions without sensitive data, and consider database-level tenant isolation. |
| F11 | Staff-only information is returned to a customer | A new DTO/controller can expose entities directly or omit the `internal` filter. The model contains internal notes, case comments, and customer risk score that must never cross the customer boundary. | Fraud indicators, hardship information, or internal investigative comments leak to the customer; this can compromise controls and create privacy, conduct, and support issues. | Contract tests asserting DTO fields and internal records are absent for customer roles; response schema scanning; review every new endpoint. | 9 | 3 | 7 | 189 | Use role-specific DTOs and explicit repository filters, deny entity serialization, and add regression tests for internal notes/comments/risk score. |
| F12 | Authentication or authorization rejects valid callers, or accepts malformed claims | Identity-service key rotation, wrong public key, expired tokens, missing `tenant`/`sub`, or unexpected roles/scope format can make all calls 401/403 or produce incorrect authorities. | Customers cannot view or pay; support and approvers cannot work cases/closures. Identity, customer support, and incident teams see an outage or inconsistent access pattern. | Login/API synthetic tests per role and tenant; monitor 401/403 rate by issuer/key/version; alert on JWKS/key-rotation and token-claim validation errors. | 8 | 4 | 5 | 160 | Define issuer/audience/key-rotation behavior, validate required claims, use staged key rollout, and monitor authorization outcomes with safe correlation IDs. |
| F13 | Primary database is unavailable, loses data, or starts with wrong schema/fixtures | The configured database is in-memory H2 with `create-drop` and startup `data.sql`; production persistence, migration, backup, HA, and restore behavior are not represented in the repository. | API is unavailable or starts with empty/lossy data; balances, payments, cases, and audit history are lost or inconsistent. Every dependent channel and operations team is affected. | Startup/readiness checks, database connection/error and latency metrics, backup/restore drills, row-count/data-integrity checks, and migration smoke tests. | 10 | 3 | 6 | 180 | Use a supported persistent production database and versioned migrations, define backup/RPO/RTO, test restore and failover, and prohibit fixture initialization outside test/dev profiles. |
| F14 | Account balance and payment records diverge after partial transaction failure | The JPA transaction spans local writes and remote calls; rollback boundaries do not include the scheme or ledger. Runtime exceptions other than the handled timeout can roll back local state after remote side effects. | Customer balance, payment history, scheme and ledger can disagree. Statements, limit calculations, reconciliation, and customer support decisions become unreliable. | Invariant checks (balance vs ledger, payment state vs external state), transaction rollback/error metrics, and exception alerts with payment IDs. | 10 | 4 | 8 | 320 | Adopt an explicit saga/outbox design, idempotent external commands, durable transition audit, and automated reconciliation with quarantine for mismatches. |
| F15 | Closure request cannot be approved or rejected | The API exposes request creation and pending-listing only; `ClosureRequest` has status setters but there is no approver decision endpoint/service. | Customers cannot complete account closure; stale pending requests accumulate and support/operations must use manual database intervention, undermining the two-person control. | Monitor pending-request age/count and customer closure completion rate; API contract test must cover request-to-decision lifecycle. | 7 | 7 | 4 | 196 | Implement an approver-only, same-tenant approve/reject operation with separation-of-duties checks, state transition auditing, and a close-account workflow. |
| F16 | Duplicate closure requests are created | The check for an existing `PENDING` request is application-level and there is no unique constraint; concurrent requests can both pass it. | Duplicate work and ambiguous approval state; customers and approvers may act on different requests, while operations must clean up duplicates. | Query for multiple pending requests per account; alert on duplicates; concurrency test. | 6 | 4 | 6 | 144 | Add a database-enforced uniqueness strategy for one pending request per account and handle constraint conflicts as 409. |
| F17 | API is unavailable or latency grows without actionable operational signal | The project has no actuator dependency despite the README/security rule naming `/actuator/health`; logging is root `WARN`, and there are no visible metrics, traces, request correlation, or dependency dashboards. | Load balancers may route traffic to unhealthy instances; customers see timeouts; teams cannot distinguish database, identity, scheme, ledger, or application failure quickly. | Current control is mainly logs and synthetic calls; verify health endpoint and alert coverage in deployment. | 8 | 6 | 9 | 432 | Add readiness/liveness and dependency health, Micrometer metrics, structured logs with correlation/payment IDs, distributed tracing, dashboards, and alerts tied to customer SLOs. |
| F18 | Input or unexpected dependency errors become generic 500s and retry storms | `ApiExceptionHandler` covers only selected exceptions; validation and external clients can throw other runtime exceptions. Clients have no documented retry-after/idempotency contract. | Customers get unclear outcomes and may repeat payments; dependent channels amplify load during an outage; support receives incomplete error context. | Track 4xx/5xx by route and exception, add contract tests for dependency failures, and inspect repeated requests by idempotency key. | 8 | 5 | 6 | 240 | Add a safe global error envelope, classify retryable vs non-retryable errors, use idempotency keys, and apply client/server backoff and rate limits. |
| F19 | Production time/cutoff or scheduler behavior is wrong around DST/clock skew | Payment cutoff and worker use UK time, but external systems and nodes may use different zones; only the application clock is injected and the scheme’s accepted date/time contract is not visible. | Payments are executed or delayed on the wrong business day; customers and scheme operations see missed cutoffs and reconciliation discrepancies. | Boundary tests for weekends, bank holidays, DST, and node clock skew; compare accepted execution date with configured policy. | 7 | 4 | 6 | 168 | Use a single business-calendar service including bank holidays, synchronize clocks, document scheme timezone contract, and alert on cutoff/dispatch-date anomalies. |

## Priorities before release

The highest RPN items are the fraud-screening bypass (F9), missing observability (F17), queued-worker failure (F7),
queued-payment overspend (F3), and ambiguous external-payment state (F1/F2/F5/F14). F9 should block release because it
removes a stated financial-crime control. The payment saga and reconciliation work should be the next engineering
priority; operational dashboards and due-work alerts are the minimum interim control while that redesign is delivered.

The existing offline test suite passes, including happy-path, scheme rejection, ledger-timeout, authorization, and
queued-payment tests. Those tests demonstrate current behavior, but do not provide production health checks,
concurrency coverage, crash/rollback coverage, external-system reconciliation, or fraud-screening invocation coverage.
