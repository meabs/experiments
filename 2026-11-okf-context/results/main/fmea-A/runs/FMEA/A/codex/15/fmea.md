# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its H2/JPA persistence, bearer-token security, payment dispatch and
scheduled queue, and the configured scheme, fraud, ledger and notification integrations. It is based on the current
implementation and configuration in this repository, not on production telemetry. The ratings are qualitative:

- **Severity (S):** 1 = negligible customer or operational impact; 10 = major financial loss, privacy/security breach,
  or prolonged multi-tenant outage.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 = rare; 10 = expected frequently or on the normal critical path.
- **Detection (D):** 1 = almost certain to be detected immediately; 10 = unlikely to be detected before customer or
  dependent-team impact.
- **RPN:** `S × O × D`. Higher numbers should be addressed first; the number is a prioritisation aid, not a measure of
  safety or compliance.

The service currently uses an in-memory H2 datasource by default, has no visible metrics/alert configuration, and has no
reconciliation or dead-letter workflow in the repository. Production deployments may provide those outside the code;
where that is unknown, the detection rating assumes only the application behavior shown here.

## FMEA table

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers, systems and teams | Detection method | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F-01 | A payment is accepted by the scheme but is left `POSTING_FAILED` | The scheme call succeeds, then the ledger times out or returns an error. The payment is not automatically retried or reconciled. | The customer may see an incomplete/ambiguous payment; the scheme may pay the beneficiary while the local ledger and account balance disagree. Operations and finance must investigate manually; retrying can duplicate the payment. | Alert on `POSTING_FAILED`; compare scheme instructions, ledger postings and local payments; daily financial reconciliation. | 10 | 5 | 8 | **400** | Make scheme and ledger processing an explicit durable state machine. Use one stable idempotency/reference per payment at both dependencies, retry safely, provide a repair/reconciliation job, and prevent duplicate manual dispatch. |
| F-02 | Duplicate payment submission | Customer retries after a timeout/5xx, or the queue/job is run again; there is no request idempotency key and no uniqueness constraint on the scheme reference. | Duplicate money leaves the account or beneficiary receives two payments; support, scheme and ledger teams handle disputes and refunds. | Reconcile same-account/payee/amount/time patterns and duplicate scheme/ledger references; monitor retry and timeout rates. | 10 | 5 | 9 | **450** | Require an idempotency key for create, persist it with a unique constraint, return the existing payment for repeats, and use idempotent downstream references with an outbox/state machine. |
| F-03 | Concurrent payments bypass balance or daily-limit checks | Two requests read the same balance and today's payments before either transaction commits; `Account` has no locking/version field and `paidToday` is calculated in application code. | Overspending, negative local balance, and daily-limit violations; ledger/scheme reconciliation breaks and customers or fraud teams bear the loss. | Alert on negative balances, limit breaches, and local-vs-ledger variance; concurrency/load tests. | 10 | 6 | 8 | **480** | Use optimistic/pessimistic locking or an atomic reservation ledger, enforce limits transactionally in the database, and test parallel requests. Reconcile balance after every posting. |
| F-04 | Scheme or fraud dependency is unavailable or malformed | Scheme timeout is thrown from `HttpSchemeClient` but is not handled by `PaymentDispatcher`; fraud client can fail, and no payment path invokes `FraudScreeningClient`. | A scheme outage produces an unhelpful 500 and may lose the newly created payment due to transaction rollback. More seriously, the apparent fraud control is not on the payment path, so risky payments can proceed. Customer payments fail or become uncertain; fraud, support and operations teams lose a control and an audit trail. | Synthetic payment probes; alert on 5xx/latency; trace every payment to a fraud decision; report payments with no screening record. | 10 | 6 | 9 | **540** | Put screening before scheme submission and define fail-closed behavior for unavailable/`REFER` decisions. Handle timeouts with a durable `PENDING_REVIEW` state, bounded retries and operator visibility. Add contract tests proving every payment is screened. |
| F-05 | Queued payment is not dispatched on time | The single in-process `@Scheduled` job is disabled, the instance is down at 06:00, timezone/clock configuration is wrong, or a runtime exception stops a batch. There is no distributed lock, catch-up alert, or durable job queue. | Payments miss their execution date; customers miss bills and support must explain delays. The scheme, ledger and notification systems see late or missing work. | Gauge due queued payments, oldest queue age and dispatch lag; alert when due count or age is non-zero after the cutoff; job heartbeat. | 8 | 5 | 8 | **320** | Move dispatch to a durable queue/worker or implement a restart-safe catch-up job with leader locking, per-payment error isolation, backoff, dead-letter handling and lag alerts. |
| F-06 | Account is debited locally even though the external payment is not financially complete | `PaymentDispatcher` calls `account.debit` immediately after scheme acceptance, then marks `POSTING_FAILED` on ledger timeout. There is no compensating credit or authoritative balance source. | Customers see a reduced balance for a payment whose ledger posting is absent; available funds and later payments are wrong. Finance must manually correct the account. | Reconcile account balances and ledger balances; alert when `POSTING_FAILED` has a non-null scheme id or when local debit lacks a matching ledger posting. | 10 | 5 | 8 | **400** | Stop treating the local balance mutation as final until a durable ledger acknowledgement, or use a ledger reservation/commit protocol. Add compensation and reconciliation with ownership and SLA. |
| F-07 | Notification is not sent or is duplicated | Notification call has no configured timeout/retry/idempotency and is only caught after the payment is saved; retries around the payment workflow can send again. | Customers do not know a payment was sent or receive confusing duplicates. Support handles avoidable contacts; the payment itself may still be financially correct. | Notification delivery receipts, failed-send counter, provider DLQ and correlation by payment id. | 5 | 6 | 7 | **210** | Publish notifications through a transactional outbox, use a stable message id, provider idempotency and retry/DLQ policy. Make notification health separate from payment completion. |
| F-08 | Payment request is persisted as `RECEIVED` but the API fails, or the external outcome is unknown | Payment is saved before synchronous dispatch; a scheme timeout/unchecked integration exception can abort the transaction, while a network timeout can occur after the scheme accepted the instruction. | Customer receives an error and retries, potentially creating a duplicate; an accepted instruction may have no locally visible payment. Operations lack a reliable case to repair. | Trace request id to payment and scheme reference; query scheme for unknown outcomes; alert on 5xx after payment submission and on orphan scheme instructions. | 9 | 5 | 9 | **405** | Decouple acceptance from dispatch with an outbox and explicit `PENDING`/`UNKNOWN` status. Return a stable payment id, reconcile unknown scheme outcomes, and never ask the customer to infer whether to retry. |
| F-09 | Customer or staff data is exposed across tenants or roles | A future endpoint bypasses `AccessPolicy`, trusts an id without tenant filtering, or returns staff-only fields. The model has many repositories whose methods do not all include tenant criteria. | Cross-bank disclosure of balances, transactions, PII, internal notes or risk scores; regulatory breach and loss of customer trust. | Automated cross-tenant/role authorization tests for every endpoint; access-log anomaly detection; security review of new controllers. | 10 | 3 | 8 | **240** | Centralise tenant/resource authorization, require tenant-scoped repository queries, add deny-by-default endpoint tests and response-schema tests ensuring internal fields never reach customer roles. |
| F-10 | Forged, stale or misinterpreted identity claims grant wrong access | Identity-service key/configuration drift, incorrect `roles`/`scope` claim mapping, missing tenant claim, or compromised signing key. | Unauthorized reads or payments, or broad denial of service if valid tokens stop verifying. Identity, security and every tenant are affected. | Token-validation failure rate, key-rotation canaries, auth decision logs without sensitive token contents, and security monitoring. | 10 | 2 | 8 | **160** | Establish key rotation and rollback tests, issuer/audience/expiry validation, claim-contract tests, short token lifetimes/revocation strategy, and alerts for unusual role/tenant access. |
| F-11 | Database state is lost or reset | The configured `jdbc:h2:mem:accounts;DB_CLOSE_DELAY=-1` database is process memory and JPA uses `create-drop`; a restart loses payments, closure requests and operational data and reloads fixtures. | Balances, payment history, cases and audit evidence disappear or revert. Customers and dependent ledger/support teams receive inconsistent answers; this is a severe release/production risk if used outside tests. | Startup logs, datasource/configuration policy checks, persistence smoke test across restart, compare local records to ledger. | 10 | 3 | 10 | **300** | Use a production persistent, tenant-safe database with migrations and backups; prohibit in-memory/create-drop outside test profiles; add restore drills and startup guardrails that fail deployment on unsafe configuration. |
| F-12 | Closure workflow stalls or closes inconsistently | The API creates and lists closure requests but exposes no approval/rejection or account-state transition endpoint; `ClosureRequest` uses `Instant.now()` rather than the configured clock. | Customers cannot complete closure; pending requests accumulate, or an out-of-band process may close an account without coordinated payment checks. Support and approvers lack a complete audit trail. | Monitor pending-request age/count, reconcile closed accounts with requests, and alert on requests older than SLA. | 7 | 6 | 8 | **336** | Implement an atomic, authorized approve/reject workflow with second-person enforcement, payment/ balance checks, idempotency and audit timestamps from the injected clock. Add expiry/escalation for stale requests. |
| F-13 | Scheduled job or one payment aborts processing for the remaining queue | `QueuedPaymentJob.runDue` processes a list in one transaction and does not isolate exceptions per payment; unchecked scheme errors can stop the loop and roll back the batch. | One bad dependency response delays unrelated customers' payments and may cause repeated retries or duplicate attempts after restart. Operations sees a partial, unclear batch result. | Per-payment dispatch outcome, batch completion metric, queue age and alert when due items remain after a run. | 8 | 5 | 8 | **320** | Process each payment independently, record attempt/error state, use bounded retries and dead-letter items, and expose batch metrics and an operator replay action. |
| F-14 | Public API returns internal exception details or inconsistent error status | Only a small set of domain exceptions are mapped; integration/runtime/JPA failures fall through to generic framework handling. Timeouts and dependency failures have no consistent customer-safe contract. | Customers receive unstable 500 responses and may retry; sensitive stack/configuration details may leak depending on deployment. Client teams cannot implement reliable recovery. | API contract tests for all dependency failures; scan responses/logs for stack traces; 4xx/5xx dashboards. | 6 | 6 | 7 | **252** | Add a global safe error envelope with correlation id, explicit dependency-timeout/unknown-payment statuses, structured logs, and tests for every integration exception. Do not expose stack traces or dependency URLs. |
| F-15 | Service becomes unavailable or overloaded | Synchronous calls can occupy request threads for up to scheme 10s, fraud 2s and ledger 5s; there is no visible circuit breaker, bulkhead, rate limit or backpressure. Expensive unpaged account/payment queries can also grow with data. | Login-protected API requests time out or return 5xx; customers cannot view or pay, and support/back-office work queues grow. Dependencies can be amplified by retry storms. | Latency/error/saturation metrics, dependency timeout rate, thread-pool and DB-pool utilisation, load tests and health probes. | 8 | 6 | 7 | **336** | Add timeouts to every client including notifications, circuit breakers/bulkheads, rate limits and bounded retries. Page large reads, set DB/query limits, autoscale on measured saturation and test dependency degradation. |
| F-16 | Sensitive payment and customer data is logged or retained beyond need | Payment objects contain full payee account details; exception logging currently includes exception objects, and operational logging/retention policy is not defined in the repository. | Bank-account data may reach logs, traces or support tooling, increasing privacy and breach impact. Incident response and compliance teams inherit a larger data footprint. | Secret/PII scanning of logs and traces, retention audits, access review and red-team tests. | 8 | 4 | 8 | **256** | Redact account numbers and payment payloads, use structured allow-listed fields and correlation ids, encrypt/restrict logs, define retention/deletion policy, and add automated log redaction tests. |

## Priority actions before release

1. Block payment release until idempotency, concurrency-safe balance/limit enforcement, fraud-screening invocation, and
   scheme/ledger reconciliation are designed and tested (F-01 through F-04 and F-06/F-08).
2. Replace the in-process queue behavior with restart-safe, observable processing and per-payment failure isolation
   (F-05 and F-13).
3. Ensure production cannot run the in-memory `create-drop` datasource, and prove backup/restore behavior (F-11).
4. Add actionable metrics and alerts for payment states, queue lag, dependency failures, authorization failures,
   pending closures and reconciliation differences. Logging alone is not sufficient detection for financial failures.
5. Complete and test the closure approval workflow and establish safe, stable error responses for clients (F-12 and
   F-14).
