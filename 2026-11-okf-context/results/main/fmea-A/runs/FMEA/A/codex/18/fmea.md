# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and method

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its H2/JPA persistence, authentication and tenant authorization, payment lifecycle, closure workflow, support cases, and the configured scheme, fraud, ledger, and notification integrations. It is based on the implementation, `README.md`, `application.yml`, fixture data, and API tests; it is not a substitute for production traffic, schema, resilience, or operational telemetry review.

Scores use the conventional FMEA scale: severity (S) 1 = negligible, 10 = financial loss, material customer harm, regulatory/security breach, or prolonged outage; occurrence (O) 1 = rare, 10 = frequent or expected under normal operation; detection (D) 1 = almost certain to detect before customer impact, 10 = unlikely to detect until a customer, downstream system, or reconciliation process reports it. RPN = S × O × D. Scores are relative release-triage scores, not probabilities. Highest RPNs should be addressed first, but any S=10 security or money-movement issue is release-critical regardless of RPN.

## Failure mode analysis

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers | Effect on dependent systems and teams | Detection in operation | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| FMEA-01 | A payment is sent without fraud screening | `FraudScreeningClient` and `Decision` exist, but `PaymentService`/`PaymentDispatcher` never invokes them; `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` is not used | Fraudulent or mule payments can be submitted; customers can suffer loss and may receive no protective hold | Fraud, financial-crime, disputes, and operations teams lose a mandatory control; scheme and ledger receive unsafe instructions | Compare payment volume with fraud-screening requests (currently expected to be zero); review fraud-loss/dispute alerts and audit trails | 10 | 6 | 9 | 540 | Make screening a mandatory, fail-closed step before scheme submission. Persist the decision and request ID, define `REFER`/`REJECT` handling, timeout policy, and add tests proving every payment is screened and unavailable screening cannot pay out. |
| FMEA-02 | Closure requests remain pending and accounts are not closed | There is an endpoint to create requests and list pending requests, but no approve/reject endpoint or state-transition service; `Account.status` is never changed by the closure flow | Customers cannot complete a requested closure; closed-account servicing and payment eligibility can be wrong | Approvers, customer support, complaints, and regulatory operations carry stale work; downstream account state never changes | Ageing report of `PENDING` requests, requests older than SLA, and comparison of approved requests to account status; synthetic approval workflow test | 8 | 7 | 8 | 448 | Implement authenticated approve/reject transitions with tenant and approver checks, separation of requester/approver, optimistic locking/idempotency, and an atomic account-state update. Add alerts for pending age and reconciliation of request versus account state. |
| FMEA-03 | Scheme accepted a payment but the API reports an error or loses the payment record | A scheme timeout can occur after acceptance; `PaymentDispatcher` only handles rejection, so the exception rolls back the local transaction. A retry creates a new `P<id>` reference | Customer sees failure/unknown outcome and may retry, causing a duplicate payment; balance and payment history become misleading | Scheme has an instruction that accounts service cannot reconcile; payments, operations, and complaints teams must investigate manually | Reconcile scheme instructions against local `schemeId`/payment IDs; alert on timeout exceptions and unknown references; customer reports duplicate/missing payment | 10 | 4 | 9 | 360 | Use a durable outbox/state machine and a stable idempotency key created before submission. On timeout, persist `SUBMISSION_UNKNOWN` and reconcile/query the scheme before retrying; never generate a new instruction for an unresolved attempt. |
| FMEA-04 | Scheme payment and local ledger diverge | Scheme submission succeeds, then ledger posting times out. The code debits the local account object and stores `POSTING_FAILED`; there is no retry/reconciliation worker. A timeout may also mean the ledger applied the posting | Customer sees a payment in an ambiguous `POSTING_FAILED` state and may have an incorrect available balance | Core ledger, scheme, finance, and operations disagree; manual repair and financial reconciliation are required | Alert on `POSTING_FAILED`, compare scheme accepted instructions, ledger postings, account balances, and local payment status; inspect timeout logs | 10 | 5 | 8 | 400 | Persist a stable ledger reference before calling the ledger; make ledger posting idempotent; add bounded retry/reconciliation and a clear customer-visible state. Reconcile every scheme-accepted payment to exactly one ledger debit before marking complete. |
| FMEA-05 | Duplicate or excessive payments pass balance/daily-limit checks | Concurrent requests read the same balance and `paidToday` total; no database lock, unique idempotency key, or atomic reservation exists | Two requests can overdraw an account or exceed the configured daily limit, with multiple external instructions | Ledger, scheme, fraud, finance, and support teams see duplicate obligations and have to unwind them | Aggregate payments by account/day and compare ledger debits with limits; alert on negative balances, duplicate customer references, and limit breaches; concurrency tests | 9 | 5 | 8 | 360 | Require an idempotency key; enforce unique persistence; reserve funds and daily-limit capacity atomically with row locking/serializable transaction semantics before dispatch. Test concurrent submissions and retries. |
| FMEA-06 | Queued payments are not executed on time | Scheduled execution is a single in-process `@Scheduled` job at 06:00; process downtime, deployment, clock/time-zone error, or an exception can prevent or interrupt processing. There is no claimed/leased work model | Payment is delayed beyond its promised execution date; the customer may retry and create a duplicate | Scheme receives late instructions; customer notifications, operations, support, and settlement teams lose predictable cut-off behavior | Alert on queued payments whose execution date is in the past; metric for oldest queue age and job success/count; heartbeat for the 06:00 run | 8 | 5 | 7 | 280 | Use a durable queue/outbox and a repeatable worker with row claiming/leases, retry/backoff, dead-letter handling, and idempotent dispatch. Alert on missed runs and overdue queue age; define late-payment policy. |
| FMEA-07 | A single bad queued payment prevents later queued payments from being processed | `QueuedPaymentJob` loops in one transaction and does not isolate failures per payment; an uncaught scheme/ledger exception exits the loop and can roll back work | Some customers receive no payment or notification even though their payment was due | Backlog grows silently; scheme, ledger, and support teams see partial batch behavior | Compare due count with dispatched count; alert on job exception, queue age, and per-payment retry count | 8 | 5 | 7 | 280 | Process each payment independently with durable status transitions, bounded retries, and a dead-letter/manual-review state. Continue the batch after one failure and expose per-payment outcome metrics. |
| FMEA-08 | Cross-tenant or unauthorized account/customer data is disclosed or changed | Authorization is implemented in application code using token `tenant`, roles, assignments, and customer lookup; a claim-mapping/configuration error or future endpoint that omits `AccessPolicy` can bypass it. Payment reads also rely on account visibility rather than an explicit payment tenant predicate | Personal, financial, internal-note, risk-score, or transaction data can be exposed; unauthorized payment/closure actions may occur | Tenant banks, identity/security, privacy, compliance, and support teams face breach response and loss of trust | Deny/allow matrix tests for every endpoint and tenant; security audit logs keyed by subject/tenant/resource; canary cross-tenant requests and automated authorization scans | 10 | 3 | 8 | 240 | Centralize and enforce tenant/resource authorization at service/repository boundaries, include tenant predicates on every resource query, add deny-by-default tests for new routes, and log security decisions without sensitive payloads. |
| FMEA-09 | Internal notes/comments or risk data are returned to customers | Staff-only entities exist, but the reviewed controllers do not provide a complete notes/comments API; a future serialization or projection could expose entities directly, especially if a query returns all comments without filtering `internal` | Fraud, hardship, dispute, and other confidential staff information leaks to the customer or wrong staff user | Privacy/compliance incident; support and fraud investigations are compromised | Contract tests asserting customer payloads never contain internal fields; DLP/schema scanning; review access logs and outbound payloads | 9 | 3 | 8 | 216 | Use explicit customer/staff DTOs and repository queries that filter `internal`; never serialize JPA entities. Add negative tests for each role, tenant, note, and comment path. |
| FMEA-10 | Identity or tenant claims are accepted incorrectly | Authorization trusts signed JWT claims; `CurrentUserProvider` accepts a missing tenant as a string value and role/scope mapping is configuration-sensitive. Key rotation or identity-service outage/misconfiguration can reject all traffic or grant wrong authorities | Customers may be locked out, or in the worst case access the wrong tenant or capability | Identity platform, all API consumers, support, and security operations see a broad outage or security incident | Monitor 401/403 rate by issuer/key/tenant; validate JWT issuer/audience/key rotation; alert on unusual tenant/role distributions and authentication failures | 10 | 2 | 8 | 160 | Validate issuer, audience, required `sub` and tenant claims, approved roles/scopes, and key rotation in configuration. Add startup/configuration checks, sampled security audit logs, and authentication/authorization availability alerts. |
| FMEA-11 | Database is unavailable, loses data, or starts with the wrong schema/fixtures | Production configuration shown uses in-memory H2 with `create-drop` and SQL fixtures; a real deployment may also suffer connection exhaustion, migration failure, corruption, or accidental fixture loading | API requests fail or show stale/missing accounts, balances, cases, and payments; payment outcomes become uncertain | All dependent channels fail; operations and data/platform teams need restore/reconciliation; money movement may be left mid-flight | Database connectivity/latency/error metrics, migration/startup checks, backups and restore drills, row-count/checksum and balance reconciliation | 10 | 3 | 7 | 210 | Use a supported durable production database with explicit migrations, environment-specific configuration that cannot load fixtures or drop schema, HA/backups/PITR, connection-pool limits, and restore/reconciliation runbooks. |
| FMEA-12 | API or integration dependency becomes unavailable or slow | External clients use fixed timeouts (fraud 2s, ledger 5s, scheme 10s); notification has no configured timeout; there is no circuit breaker, bulkhead, or general rate limit | Reads/writes time out; payments may be ambiguous; notifications are delayed; retries from customers amplify load | Thread/connection pools can be exhausted; scheme, fraud, ledger, notification, and incident teams receive cascading load | RED metrics (rate, errors, duration), dependency health probes, timeout/circuit alerts, saturation metrics, and trace correlation by payment ID | 8 | 5 | 6 | 240 | Set explicit connect/read timeouts for every client, add circuit breakers/bulkheads/rate limits, propagate correlation/payment IDs, and define retry policy by operation (never blindly retry money movement). |
| FMEA-13 | Notification is lost or misleading after a payment | Notification is sent synchronously after dispatch; failures are caught and only logged, with no durable outbox or retry. The message is labelled `PAYMENT_SENT` even for `POSTING_FAILED` | Customer may not know a payment was made, or may receive a sent notification for a payment whose ledger posting failed | Support and complaints teams handle avoidable “missing notification” cases; notification service has no guaranteed delivery record | Compare completed/submitted/failed payments with notification delivery receipts; alert on notification exceptions and outbox age | 7 | 6 | 8 | 336 | Publish payment events through a durable transactional outbox, retry with idempotent notification keys, and send distinct templates for submitted, completed, failed, and review states. |
| FMEA-14 | Service reports an unhandled error or leaks operational details | `ApiExceptionHandler` maps only selected exceptions; integration/client, database, and scheduling exceptions are not consistently mapped or assigned correlation IDs. Logs are WARN-only by default | Customers receive 500s with inconsistent behavior and cannot tell whether a payment succeeded; support lacks a reliable reference | On-call triage is slower; downstream clients may retry unsafe operations; incident diagnosis and auditability suffer | 5xx rate, exception-class alerts, structured logs, traces, and synthetic tests for each dependency failure | 7 | 6 | 6 | 252 | Add a safe catch-all error contract with correlation ID, structured redacted logs, metrics/traces, dependency-specific status mapping, and client guidance for unknown payment outcomes. |
| FMEA-15 | Customer or dependent channel cannot use the API during a release/configuration failure | The only explicitly permitted health route is `/actuator/health`, but no actuator dependency is present in `pom.xml`; startup depends on public-key and integration configuration, and there is no documented readiness/liveness behavior | Login/API access is unavailable or clients receive misleading health status during rollout | Load balancers may route to unready instances; release, platform, and support teams cannot distinguish startup, dependency, and application failure | Deployment smoke tests for health/readiness, startup logs, instance availability, and synthetic authenticated API checks | 8 | 4 | 7 | 224 | Add the actuator/health dependency and distinct liveness/readiness checks, verify the route actually exists, fail readiness on essential local dependencies, and gate deployment on authenticated API smoke tests. |

## Release priorities

1. Treat FMEA-01 (fraud screening bypass), FMEA-02 (non-functional closure approval), and FMEA-03/FMEA-04 (ambiguous money movement) as release blockers until the intended control and reconciliation behavior is implemented and tested.
2. Before launch, add idempotency/concurrency protection (FMEA-05), durable queued-payment processing (FMEA-06/FMEA-07), and production database/migration safeguards (FMEA-11).
3. Instrument the service so that payment, queue, dependency, authentication, and data-reconciliation failures are detectable without waiting for customer reports (FMEA-08, FMEA-10, FMEA-12–FMEA-15).

## Verification baseline

The repository's API tests use MockMvc, H2 fixtures, fake external integrations, and a mutable test clock. They cover normal authorization, scheme rejection, one ledger timeout, queue dispatch when invoked directly, and basic tenant isolation. They do not establish production resilience or prove fraud screening, approval transitions, concurrent payment safety, durable retries, database recovery, or observability. Run the repository's required offline test command after changes:

```text
mvn -o -q test
```
