# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its scheduled payment dispatcher, the database, authentication and
the scheme, fraud, ledger and notification integrations described in `README.md` and `application.yml`. It is based on
the implementation in this repository; production deployment, network controls and operational runbooks were not
provided.

Scores use the conventional FMEA direction: Severity (S) is 1 = negligible, 10 = customer-money loss, material
security/privacy breach or prolonged regulatory/business outage; Occurrence (O) is 1 = unlikely and 10 = frequent or
expected without a control; Detection (D) is 1 = almost certain to detect before customer impact and 10 = unlikely to
detect without a customer or reconciliation report. The risk priority number is `RPN = S × O × D`. RPN is a triage
aid, not a substitute for judgement: any S=9 or S=10 item should be treated as a release blocker until controlled.

The code has useful API-level tests for tenant isolation, scopes, payment cutoff/rejection, ledger timeout status and
the queued job. Those tests are an existing detection control, but they do not prove production persistence,
concurrency, network failure recovery or observability.

## Failure modes

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers and dependent systems/teams | Detection | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F-01 | Account/payment/case data disappears on restart or is not durable | `application.yml` uses an in-memory H2 database (`DB_CLOSE_DELAY=-1`), with `ddl-auto: create-drop` and startup fixtures | Balances, payments, cases and closure requests are lost when the process stops. Ledger, support and reconciliation records diverge; customers may see an empty or reset account view. | Persistence smoke test across restart; database/storage alerts; reconciliation against the ledger | 10 | 10 | 7 | 700 | Use a managed production database with migrations, backups, restore drills and HA. Remove `create-drop`/fixture initialization outside tests and make durability a deployment gate. |
| F-02 | Payment accepted by scheme but not represented locally or retried safely | Scheme timeout can occur after acceptance; `SchemeTimeoutException` is not handled by `PaymentDispatcher`. The transaction may roll back after the remote side has accepted `P{id}`, and there is no reconciliation or retry state. | A payment can leave the customer uncertain, be paid once remotely but absent locally, or be submitted again later. Operations and the scheme cannot reliably resolve the instruction. | Alert on 5xx/timeouts and compare scheme acknowledgements with local `schemeId`/payment records; customer complaints | 10 | 4 | 8 | 320 | Persist an outbox/instruction record before submission; use a stable idempotency key and query/reconcile unknown outcomes. Handle timeouts as `UNKNOWN`/`REVIEW`, with bounded retry and an operator workflow. |
| F-03 | Scheme is unavailable or rejects unexpectedly and payment creation fails noisily | Scheme call is synchronous with a 10-second timeout; timeout/error paths are not converted to a stable API status or queued state. Only explicit `SchemeRejectedException` is handled. | Customers receive a generic server error or long wait; no reliable payment status may be available. Support cannot distinguish retryable outage from rejection; upstream clients may retry and create duplicate intent. | Request latency/5xx/timeout metrics, scheme health/SLO alerts, structured payment status counts | 8 | 5 | 5 | 200 | Add a failure-state contract and idempotent create key; circuit breaker/bulkhead and bounded retries only for safely retryable outcomes; provide a status endpoint and runbook. |
| F-04 | Scheme submission succeeds but ledger posting fails or is ambiguous | The service submits to the scheme, debits its local `Account`, then posts to the ledger. On `LedgerTimeoutException` it records `POSTING_FAILED`, but there is no automatic retry/reconciliation; the timeout may occur after the ledger applied the posting. | Money may move at the scheme while the ledger or account view is inconsistent. Customers can be charged incorrectly or see a stale balance; finance and operations need manual repair. | `POSTING_FAILED` status and warning log exist, but no metric/alert or reconciliation job is implemented; ledger-vs-service comparison | 10 | 4 | 8 | 320 | Make ledger posting idempotent on the stable payment reference, persist a durable outbox, retry/reconcile `POSTING_FAILED`, and alert with payment/account identifiers. Define which system is authoritative for balance. |
| F-05 | Duplicate payments or daily-limit/balance bypass under concurrency | Balance and `paidToday()` are read before dispatch; there is no locking, idempotency key or unique business constraint. Concurrent requests can pass the same checks. | Customers can exceed daily limits, overdraw the local view or send duplicate scheme instructions. Ledger, fraud and support teams must unwind transactions. | Load/concurrency tests; reconciliation of daily totals and duplicate references; alerts on negative/limit exceptions | 9 | 5 | 8 | 360 | Require an idempotency key; serialize per account or use optimistic/pessimistic locking and atomic limit reservation. Enforce constraints at the authoritative ledger and test concurrent requests. |
| F-06 | Queued payment is missed, duplicated or runs with no operator visibility | `@Scheduled` runs in-process at 06:00 on one instance; no lease/claim, retry, dead-letter state, misfire handling or explicit job metrics. Multiple instances can process the same row. | Payments are late, absent or submitted twice. Customers and payees miss expected settlement; operations cannot see backlog or stuck work. | Gauge queued-due age/count, job success/failure and duration; alert on missed 06:00 run and old `QUEUED` rows; idempotency reconciliation | 9 | 5 | 8 | 360 | Move dispatch to a durable queue/workflow with leases and retry/dead-letter handling, or implement DB claiming plus distributed scheduling. Add backlog and oldest-item alerts and an operator replay tool. |
| F-07 | Fraud screening is bypassed | Fraud client and decisions exist, but no production payment path calls `FraudScreeningClient`; `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` is never set by the shown service. | Suspicious payments can be sent directly to the scheme, increasing fraud loss and compliance exposure. Fraud operations receive no screening event or review queue. | Test that every payment invokes screening; monitor screening request/payment ratios; fraud-loss and scheme anomaly reports | 10 | 3 | 10 | 300 | Put screening before scheme submission. Fail closed on `REJECT`, route `REFER` to a durable review queue, define unavailable behavior, and add a release test proving no payment bypasses screening. |
| F-08 | Internal account/customer data is exposed across tenant or role boundary | Authorization depends on token `tenant` and role claims, plus resource checks. A compromised/misissued token or inconsistent database relationship can defeat assumptions; there is no visible audit trail. Staff-only entities also have no implemented read controller to verify all future paths. | Cross-bank disclosure of balances, PII, risk scores or internal notes; regulatory incident and loss of customer trust. Dependent support/admin tools may consume unauthorized data. | Existing cross-tenant API tests; security integration tests with forged/mismatched claims; access-denied audit logs and periodic authorization review | 10 | 3 | 8 | 240 | Validate token issuer/audience/key rotation and tenant membership at the identity boundary; enforce tenant predicates in repositories/DB policies, not only in Java; add deny-by-default endpoint tests and immutable access audit logs. |
| F-09 | JWT verification or authorization configuration is wrong in production | The public key is a classpath file (`keys/public.pem`); issuer/audience validation is not configured in `application.yml`; roles and scopes are trusted from claims. Key rotation/config drift can reject all users or accept an incorrectly trusted token. | Total API outage, or unauthorized access to customer and payment operations. Identity, support and incident teams are forced into emergency recovery. | Startup/config validation; synthetic 401/200/403 probes; key-rotation test; security alerts for unusual role/scope use | 10 | 3 | 8 | 240 | Configure issuer/audience and managed JWKS/key rotation, fail startup on invalid security config, separate environment secrets/configuration, and run synthetic authentication probes. |
| F-10 | Closure requests accumulate and accounts cannot be closed | The repository exposes request creation and pending-list endpoints, but no approval/rejection/closure operation is implemented. A request stays `PENDING`; no SLA or ageing job exists. | Customers cannot complete closure, funds/support work remains open, and approvers/operations have an unbounded queue. | Count and age pending requests; customer case/contact; endpoint/workflow acceptance test | 7 | 7 | 6 | 294 | Implement a second-person approve/reject workflow with tenant and role checks, atomic account state transition, notification, audit trail and pending-age alerts. |
| F-11 | Database or connection pool becomes unavailable | All API reads/writes depend on JPA; there is no explicit production DB configuration, pool limits, readiness behavior or resilience policy in the repository. | Account, payment and support APIs return errors or hang; scheduled payment work may fail. Customers and dependent channels lose service. | `/actuator/health` is permitted in security rules, but no actuator dependency/configured checks are present; add DB readiness, latency and pool alerts | 9 | 4 | 5 | 180 | Add production DB health/readiness, bounded pool/timeouts, HA/failover and backup monitoring. Ensure failed queued work remains recoverable and does not disappear in a rolled-back transaction. |
| F-12 | Notification failure delays or loses customer communication | Notification client has no configured timeout in `application.yml`; it is called inline after payment processing. Exceptions are caught and only logged, with no durable message/outbox or retry. | Payment may complete but the customer does not know; dependent notification/support teams cannot prove delivery, and duplicate customer contact may result. | Notification delivery/failure metrics, provider callbacks and reconciliation of completed payments to messages; current warning logs are insufficient | 6 | 6 | 8 | 288 | Publish notifications from a durable outbox after commit, add client timeouts/circuit breaker and retry/dead-letter handling, and monitor delivery lag/failure rate. |
| F-13 | Local balance and transaction history diverge from the core ledger | The service mutates `Account.balance` locally, while `Transaction` is read-only in the shown API and ledger posting is a separate call. There is no reconciliation or atomic cross-system protocol. | Customers see an incorrect balance/history; payment limit decisions become unsafe; finance and support cannot trust statements. | Daily ledger-to-account reconciliation, balance-difference alerts and customer statement checks; no such job is implemented | 10 | 5 | 8 | 400 | Make the ledger authoritative or implement a transactional outbox/consumer that updates a durable projection. Reconcile every payment and surface discrepancies to operations before allowing further risk. |
| F-14 | API becomes slow/unavailable under large tenant or support workloads | List endpoints return unpaged collections; support listing first loads assignment IDs then `findAllById`; JPA queries have no visible pagination/index strategy. Payment calls also hold a request through external timeouts. | Admin/support consoles time out, API threads and DB resources are exhausted, and customer payment/account requests are delayed. | p95/p99 latency, request/thread/DB pool saturation, payload-size and query-time alerts; load tests with production-sized tenants | 8 | 5 | 6 | 240 | Add pagination and bounded response sizes, indexes and query plans, connection/client timeouts, bulkheads, rate limits and load tests at expected tenant scale. |
| F-15 | Sensitive payment data is overexposed in responses or logs | `PaymentView` returns full payee sort code and account number; exception handling/logging and HTTP client logging are not centrally redacted in the repository. | PII/payment details can leak to clients, logs or observability vendors, increasing fraud and privacy impact. | DLP/log scanning, API schema review, access-log review and penetration tests; current tests do not assert redaction | 8 | 4 | 8 | 256 | Return only masked payment details where possible, classify and redact logs/traces, encrypt/protect observability data, apply retention limits and add schema/security regression tests. |
| F-16 | Clock/cutoff and calendar rules schedule payments incorrectly | Working days are hard-coded Monday-Friday; bank holidays are not modelled. Scheme cutoff and scheduled job use Europe/London, while persistence and some entity timestamps use `Instant.now()` directly. | Payments execute late/early or breach customer expectations; reconciliation and customer communications carry inconsistent dates. | Boundary tests around DST, weekends and bank holidays; compare requested/executed dates and alert on overdue queue | 7 | 5 | 7 | 245 | Use an injected, centrally configured business calendar and timezone; use the injected clock everywhere; test DST/holiday/cutoff boundaries and publish the calculated execution date clearly. |
| F-17 | Generic/unhandled integration or application exceptions produce opaque 500s | `ApiExceptionHandler` maps validation and a few domain exceptions only. Rest-client 4xx/5xx behavior differs by client, and timeout/retry semantics are inconsistent. | Customers and dependent clients cannot tell whether to correct input, wait or retry; repeated retries can amplify payment risk and support volume. | 5xx rate, exception classification, trace IDs and contract tests; current tests cover only selected happy/error paths | 7 | 6 | 6 | 252 | Define an error taxonomy and stable problem responses, map integration failures explicitly, include correlation IDs, and document retryability for each endpoint/status. |

## Highest-priority release actions

1. Replace H2/in-memory state with durable, migrated production storage and prove backup/restore (F-01).
2. Make payment submission, ledger posting and queued dispatch idempotent and recoverable, with an outbox/reconciliation
   workflow and alerts for unknown/failed states (F-02, F-04, F-05, F-06, F-13).
3. Put fraud screening in the payment path and define fail-closed/review behavior (F-07).
4. Validate production JWT issuer/audience/key rotation and add authorization/audit regression coverage (F-08, F-09).
5. Add production observability: readiness, metrics for payment states/queues/integrations, traces with redaction, and
   actionable runbooks (F-03, F-06, F-11, F-12, F-14, F-15, F-17).
6. Complete the closure approval workflow and its audit/notification controls before presenting closure as supported
   (F-10).
