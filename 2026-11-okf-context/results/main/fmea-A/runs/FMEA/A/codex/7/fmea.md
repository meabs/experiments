# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its H2/JPA persistence, JWT authorization, payment workflow,
scheduled payment dispatch, account/case/closure APIs, and the configured scheme, fraud, ledger, and notification
integrations. It is based on the repository implementation and configuration, not on production incident data.

Scores use the conventional 1–10 scale: severity (S) is the impact if the failure occurs, occurrence (O) is the
expected frequency or exposure, and detection (D) is how unlikely the failure is to be detected before customer or
dependent-system impact (1 = very easy to detect, 10 = difficult). RPN = S × O × D. Higher RPN should be addressed
first, but any item with S 9 or 10 warrants explicit release acceptance even when its RPN is lower.

The service has no visible metrics, tracing, alert definitions, retry/dead-letter mechanism, or durable reconciliation
process in this repository. Detection recommendations therefore assume standard API, database, scheduler, and
integration telemetry is added where noted.

## Failure mode analysis

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers | Effect on dependent systems and teams | Detection | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| FMEA-01 | Payment is accepted by the scheme but ledger posting fails or times out | `PaymentDispatcher` submits to the scheme, then debits the local account and posts separately; a ledger timeout leaves `POSTING_FAILED` with no automatic reconciliation | Payment may be sent while balance and transaction history are wrong; customer support cannot reliably explain or correct the state | Scheme and ledger disagree; finance/reconciliation and incident teams must manually investigate; a retry could duplicate the payment | Alert on `POSTING_FAILED`; compare scheme receipts, ledger postings, account debits and payment rows by id/reference; track age and count of unreconciled items | 10 | 4 | 7 | 280 | Make the workflow explicitly recoverable: use an idempotency key at scheme and ledger, persist an outbox/state transition, implement bounded retry plus dead-letter/manual repair, and run continuous reconciliation. Do not treat a timeout as proof that no posting occurred. |
| FMEA-02 | Duplicate external payment on retry or job replay | No idempotency check on create or dispatch; the scheme key is derived from the local payment id, but repeated dispatch and upstream behavior are not reconciled; scheduled jobs may overlap across instances | Customer can be charged twice or see duplicate outgoing payments | Scheme, ledger, support and finance records diverge; urgent payment operations response is required | Alert on duplicate scheme/ledger references, duplicate customer requests, repeated dispatch attempts, and payment count/amount anomalies | 10 | 5 | 8 | 400 | Require client idempotency keys with a unique database constraint, make dispatch claim rows atomically, enforce a single-consumer/lease strategy, and verify downstream idempotency semantics before retrying. |
| FMEA-03 | Payment is lost or remains `QUEUED` past its execution date | `QueuedPaymentJob` is a single 06:00 London cron with no catch-up trigger, retry, lease, or failure queue; scheduling is process-local | Delayed or missing payment, missed customer expectation, and possible late-payment fees | Scheme receives fewer instructions; support and payment operations need a backlog/replay process | Gauge due queued payments and oldest age; alert when due count or oldest queued age exceeds SLA; emit one outcome event per dispatch | 9 | 5 | 6 | 270 | Move due work to a durable queue/outbox with retries and dead letters, use a distributed lock/claim, and run a frequent catch-up poll so restarts do not lose the 06:00 window. |
| FMEA-04 | Scheme rejection, timeout, or malformed response is mishandled | Only HTTP 4xx is mapped to `REJECTED`; timeouts become an unhandled runtime error, and a null/malformed response can fail after the request | Customer receives a 5xx or ambiguous result and may retry; a timeout may hide whether the payment was accepted | Operations cannot tell whether to retry safely; scheme and local state may diverge | Measure integration latency/status/exception class; alert on 5xx spikes, timeout rate, malformed payloads, and `RECEIVED` rows older than a threshold | 9 | 5 | 7 | 315 | Define an explicit state machine for accepted/rejected/unknown, validate response schemas, persist correlation/idempotency keys, and provide a safe unknown-payment reconciliation path. Map expected upstream errors to documented API responses. |
| FMEA-05 | Fraud screening is bypassed | Fraud client and `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` status exist, but no production payment path calls `FraudScreeningClient` | Fraudulent payments may be released without screening; customers and banks bear financial and regulatory exposure | Fraud service receives no expected traffic; fraud, compliance, and risk teams lose a control and audit evidence | Compare payment volume with screening volume; alert on zero/low screening coverage; add an audit event for every decision | 10 | 3 | 10 | 300 | Put screening before scheme submission, fail closed on unavailable/invalid decisions, persist decision and policy version, and add a mandatory integration/contract test proving every eligible payment is screened. |
| FMEA-06 | Local balance, daily limit, or transaction history becomes incorrect | Account balance is debited locally after scheme acceptance, while ledger is a separate source; concurrent requests have no visible locking/atomic conditional debit; payments are not inserted into transactions | Customer may see spendable balance or limits that are too high/low and may be overdrawn or blocked incorrectly | Ledger, account API, statements, and finance reports disagree; support receives inconsistent data | Reconcile account balances and daily totals to ledger; alert on negative/limit anomalies and concurrent payment conflicts; monitor transaction freshness | 10 | 4 | 8 | 320 | Establish a single authoritative balance model, use database row locking/conditional updates and unique request constraints, record payment movements in the transaction model or consume ledger events, and reconcile continuously. |
| FMEA-07 | Cross-tenant or unauthorized data/action is exposed | Authorization depends on token claims and distributed `AccessPolicy` checks; any missing check, incorrect identity-service claim, or future endpoint can break the invariant | Confidential account, transaction, customer, risk-score, case, or payment data can be disclosed; unauthorized payment/closure is possible | Tenant isolation, legal, audit, and trust obligations are breached; security and incident teams must investigate | Deny/allow metrics by tenant and role; security audit logs; automated cross-tenant negative tests and authorization fuzzing; alert on unusual access patterns | 10 | 3 | 8 | 240 | Centralize resource authorization, validate tenant/role claims (including issuer, audience, expiry and key rotation), add endpoint inventory tests and database-level tenant constraints, and log actor/tenant/resource decisions without sensitive payloads. |
| FMEA-08 | JWT validation or identity-key rotation makes all traffic unauthorized, or accepts invalid claims | Stale/misconfigured public key, issuer/audience not enforced in visible config, token claim shape changes, or identity service outage/rotation | Customers and staff receive widespread 401/403 responses; payments and support work stop | All API consumers, identity operations, and customer support are affected simultaneously | Synthetic authenticated probes; 401/403 rate segmented by endpoint/issuer/key id; startup/config validation and key-rotation rehearsal | 9 | 3 | 4 | 108 | Configure and verify issuer/audience and key rotation, support a reliable JWKS/cache strategy, maintain synthetic probes for each role, and provide an identity/API incident runbook. |
| FMEA-09 | Database is unavailable, reset, or unsuitable for production durability | Visible configuration uses an in-memory H2 database with `create-drop` and fixture initialization; connection exhaustion, restart, or accidental production use loses state | Accounts, payments, cases, and closure requests disappear or become unavailable; payment status cannot be trusted | Dependent services see errors or stale data; recovery, compliance, and finance teams face data-loss response | DB connection/error/latency metrics; startup checks for database profile and persistence; backup-restore and restart tests; alert on unexpected schema initialization | 10 | 3 | 6 | 180 | Use a supported durable production database/profile, disable fixture loading and `create-drop` outside tests, apply migrations, backups/PITR, restore drills, and schema/data integrity checks. |
| FMEA-10 | Service becomes unavailable or latency exhausts callers | Synchronous calls to scheme (10s), ledger (5s), and fraud (2s); notification has no configured timeout; no circuit breakers, bulkheads, rate limits, or request correlation visible | Reads and payment submissions time out; customers retry and amplify load | API gateways, mobile/web clients, and upstream integrations experience cascading failures; on-call receives noisy incidents | RED metrics (rate, errors, duration), dependency latency/error dashboards, saturation (threads/connections), timeout and circuit-breaker alerts | 9 | 5 | 6 | 270 | Set bounded timeouts for every client, add circuit breakers/bulkheads and admission/rate limits, make payment submission asynchronous where appropriate, propagate correlation IDs, and load-test dependency degradation. |
| FMEA-11 | Notification failure is silent or misleading | Notification is best-effort and only logs a warning; the client has no explicit timeout/error policy; notification is sent after a ledger failure too | Customer may not learn a payment completed, or may be told a payment was sent when ledger state is `POSTING_FAILED` | Notification team cannot distinguish backlog from permanent failure; support handles avoidable contacts | Notification success/failure/latency metrics, durable delivery status and age, and alerts on failed or delayed messages | 6 | 6 | 8 | 288 | Publish notifications through a durable outbox after the authoritative payment outcome, retry with backoff/dead letter, give messages an event/payment status, and monitor delivery SLAs. |
| FMEA-12 | Closure requests accumulate without approval/rejection or account closure | API creates pending requests and lists them, but no approval/rejection endpoint or workflow is implemented; `Account.status` is never changed here | Customers wait indefinitely and cannot close accounts; duplicate requests are blocked | Approvers, support, retention, and compliance teams have an inbox with no completion path; operational backlog grows | Gauge pending requests by age/tenant; alert on SLA breaches and requests with no decision; audit workflow transitions | 8 | 5 | 5 | 200 | Implement the complete two-person workflow with authorization, explicit approve/reject transitions, atomic account closure and balance checks, audit trail, notifications, and an escalation for stale requests. |
| FMEA-13 | Wrong execution date around weekends, holidays, timezone or DST | Queue logic uses `WorkingDays` and `Europe/London` cron but there is no visible bank-holiday calendar or clock-skew control; scheme cutoff and local clock can disagree | Payment is delayed or released on an unexpected day; customer may miss a deadline | Scheme and operations receive instructions outside expected windows; reconciliation and customer support handle date disputes | Test boundary times, DST transitions and holidays; compare requested, scheduled and actual dates; alert on out-of-window scheme submissions | 8 | 4 | 6 | 192 | Use an explicit versioned business-calendar service/configuration, one authoritative timezone/clock, boundary tests, and an operational replay/re-date procedure. |
| FMEA-14 | Upstream or database errors leak as generic 5xx with no useful customer-safe contract | `ApiExceptionHandler` covers only selected application/validation exceptions; RestClient, JPA, and scheduling exceptions are not consistently mapped or correlated | Customers get opaque errors and may retry unsafe operations; support lacks a reference | API consumers cannot classify retryable vs permanent failures; on-call diagnosis takes longer | Track 5xx by exception/correlation ID; structured error-rate alerts; synthetic tests for each dependency failure mode | 7 | 5 | 6 | 210 | Add a global safe error handler with stable error codes and correlation IDs, preserve 401/403 semantics, classify retryability, and ensure sensitive exception details never reach responses. |
| FMEA-15 | Sensitive operational or customer data is overexposed through logs or responses | Payment payloads contain payee/account details; logging and exception behavior are not centrally redacted; staff-only data protection relies on controller/model discipline | Account identifiers, personal data, or risk information may be disclosed | Privacy, security, and compliance incident; downstream log/observability users gain unnecessary access | DLP/log scanning, access review, response schema tests, and alerts for sensitive-field patterns in logs | 9 | 3 | 7 | 189 | Define a data classification/redaction policy, never log full account/payment data, use structured allow-listed fields, encrypt and restrict logs, and add response-contract tests for customer vs staff views. |
| FMEA-16 | Concurrent closure/payment or payment/payment race produces an invalid state | Closure checks and payment checks are separate reads; account status and balance updates are not visibly protected by locking/versioning | A payment can be accepted while closure is being processed, or multiple payments can pass the same balance/limit check | Account, scheme, ledger, and closure records conflict; support and finance need repair | Conflict/version/lock-wait metrics; invariant checks for closed accounts with new payments and duplicate balance debits; concurrency tests | 9 | 4 | 7 | 252 | Use optimistic/pessimistic locking and transactional invariants around account state, balance, and limits; define whether in-flight payments block closure; test races under load. |

## Release priorities

Before release, the highest-value controls are the payment consistency and duplicate-prevention path (FMEA-01,
FMEA-02, FMEA-04, FMEA-06 and FMEA-16), the missing fraud gate (FMEA-05), and durable queued-payment processing
(FMEA-03). These are money-movement or control failures and should not rely on manual log inspection.

The minimum operational baseline should also include durable production persistence and recovery (FMEA-09), dependency
timeouts/circuit breaking (FMEA-10), end-to-end metrics and alerts, correlation IDs, synthetic authenticated checks,
and reconciliation dashboards. Validate the resulting controls with failure-injection tests: scheme timeout after
acceptance, ledger timeout after submission, process restart with due queue items, two concurrent payment requests,
fraud-service outage, database restore, and identity-key rotation.
