# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the implemented accounts-service API and its payment, account, customer, case,
closure, security, persistence, and scheduled-job responsibilities. It uses the service description in
`README.md`/`AGENTS.md` and the current Java implementation as of 4 November 2026. It is an operational
FMEA, so a row describes a credible failure of the service or one of its integrations, including failures
where an external system may have accepted work even though this service did not receive a response.

Scores are on a 1–10 scale:

- Severity (S): 1 = negligible inconvenience; 10 = customer money loss, duplicate payment, material
  regulatory/data-protection breach, or prolonged service-wide outage.
- Occurrence (O): 1 = rare/strongly controlled; 10 = expected or frequent under normal operation.
- Detection (D): 1 = automatically and immediately detected; 10 = unlikely to be detected before customer,
  reconciliation, or regulatory impact.

`RPN = S × O × D`. RPN is a prioritisation aid, not a substitute for judgement: any row with S >= 9 is
release-critical even if its RPN is moderate. Ratings describe the current implementation before the
recommended actions are completed.

## Failure modes

| F-01 | A payment is sent without fraud screening | `PaymentService` never calls the available `FraudScreeningClient`; there is no ACCEPT/REFER/REJECT decision in the create or queue flow | Fraudulent or sanctioned payments can reach the scheme and ledger. Customers may lose money; Fraud, Operations, Finance, and Compliance lose the policy control and its evidence | No screening record, metric, or gate; only downstream complaints/reconciliation may reveal it | 10 | 8 | 9 | **720** | Make screening a mandatory state transition before dispatch, persist the decision and timestamp, and fail closed on timeout/error. Add tests for ACCEPT, REFER, REJECT, and unavailable cases; alert on any send without an ACCEPT. |
| F-02 | REFER or REJECT is handled incorrectly | Fraud outcomes are not mapped into `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` and `REJECTED`; review workflow and reason handling are absent | REFER could be paid or lost instead of held; REJECT could be paid or expose the fraud reason. Fraud team cannot meet its four-hour review process and customers receive the wrong status | No fraud decision/status dashboard or held-payment queue exists | 10 | 7 | 9 | **630** | Implement explicit decision mapping: REFER => hold, no balance debit, review queue and `PAYMENT_UNDER_REVIEW`; REJECT => `REJECTED`/`FRAUD`, no customer disclosure. | F-03 | Scheme timeout loses or duplicates a payment | `SchemeTimeoutException` is not caught. ## Release priorities

Before release, treat F-01 through F-05, F-12, F-13, F-14, and F-16 as blocking for a production banking
service. In particular, payment acceptance must not be possible without a durable fraud decision and
idempotent scheme/ledger reconciliation. The next tranche should address F-06 through F-08 and F-10/F-11,
then implement the monitoring, notification, authentication, and resilience controls in F-09 and F-15
through F-18.

## Minimum evidence to retain

For every payment, retain an immutable audit trail linking the payment id, tenant, account id, fraud decision,
scheme reference, ledger reference, status transitions, execution date, actor, and timestamps. Operational
dashboards should expose payment counts by status and age, scheme/ledger unknowns and duplicates, overdue
queues, held-payment SLA, balance-import freshness/variance, statement completeness, notification failures,
authorization failures, dependency latency, and backup freshness. These signals are the practical detection
controls needed to reduce the D scores in this FMEA.
