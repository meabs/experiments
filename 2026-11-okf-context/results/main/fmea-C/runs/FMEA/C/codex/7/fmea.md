# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the API, persistence, scheduled work, security boundaries and the
scheme, fraud, ledger and notification integrations represented in this repository.
It uses the current implementation and the operating rules in `README.md` and
`AGENTS.md`; it is therefore also a release-readiness assessment, not just a list of
hypothetical infrastructure failures.

Scores use the usual 1–10 FMEA scale:

- **Severity (S):** 1 = negligible inconvenience, 10 = customer money loss,
  duplicate payment, material privacy breach or regulatory harm.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 = exceptional, 10 = expected or frequent.
- **Detection (D):** 1 = almost certain immediate detection, 10 = unlikely to be
  detected before customer, reconciliation or regulatory impact.
- **RPN:** `S × O × D`. RPNs are for prioritisation, not a substitute for judgement;
  any mode with S 9 or 10 should be treated as release-blocking until controlled.

Detection references operational controls that should exist; where the code has no
such control, that is called out explicitly.

## Failure mode analysis

| ID | Failure mode | Cause | Effect on customers and dependants | Detection | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F-01 | Payment is sent without fraud screening | `FraudScreeningClient` exists, but `PaymentService` never calls it. This also means `REFER` and `REJECT` are not handled. | A prohibited or suspicious payment can be sent. Fraud team never receives a hold, customers may lose money, and the bank breaches FRD-7; operations and compliance inherit the incident. | Monitor scheme submissions against fraud-screening decisions; reconcile every payment with a screening event. Current code has no such control, so this may only be found by audit or an incident. | 10 | 6 | 9 | **540** | Screen every payment before persistence/dispatch, fail closed on timeout/error, map `ACCEPT`, `REFER` to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, and `REJECT` to `REJECTED` with reason `FRAUD`. Add API and failure-path tests and a metric for unscreened submissions. |
| F-02 | A queued payment bypasses fraud screening | Queueing occurs in `PaymentService`, while `QueuedPaymentJob` calls `PaymentDispatcher` directly; neither path screens. | Payments created after cut-off or on weekends can be sent without the mandated check. The customer, fraud team, reconciliation and compliance teams see the same exposure as F-01, delayed until the next working day. | Alert when a scheme submission has no accepted screening record; report queued payments without a screening decision. | 10 | 5 | 9 | **450** | Screen before queueing and persist the decision, or screen immediately and hold/reject before creating an executable queue item. The job must refuse to dispatch unless a valid accepted decision exists. |
| F-03 | Scheme accepts a payment but the service reports failure and loses its local record | `SchemeTimeoutException` is not caught. A timeout can mean the scheme accepted the instruction, but the surrounding transaction rolls back the payment row. | The customer receives an error and may retry, while the scheme may pay the beneficiary. A retry can duplicate the payment; finance cannot match the scheme settlement to a local payment and complaints/operations must investigate. | Compare scheme settlement reports with local payments; alert on gateway timeouts and unmatched scheme references. Current API error rate alone cannot identify an accepted timeout. | 10 | 4 | 9 | **360** | Catch scheme timeouts, retain the payment with an `UNKNOWN`/reconciliation state, keep reference `P<id>`, query instruction status before retrying, and expose an operations exception. Never create a new scheme reference for a retry. |
| F-04 | Same payment is submitted or posted twice | No client idempotency key is accepted, and concurrent requests can both pass balance/daily-limit checks before either debit is committed. Re-dispatch also needs explicit idempotency handling. | Customer may be debited twice or the payee may receive two payments. Ledger, scheme settlement and finance reconciliation diverge; support and operations must reverse or recover funds. | Reconcile scheme and ledger by payment/reference, detect repeated payee instructions, and alert on concurrent duplicate requests. There is no uniqueness constraint or idempotency record in the current model. | 10 | 5 | 8 | **400** | Require an idempotency key and enforce a tenant/account/key uniqueness constraint; lock the account or use an atomic reservation for balance and daily limit; persist and reuse both scheme and ledger references. Test concurrent requests. |
| F-05 | Ledger posting times out after scheme submission | Ledger timeout is caught and status becomes `POSTING_FAILED`, but the ledger may have booked the posting. The implementation generates a ledger reference during dispatch and has no safe retry workflow. | The payment is sent but local/ledger state may be uncertain. An unsafe operational retry can debit twice; a missed retry leaves the customer, finance and the daily exceptions process with an unresolved break. | Daily exceptions report, ledger reconciliation by reference, and alerting on `POSTING_FAILED`. Verify both “booked” and “not booked” timeout cases. | 10 | 4 | 8 | **320** | Save a stable ledger reference before posting, make retries/query operations reuse it, add a ledger status query or controlled operations command, and make reconciliation closure auditable. Do not generate a new reference on retry. |
| F-06 | Customer is told a payment was sent when ledger posting failed | `PaymentDispatcher` always calls `notifySent` after a scheme submission, including `POSTING_FAILED`; there is no `PAYMENT_FAILED` branch. | Customer is given a misleading status while finance sees a scheme/ledger break. The customer may spend against an inaccurate balance or contact support believing the payment is complete. | Alert on `POSTING_FAILED` payments with `PAYMENT_SENT` notifications; compare notification template to final payment status. | 9 | 4 | 7 | **252** | Notify `PAYMENT_FAILED` (or “being reconciled”) for posting failures, make notification state derived from the final status, and add tests asserting no sent confirmation for an unresolved ledger post. |
| F-07 | Queued payment is not sent on its due date | The scheduler can be disabled, the process can be down at 06:00, or a dispatcher failure can abort the loop. There is no retry/backoff or visible job-run checkpoint. | Customer payment is late; payee, support, operations and finance have stale status. Past-due payments accumulate and may miss service-level expectations. | Metric for scheduler last-success timestamp, queue age/oldest due item, dispatch success rate and a paged alert for due items past execution date. | 8 | 4 | 7 | **224** | Make the job restart-safe and independently retryable, continue after one item fails, record attempts/errors, and produce the documented report of queued payments past due. Add a run/lag health indicator. |
| F-08 | Payment is refused or rejected on a bank holiday instead of queued | `WorkingDays` only excludes Saturday and Sunday. Bank holidays are not modelled, so the scheme can reject with `DT01`. | A valid customer payment is rejected or delayed unexpectedly; customers retry, support receives avoidable complaints, and scheme/finance reports contain avoidable rejects. | Monitor `DT01` by date and compare the calendar with the UK bank-holiday calendar. Current tests cover weekends/cut-off but not bank holidays. | 7 | 5 | 5 | **175** | Use a maintained UK bank-holiday calendar (with versioned updates), calculate the next working day consistently in API and job, and test all holiday boundaries. |
| F-09 | Stale customer balance permits an invalid payment or blocks a valid one | `accounts.balance` is a copy overwritten by the ledger at 02:00; payment checks use that copy, and the service has no just-in-time ledger balance check. | Customer can be overdrawn or a payment can be rejected despite funds in the ledger. Closure, customer support, finance reconciliation and complaints are affected. | Reconcile account balances after the ledger refresh and alert on negative/large deltas or payments exceeding ledger available funds. | 9 | 6 | 7 | **378** | Treat the ledger as authoritative for payment availability and closure zero-balance checks; add refresh freshness/version metadata and reconciliation alerts. Define the safe behaviour when the ledger is unavailable. |
| F-10 | Account closure is approved/processed without proving zero balance | The repository has request creation and listing but no approval/closure implementation, and no ledger balance check. The copied balance is not sufficient for exact closure. | A customer can be left with funds in a closed account, or closure cannot complete at all. Operations, finance, complaints and regulatory records require manual remediation. | Report pending requests, closed accounts with non-zero ledger balances, and requests older than the SLA. End-to-end closure approval is not currently exposed by the API. | 9 | 5 | 6 | **270** | Implement the approver transition with segregation of duties, idempotent state transitions and a live ledger zero-balance check; block closure on pending payments and retain an audit trail. |
| F-11 | Monthly statements and regulatory return are missing or incomplete | `Statement` is only a persistence entity; there is no statement generation, ledger closing-balance integration, endpoint or scheduled job in the service. | Customers cannot rely on statements; the regulator’s monthly return can be wrong or late, and finance/regulatory/complaints teams lack the required record. | Reconcile expected statement count by account/period, monitor the 1st/5th-working-day deadlines, and compare statement totals to ledger extracts. | 9 | 4 | 8 | **288** | Build an idempotent statement cut from ledger closing balances, with completeness and total checks, immutable/audited output, retrieval APIs, and a deadline alert. |
| F-12 | Cross-tenant or unauthorised data/action becomes visible | Access is implemented in several service/controller paths; a future endpoint or repository query can omit tenant filtering. Payment records include tenant data but `Payment.get` relies indirectly on account visibility, and `PaymentView` returns full payee sort code/account number. | Confidential account, transaction, case or payment data can cross bank-brand boundaries; an attacker can act on another customer’s account, causing privacy, fraud and regulatory impact. | Automated negative tests for every endpoint and tenant, authorization logs, access anomaly monitoring, and periodic security review. | 10 | 2 | 8 | **160** | Make tenant ownership a repository/query invariant, centralise authorization, add contract tests for every role/scope/tenant combination, and minimise payment response fields; review whether full payee details need to be returned. |
| F-13 | Notification failure delays or suppresses customer information | Notification client has no configured timeout shown in `application.yml`; although runtime failures are caught, an unbounded call can hold a request. Queued payments also have no queued notification path. | Payment API latency or thread exhaustion can reduce availability. Customers may not know a payment is queued, failed or under review, increasing duplicate submissions and support load. Payment itself should not be undone. | Track notification latency/failures, request thread saturation, and notification coverage by payment status. | 6 | 5 | 6 | **180** | Set connect/read timeouts, use bounded asynchronous best-effort delivery, instrument failures, and add `PAYMENT_QUEUED`, `PAYMENT_UNDER_REVIEW` and `PAYMENT_FAILED` notifications without including full account identifiers. |
| F-14 | Audit timestamps are inconsistent with the injected UK clock | `ClosureRequest` and `SupportCase` use `Instant.now()` directly, while payment logic uses the injected `Clock`. | Records can have inconsistent or non-reproducible ordering around cut-offs, deployments and tests; complaint, closure and regulatory timelines become harder to prove. | Compare event timestamps to service clock and inspect records around DST/cut-off boundaries; static analysis can find direct clock calls. | 5 | 5 | 6 | **150** | Inject `Clock` into all write services/entities, persist an explicit event/audit record, and test UK DST and boundary dates. |
| F-15 | Customer/payment data disappears on service restart | The checked-in datasource is an H2 in-memory database (`DB_CLOSE_DELAY=-1`) with fixture initialization. If used outside a test/demo profile, restart loses accounts, payments, cases and statements. | Customers lose history and payment state; retries can duplicate payments, reconciliation loses its local side, and complaints/regulatory records are unavailable. | Persistence smoke test across restart, database durability/backup monitoring, and reconcile row counts to core systems. Current config inspection would expose the risk, but runtime monitoring would not recover data. | 10 | 3 | 4 | **120** | Use a durable production database with migrations, backups, restore drills, and a separate test profile. Fail deployment validation if the in-memory datasource is enabled in production. |
| F-16 | API returns an internal error for an external failure without a recoverable business state | Scheme timeouts and other unexpected integration exceptions are not consistently mapped by `ApiExceptionHandler`; only selected domain exceptions become 4xx responses. | Customers see 500s with uncertain outcomes and may retry; support cannot distinguish safe retry from reconciliation. Availability and duplicate-payment risk increase. | Dashboard 5xx by dependency, distributed tracing with payment id/reference, and alert on external exception classes. | 8 | 5 | 7 | **280** | Define an explicit error/state contract for each dependency, persist payment state before/after every side effect, return a stable correlation id, and make retry guidance depend on known outcome rather than generic 500. |

## Release priorities

1. **Block release:** F-01/F-02 (fraud control), F-03/F-04 (scheme timeout and
   duplicate-payment protection), and F-05/F-06 (ledger uncertainty and misleading
   confirmation). These can create fraud, duplicate debits or an untraceable payment.
2. **Complete before relying on the service operationally:** F-07–F-11, especially
   durable scheduling, bank-holiday handling, ledger-authoritative balance checks and
   closure/statement workflows.
3. **Harden and verify continuously:** F-12–F-16 with tenant-negative tests,
   dependency timeouts, durable persistence, structured audit events and dependency
   reconciliation dashboards.

The most useful first operational dashboard should join payment id, tenant, scheme
reference, ledger reference, fraud decision, current status, execution date and
notification outcome. Without that correlation, the existing exception statuses are
not sufficient to prove whether money moved.
