# Accounts service FMEA

Review scope: the Spring Boot accounts API, its payment dispatch path, scheduled queue
processing, account/customer/case/closure APIs, and the four integrations described in
`README.md` and `AGENTS.md`. This is a design-and-code review for the next release; it
includes risks that are already visible in the implementation and risks in required
operational behaviour that is not yet implemented.

## Scoring

Scores are on a 1–10 scale. Severity (S) measures the worst credible effect, occurrence
(O) estimates how often the cause can arise under normal operation, and detection (D)
measures how likely the failure is to escape detection before customer or downstream
impact (10 = unlikely to be detected). The risk priority number is `S × O × D`.

RPNs are a prioritisation aid, not a substitute for judgement: any row involving an
unauthorised disclosure, duplicate debit, unscreened payment, or stranded customer funds
should be treated as release-blocking even if its RPN is not the highest.

## FMEA register

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers and dependent systems/teams | Detection | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| F-01 | Payment is sent without fraud screening | `FraudScreeningClient` exists but `PaymentService`/`PaymentDispatcher` never calls it | A prohibited or suspicious payment can be sent; fraud team loses its control point and the bank may breach FRD-7 | Fraud-team comparison of scheme submissions with screening records; post-incident review (currently no reliable real-time control) | 10 | 8 | 9 | **720** | Make screening a mandatory pre-dispatch state transition. Persist the decision. Treat `REFER` and timeout/error as `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`; send only `ACCEPT`; use `FRAUD` for `REJECT`. Add contract and API tests. |
| F-02 | A fraud `REFER` is rejected or sent | Decision handling is not wired, so there is no explicit hold state or review workflow | Customer either loses a legitimate payment or an intended hold is paid; fraud operations cannot review within four hours | Held-payment queue and screening-to-scheme reconciliation; currently absent | 10 | 6 | 9 | **540** | Add `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, customer review notification, fraud-team work queue, audit fields, and a controlled release/reject operation. Never expose the rejection reason to the customer. |
| F-03 | Scheme timeout leaves an accepted payment invisible or causes a duplicate on retry | `SchemeTimeoutException` is not caught; the transaction rolls back, although the gateway may have accepted `P{id}`. A later attempt may use a different reference | Customer may see a failed request while the payee is paid; retry can pay twice. Finance reconciliation and operations must investigate an untracked scheme settlement | Reconcile scheme settlement by payment/reference; alert on scheme items with no local payment; customer support reports | 10 | 5 | 9 | **450** | Persist the payment and immutable scheme reference before submission. On timeout, mark `SUBMITTED`/`UNKNOWN`, query `/v2/instructions/{reference}`, and retry only with the same reference. Add an exception report and idempotency tests for before/after-acceptance timeouts. |
| F-04 | Ledger posting is applied but local state is treated as failed, or a retry debits twice | Ledger timeout is ambiguous; the first `ledgerReference` is generated in the dispatcher and there is no status query/retry implementation | The scheme payment may settle while local/ledger views disagree; an unsafe retry can create a second debit and break finance reconciliation | Daily exceptions report, ledger-to-scheme reconciliation, duplicate-reference checks | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Generate and persist `ledgerReference` before the first call, retain it across all retries, make reposting idempotent, and add a reconciliation/status-check workflow for `POSTING_FAILED`. Never generate a new reference for recovery. |
| F-05 | Scheme payment is accepted but ledger posting fails | External calls are made sequentially; there is no durable outbox or compensating/reconciliation workflow. The local account is debited before the ledger result is known | Payee may receive funds while the customer balance, core ledger, and payment status disagree; operations and finance need manual remediation | `POSTING_FAILED` report plus 07:00 scheme/ledger reconciliation (the report/reconciliation is described but not implemented here) | 9 | 5 | 7 | **315** | Use an outbox/state machine with durable correlation IDs, retry safely, alert on age/SLA, and make reconciliation a monitored production process. Keep customer status explicit and do not silently mark complete. |
| F-06 | Two concurrent payments overdraw the account or exceed the daily limit | Balance and `paidToday` are checked from a copied balance without a row lock, optimistic version, or atomic reservation | Customer can spend the same funds twice; local balance and core ledger diverge; complaints and manual recovery follow | Ledger reconciliation and negative-balance/limit dashboards; race is not visible at request time | 9 | 6 | 8 | **432** | Reserve funds atomically under optimistic/pessimistic locking, enforce the daily limit in the same transaction, and test concurrent requests. Treat the ledger as the authority for remediation. |
| F-07 | Stale account copy authorises or rejects a payment incorrectly | `accounts.balance` is only a copy overwritten by the ledger at 02:00; payment checks do not consult or refresh the ledger | A payment may be refused despite funds, or accepted against funds already spent elsewhere; closure and customer balance views become misleading | 02:00 balance-load controls, ledger comparison, customer complaints; no freshness/version is exposed | 8 | 7 | 8 | **448** | Record balance-source timestamp/version, monitor freshness, reconcile continuously, and use a ledger balance/hold for decisions requiring exact funds (especially closure). Define behaviour when the copy is stale. |
| F-08 | Queued payment is sent late, on a bank holiday, or not sent | `WorkingDays` excludes weekends only; bank holidays are a known gap. The 06:00 scheduler has no visible misfire/retry/lock handling | Customer payment misses its promised working day or is rejected by the scheme with `DT01`; operations must find overdue queue items | Query for `QUEUED` payments past `executionDate`, scheduler heartbeat, scheme rejection metrics | 7 | 6 | 6 | **252** | Use an authoritative UK bank-holiday calendar and scheme working-day rules, persist the next valid date, make the job durable/idempotent, and alert on missed runs and overdue queue items. |
| F-09 | Payment at the 15:30 boundary is rejected instead of queued | Code queues only when `now.isAfter(cutoff)`; exactly 15:30 is dispatched even though the scheme cutoff is exclusive in practice | Customer receives an avoidable rejection and may miss the next working-day payment | `DT01` reason-code metric and boundary-time integration test | 7 | 4 | 6 | **168** | Define cutoff semantics with the scheme and use `>=` if 15:30 is no longer eligible. Add tests for 15:29:59, 15:30:00, DST transitions, weekends, and holidays. |
| F-10 | Queue job dispatches the same payment more than once or misses work after a restart | No claim/lease or state transition is made before dispatch; scheduler execution and transaction boundaries are not coordinated for multiple instances | Duplicate scheme submissions or delayed payments; a new scheme reference would be especially dangerous | Duplicate `P{id}`/scheme-reference metrics, overdue queue report, scheduler heartbeat | 9 | 4 | 8 | **288** | Add a database claim/lease, unique scheme-reference constraint, distributed scheduler coordination, and restart/misfire tests. Dispatch must be idempotent from the persisted payment state. |
| F-11 | Customer is told the wrong payment outcome | Notification is sent for both `COMPLETED` and `POSTING_FAILED` via `notifySent`, using the `PAYMENT_SENT` template; queued, held, and rejected paths do not have the specified notifications | Customer may believe a payment completed when ledger posting failed, or receives no explanation for a queued/held payment; support workload rises | Notification payload audits, status/template mismatch metric, customer complaints | 7 | 5 | 7 | **245** | Map templates to durable status transitions (`QUEUED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `SENT`, `FAILED`), send only after the relevant state is committed, and add notification contract tests. Keep notification failure best effort. |
| F-12 | Notification failure affects payment processing or silently loses customer communication | Client errors are caught only around the send, with logging but no durable delivery record or alert | Payment should remain intact, but customers may miss important status changes and operations cannot prove notification delivery | Structured logs and notification failure rate; current logging is WARN-only | 5 | 5 | 6 | **150** | Keep the non-blocking behaviour, but persist an outbox/delivery status, add bounded retry/dead-letter handling, alert on failures, and verify payloads contain only `accountEnding`. |
| F-13 | Sensitive data is disclosed across tenants or to an unauthorised role | Access is implemented in several controllers/services and depends on token `tenant`/roles; a future endpoint or repository query can omit the tenant predicate. Payment GET relies on account visibility but has no explicit payment-tenant check | Customer/account/payment, notes, or risk data can be exposed; regulatory breach and loss of trust | Cross-tenant negative API tests, access logs, security review, data-loss-prevention monitoring | 10 | 3 | 8 | **240** | Centralise tenant-scoped repository queries, explicitly check payment tenant and role/scope on every payment read, add deny-by-default endpoint tests for every role, and audit production access. |
| F-14 | Full account or payee data is exposed in an inappropriate channel | Payment API returns full payee sort code/account number; notification correctly uses the last four digits, but the DTO and operational logs have no field-level redaction policy | Excessive personal/payment data exposure to clients, support tooling, logs, or downstream consumers | API schema review, log scanning, DLP checks, penetration test | 8 | 4 | 7 | **224** | Define the minimum data for each audience, mask account identifiers in customer/support views where not required, prohibit logging request/response bodies, and add serialization/DLP tests. |
| F-15 | Account is closed with money still held, or closure is permanently stuck | Closure rules do not implement approval/closure completion or a ledger zero-balance check; the service balance is only a copy | Customer funds can be stranded, or the customer cannot complete closure; approvers, operations, and complaints teams lack a safe workflow | Pending-closure ageing report and ledger zero-balance check; closure completion monitoring is absent | 10 | 4 | 9 | **360** | Implement a second-person approval endpoint and an atomic close operation that checks the core ledger balance, outstanding payments, and pending exceptions. Alert on aged requests and make closure auditable. |
| F-16 | Statements and regulatory return are missing or wrong | `Statement` storage exists, but no statement generation, ledger closing-balance import, or monthly/regulatory job is present in the service | Customers receive incomplete statements; Finance may submit an incorrect or late return by the 5th working day | Statement completeness/reconciliation against ledger, monthly job heartbeat, regulatory pre-submission controls | 8 | 5 | 8 | **320** | Implement an idempotent monthly statement job from ledger closing balances, completeness checks, immutable statement versions, and a monitored regulatory export with a 5th-working-day SLA. |
| F-17 | Timestamps use the host clock or wrong timezone | `ClosureRequest` calls `Instant.now()` directly instead of the injected UK `Clock`; other flows depend on clock injection | Incorrect ordering/ageing and inconsistent audit evidence, especially in tests, DST changes, and operational reports | Timestamp anomaly checks and DST tests; static analysis can find direct clock calls | 6 | 4 | 7 | **168** | Inject `Clock` into every domain service/entity factory, standardise persisted timestamps to UTC instants, derive business dates in `Europe/London`, and add DST/clock-control tests. |
| F-18 | Service restart or deployment loses the system of record | Default configuration uses an in-memory H2 database with `create-drop`; payments, balances, cases, and closure requests are therefore non-durable outside a test setup | Customers lose history/statuses; reconciliation, complaints, fraud review, and regulatory records become impossible to complete | Startup/configuration check, persistence smoke test, backup/restore drill, data-loss alert | 10 | 2 | 10 | **200** | Use a supported durable production database and migrations, fail startup on an in-memory/non-production datasource, define backups and restore RPO/RTO, and run restart/restore tests. |
| F-19 | Legitimate API requests fail or latency spikes during dependency outage | Payment creation synchronously waits on scheme/ledger; timeout handling is incomplete, and there are no circuit breakers or bounded concurrency controls | Customers see 5xx/timeouts and may retry; retries amplify load and duplicate-payment risk; support and operations face an outage | Dependency latency/error dashboards, saturation metrics, synthetic payment probes | 8 | 6 | 6 | **288** | Separate acceptance from dispatch with a durable workflow, apply circuit breakers/bulkheads, return an explicit pending state, use bounded retries, and publish dependency SLO alerts. |
| F-20 | Authentication or authorisation is misconfigured | JWT claim mapping and method/URL rules are security-critical; a malformed/misissued token or unprotected endpoint can bypass tenant/role/scope checks | Unauthorised reads or payments, potentially across tenants; severe customer and regulatory impact | 401/403 rate anomalies, token/key rotation tests, endpoint inventory and penetration testing | 10 | 2 | 8 | **160** | Keep deny-by-default routing, validate issuer/audience/expiry and tenant claims, test every endpoint with missing/incorrect roles and scopes, and monitor key/config changes. |

## Release priorities

Before release, the minimum safety gate is to address F-01 through F-07, F-03/F-04
idempotency and reconciliation, F-08/F-10 queue durability, F-13/F-14 data exposure,
F-15 closure safety, and F-18 persistence. The highest-value operational controls are a
payment state machine with durable outbox, immutable scheme and ledger references,
reconciliation dashboards, overdue/failed-payment alerts, and concurrency tests against
the real database engine used in production.

The existing tests cover basic API access, normal dispatch, scheme rejection, a ledger
timeout, and weekday queue dispatch. They do not establish safety for fraud decisions,
scheme timeouts, concurrent spending, bank holidays, duplicate job execution, closure
against the ledger, statement generation, or persistence recovery; those scenarios should
be added to the release test plan.
