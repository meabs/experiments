# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its persistence, scheduled work, security boundary, and the fraud,
payment-scheme, ledger and notification integrations. It is based on the implementation and the service context in
`README.md`/`AGENTS.md`, including the known gaps described there. It is a release-readiness FMEA, so a failure mode
can be an existing defect or a credible operational failure of an existing component.

Scores use the conventional 1–10 scale:

- **Severity (S):** 1 = negligible/no material customer impact; 10 = duplicate or unauthorised movement of money,
  material data breach, or major regulatory/customer harm.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 = exceptional; 10 = expected or frequent under normal operation.
- **Detection (D):** 1 = almost certain to be detected immediately by an automated control; 10 = unlikely to be
  detected before customer, reconciliation, fraud or regulatory impact.
- **RPN:** `S × O × D`. Higher RPNs should be addressed first, but any S=10 item requires explicit release acceptance
  even when its RPN is not the highest.

The scores are relative engineering judgements, not measured incident rates. Detection assumes the controls currently
visible in the repository; where a recommendation adds a control, that is stated in the action.

| F-01 | Payment is sent without fraud screening | `PaymentService.create` never calls `FraudScreeningClient`, despite FRD-7. | A fraudulent payment can reach the scheme and ledger. Customer funds and the bank's fraud controls are exposed; the fraud team has no `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` item and complaints/regulatory evidence is incomplete. | No production guard or metric proves every payment has a decision; scheme and ledger reconciliation will not identify an unscreened payment. | 10 | 6 | 9 | **540** | Make screening a mandatory state transition before dispatch. Persist `ACCEPT`, `REFER` and `REJECT`; map `REFER` and timeout/error to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, map `REJECT` to `REJECTED`/`FRAUD`, and add tests/metrics that reject any dispatch without an accepted decision. |
| F-02 | Scheme timeout leaves an unknown payment outcome and can lose the local record | `SchemeTimeoutException` is not caught. The external call is inside the request transaction, so a timeout rolls back the payment row; the scheme may already have accepted it. | Customer sees an error and may retry; the scheme may pay once or twice. The service cannot show status, notify reliably, or reconcile the instruction, creating a payment sent but not posted/recorded exception. | Customer error logs and the scheme settlement report may disagree; there is no persisted `P` reference to query. | 10 | 4 | 9 | **360** | Persist `RECEIVED` before external submission, catch timeout, retain `P<id>`, set an explicit unknown/reconciliation status, and implement status-query reconciliation. Retry only with the same scheme reference; alert on unresolved items. |
| F-03 | Automatic/manual ledger recovery can debit twice or use an unrecoverable reference | `ledgerReference` is generated only during dispatch and there is no recovery operation. The ledger timeout may mean the debit was booked; a new reference would bypass ledger idempotency. | Scheme instruction may be sent while the local/ledger debit is missing or duplicated. Finance reconciliation and operations must investigate; customer balance and statements can be wrong. | `POSTING_FAILED` is visible in the payment row and expected in an exceptions report, but no code implements the report, retry workflow, or same-reference enforcement. | 10 | 5 | 7 | **350** | Generate and persist the ledger reference before posting; make repost/status recovery idempotent on that reference, with a bounded repair queue and audit trail. | F-04 | Payment sent but customer balance copy is not debited, or is debited inconsistently | Scheme submission precedes the local debit; timeout/error paths are not fully modelled, and the balance is only a copy of the ledger. | Customer may see an incorrect available balance and make later payments that overdraw the real account; finance and support see conflicting histories. | F-05 | Concurrent payments bypass balance or daily-limit checks | Checks and `account.debit` are ordinary read/modify/write operations with no locking or atomic conditional update. | F-06 | Fraud-referred or queued payment is not communicated and/or cannot progress | Queued creation only sets `QUEUED`; dispatcher only sends `PAYMENT_SENT`; no fraud hold workflow is implemented. Referred payments may never be reviewed/sent, and holiday payments can be submitted on an invalid date or remain queued. | F-07 | Duplicate dispatch of a queued payment | `QueuedPaymentJob` selects all due rows and dispatches them without a claim/lock/idempotent worker state. Overlapping instances or a retry after an uncertain scheme response can submit twice. | Same payment can be sent twice, potentially debiting twice; customer, scheme settlement and finance reconciliation are harmed. | The scheme may return `DUPL`, but a timeout or a new local payment state can hide the duplicate; settlement reconciliation detects it late. | 10 | 3 | 8 | **240** | Claim rows atomically (`QUEUED` → `DISPATCHING`) with lease/owner, enforce one worker per payment, and use the stable `P<id>` reference plus scheme status query on uncertain outcomes. |
| F-08 | Notification failure fails the payment, or notification contains sensitive account data | Notification client has no timeout configuration, while the dispatcher catches only runtime failures around the send. Future client behavior/configuration can block the transaction; notification payload construction exposes the account ending logic as a fragile substring boundary. | Payment may be operationally delayed if notification blocks; or a full account/sort code could leak if payload/template changes. Missing messages cause avoidable support contacts; a data leak is reportable. | Application logs only say notification failed; no delivery metric, payload redaction test, or outbound DLP check is evident. | 8 | 4 | 7 | **224** | Keep notification asynchronous/best effort with a bounded timeout and no transaction coupling. | F-09 | Payment accepted based on stale service balance | `accounts.balance` is a copy overwritten from the ledger at 02:00; payment validation uses it instead of an authoritative ledger/reservation check. | A payment can be accepted when the real balance is lower, or refused when it is higher. ## Highest-priority release actions

Before release, the blocking controls are F-01 (mandatory fail-closed fraud screening), F-02 (scheme timeout
reconciliation), F-03 (same-reference ledger recovery), F-05/F-09 (authoritative and concurrency-safe funds control),
and F-07/F-12 (single, observable queued-payment processing). F-11 should remain blocked until closure approval checks
the ledger directly. These controls protect money movement and cannot be substituted by a nightly reconciliation alone.

After those controls, implement the holiday calendar and customer review/queue notifications, then complete the
statement/regulatory and privacy/observability controls. Re-score the register using incident and reconciliation data
after the first release candidate; occurrence and detection are currently estimates because production telemetry was
not supplied.
