# Accounts service FMEA

**Review basis:** source and configuration in this repository, the documented operating model, and the API tests. This is a pre-release assessment, so rows marked as a current gap are risks in the implementation, not merely hypothetical failure scenarios. The assessment covers customer-facing accounts, payments out, cases, closure requests, security and the integrations on which these functions depend.

## Scoring

Scores use a 1–10 scale. Severity (S) is the effect if the failure occurs: 1 is negligible and 10 is a customer-money loss, duplicate payment, material confidentiality breach or regulatory breach. Occurrence (O) is the expected frequency under normal operation: 1 is exceptional and 10 is frequent or already present by design. Detection (D) is the likelihood the existing controls will **not** detect the failure before harm: 1 is almost certain to be detected and 10 is unlikely to be detected. The risk priority number is `S × O × D`; higher numbers should be addressed first. Scores are relative and should be recalibrated with production incident and volume data.

| F-01 | Payment is sent without fraud screening | `PaymentService.create` calls the dispatcher directly; the configured `FraudScreeningClient` is not wired into the payment flow. | A payment that should be held or rejected can leave the account. Fraud policy FRD-7 is breached; fraud team receives no review item and the customer may suffer loss. | Payment tests cover scheme and ledger but no screening assertion. Reconcile scheme submissions against screening decisions. | 10 | 10 | 9 | **900** | Make screening a mandatory step before queueing or dispatching. Persist the decision and timestamp; map `ACCEPT` to dispatch, `REFER`/timeout/error to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, and `REJECT` to `REJECTED` with reason `FRAUD`. Add fail-closed and bypass-resistance tests and an alert for any scheme reference without an accepted decision. |
| F-02 | Scheme timeout loses the local payment while the scheme may have accepted it | `SchemeTimeoutException` is not caught. The transaction rolls back after the external call, although the scheme may have processed the unique `P` + payment-id reference. | Customer can be paid out but see an error and no payment record; retry may create another instruction and reconciliation cannot associate the first instruction with a local payment. Complaints and operations must investigate an untracked outgoing payment. | Scheme has a status query, but the service does not use it. HTTP error rate and scheme settlement reconciliation are the only practical signals. | 10 | 4 | 9 | **360** | Persist `RECEIVED` before the external call and catch timeout into an explicit `SUBMISSION_UNKNOWN`/operational state (or an equivalent recoverable state). Query the scheme by the same reference before any retry; never generate a new scheme reference. Add reconciliation for accepted scheme instructions with no local terminal state. |
| F-03 | A payment is sent but ledger posting is absent or duplicated | Ledger timeout may be after booking; operations or future automation may retry with a new `UUID` instead of the saved `ledgerReference`. Conversely, a failure after scheme acceptance can leave the payment unposted. | Customer balance copy is debited, but the core ledger may not be; or the customer is debited twice. Finance reconciliation breaks and manual remediation is required. | `POSTING_FAILED` is recorded and included in the intended exceptions report; ledger is idempotent only when the same reference is reused. No automated status/repost workflow is present. | 10 | 5 | 7 | **350** | Allocate and persist `ledgerReference` before posting, make all reposts reuse it, and provide an idempotent reconciliation/retry worker with bounded retries and audit trail. Alert on scheme-settled versus ledger-posted mismatches and test timeout-before/after-booking cases. |
| F-04 | Same customer action pays twice | POST has no idempotency key or duplicate-request rule. A client retry after a timeout or double tap creates a second `Payment` and a second scheme reference. | Customer is charged twice; support, fraud and finance must unwind a real external payment. | Daily limit and balance-copy checks may stop some repeats, but are not an idempotency control and do not cover all races. | 10 | 6 | 8 | **480** | Require a client idempotency key scoped to tenant/account, persist a unique constraint and return the original payment for repeats. Define duplicate semantics for queued and uncertain payments and test retries at every external-call boundary. |
| F-05 | Concurrent payments overspend the account or exceed the daily limit | Balance and `paidToday` are read before any lock or atomic reservation; concurrent requests can pass both checks. The balance is only debited later in the dispatcher. | Multiple payments may be sent for more than the available funds or daily limit. The copied balance, ledger and finance reconciliation diverge. | API tests are sequential. Ledger reconciliation may find the discrepancy after money has moved. | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Use a database lock/atomic reservation per account, reserve funds and daily-limit capacity before dispatch, and make reservation release rules explicit for rejection/cancellation. Test parallel requests and multiple service instances. |
| F-06 | Incorrect balance authorizes a payment | `accounts.balance` is a daytime copy overwritten by the ledger at 02:00; it can be stale after another channel or a ledger event changes money. | Payment can be refused despite funds or sent despite insufficient funds. Customers see incorrect balances; ledger and finance must resolve breaks. | Nightly overwrite and the local balance check exist, but there is no just-in-time ledger funds check. | 9 | 7 | 8 | **504** | Treat the copy as advisory. Add a ledger authorization/reservation or current-balance check for payment and reconcile the copy continuously; expose freshness/last-sync time and alert when stale. |
| F-07 | Queued payment executes on the wrong day or is stranded | `WorkingDays` excludes only weekends, not bank holidays; the job runs only Monday–Friday and has no holiday calendar or missed-run recovery. | Payment may be submitted on a bank holiday and rejected with `DT01`, or remain queued until a later run. Customers miss expected payment timing; operations gets avoidable exceptions. | Execution date is stored and `runDue` queries overdue rows; there is no bank-holiday source or alert for overdue queue items. | 8 | 6 | 7 | **336** | Use the scheme/bank holiday calendar as a versioned dependency, calculate the next working day centrally, and run a catch-up worker on startup and every day. Alert on `QUEUED` items past execution date and test holidays, DST and missed scheduler runs. |
| F-08 | Queued payment is dispatched more than once or its state is overwritten | Multiple service instances/jobs can select the same `QUEUED` row; there is no claim/lease or state transition before dispatch. A second dispatch can receive `DUPL` or overwrite a completed payment with `REJECTED`; if it reaches ledger, a new generated ledger reference is unsafe. | Duplicate external instructions or a false rejected status; customer, fraud team and operations see contradictory history. | Scheme reference is deterministic (`P` + payment id), which protects the scheme from some duplicates; no row-claim metric or terminal-state invariant is enforced. | 9 | 4 | 8 | **288** | Atomically claim rows (`QUEUED → DISPATCHING`) with lease/owner, make dispatcher transitions monotonic, and refuse dispatch of terminal states. Keep the original scheme and ledger references on retry. |
| F-09 | External scheme refusal is recorded incorrectly or a valid payment is rejected | Unexpected scheme errors are not normalized; a timeout is not handled, and a `DUPL` from a concurrent/retried instruction can be treated as a new rejection. | Customer sees failure for a payment that may have been accepted; scheme settlement and local status disagree. Reason codes may be missing or misleading for support. | `SchemeRejectedException` records the supplied code; tests cover `AC01` only. | 8 | 4 | 7 | **224** | Define a complete response/error mapping, query status for timeout and duplicate outcomes, store raw correlation data safely, and make terminal transitions conditional on the current state. |
| F-10 | Payment is not delivered or status is misleading because notification fails | Notification is best effort and caught, but there is no durable outbox or delivery metric. `POSTING_FAILED` still calls `PAYMENT_SENT`; there is no queued-payment notification in the dispatcher/job. | Customer may not know a payment was sent or may be told it was sent when ledger posting failed. Support and complaints lack a reliable customer communication trail. | Log warning is the only control; notification failures are intentionally non-transactional. | 7 | 6 | 8 | **336** | Use an outbox after the payment state commit, retry notifications independently with deduplication, and use `PAYMENT_FAILED`, `PAYMENT_QUEUED` and `PAYMENT_UNDER_REVIEW` templates according to status. ## Release priorities

Before release, treat F-01, F-04 and F-06 as financial-control blockers. F-02, F-03, F-07, F-08, F-10, F-15 and F-16 should be release-gated with an owner, runbook and alert rather than relying on manual reconciliation. The first verification pack should include fraud fail-closed tests, idempotent retries after scheme/ledger timeouts, parallel-payment tests, holiday/DST tests, tenant/role matrix tests, durable-database startup checks, and scheme-versus-ledger reconciliation using the same payment and ledger references.

RPN is a prioritization aid, not a statement that a lower-scored security or compliance risk is acceptable. Any confirmed cross-tenant disclosure, unscreened payment, duplicate debit or data-loss event requires immediate incident handling regardless of its calculated score.
