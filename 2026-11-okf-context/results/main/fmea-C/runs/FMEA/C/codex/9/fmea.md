# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This assessment covers the customer API, payment dispatch and queue, account/transaction views, support cases,
statements, and closure requests, including the fraud, scheme, ledger, notification, identity, and database
dependencies. It is based on the current implementation and the operational rules in `README.md` and `AGENTS.md`.

Scores are current risk scores before the recommended action:

- **Severity (S):** 1 = negligible inconvenience; 10 = loss or duplication of customer money, material regulatory/data
  breach, or prolonged service outage.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 = exceptional; 10 = frequent or expected without a control.
- **Detection (D):** 1 = almost certain to detect immediately; 10 = unlikely to detect before customer, finance,
  fraud, or regulatory impact.
- **RPN = S × O × D.** RPN helps order work; a severity of 9 or 10 is release-critical even when its RPN is lower.

Detection controls listed below are the controls that exist or should be used for the current release. The FMEA should
be re-scored after actions are implemented and evidenced in tests, dashboards, and operational exercises.

## Failure-mode analysis

| F1 | A payment is sent without fraud screening. | `FraudScreeningClient` exists but `PaymentService` never calls it. | Fraudulent or sanctioned payments can reach the scheme and ledger. The customer may lose money; Fraud cannot review a hold; Operations and Finance must investigate; FRD-7 is breached. | Compare every accepted/submitted payment with a fraud-screening decision; alert on missing decision; review scheme submissions against fraud events. | 10 | 8 | 9 | **720** | Wire screening before queue/dispatch semantics are finalized. `ACCEPT` proceeds, `REFER` creates `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` without debit or send, `REJECT` records `FRAUD` without disclosing the reason, and timeout/error fails closed as hold. Add integration tests and a reconciliation metric. |
| F2 | Fraud screening fails open or has an unsafe response mapping. | Timeout/error handling is not implemented in the payment flow; an unexpected decision can also fail as an unhandled error. | An unavailable fraud service can permit unscreened payments, or legitimate payments can become unexplained 5xx failures. Fraud and customer support cannot distinguish hold from rejection. | Inject timeout, error, malformed, `ACCEPT`, `REFER`, and `REJECT` responses in tests; monitor screening timeout/error rate and submitted-without-decision count. | 10 | 6 | 8 | **480** | Enforce a two-second deadline and explicit fail-closed mapping to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`; persist the decision, timestamp, and reason category; make unknown responses safe and observable. |
| F3 | Scheme accepts a payment but the API transaction rolls back or returns an error. | `SchemeTimeoutException` is not caught. A timeout may follow scheme acceptance, so the local `Payment` and account debit can roll back while the scheme has taken reference `P<id>`. | Customer sees failure and may retry; the scheme can pay once or a new retry can pay twice. Finance sees settlement without a local posting; Operations loses the authoritative local record. | Reconcile scheme settlement/instruction status to local payments by scheme reference; alert on unknown scheme references and request errors around timeouts. | 10 | 5 | 10 | **500** | Catch the timeout, persist a durable `SUBMITTED`/`UNKNOWN` state and original `P<id>`, and query scheme status before any retry. | F4 | The ledger is booked but the service records `POSTING_FAILED`, or recovery posts twice. | Ledger timeout is ambiguous; the current code catches it but has no status query/retry workflow. | F5 | A scheme rejection or timeout is misclassified as a successful/failed payment. | Only `SchemeRejectedException` is handled; timeout is not. | F6 | Payment queued for the wrong date or rejected around a non-working day. | `WorkingDays` recognizes only Saturday/Sunday; it has no bank-holiday calendar. Cut-off logic uses `isAfter`, so exact 15:30 behavior is not explicitly tested. | A payment can be submitted on a bank holiday and receive `DT01`, or be delayed unexpectedly. Customers miss intended payment timing; scheme, support, and Operations handle avoidable failures. | Compare scheduled execution date with the bank-holiday calendar and scheme `DT01` responses; monitor queued payments past date and same-day submissions on holidays/cut-off boundary. | 8 | 7 | 7 | **392** | Use a versioned UK bank-holiday calendar and one shared cut-off policy (including exact boundary); test weekends, holidays, DST, and 15:29/15:30/15:31. |
| F7 | Queued payments are not dispatched, are dispatched late, or are dispatched more than once. | Single 06:00 scheduler can be down, delayed, or run concurrently on multiple instances; there is no claim/lock or retry state transition before dispatch. | Customer payment is late or duplicated at the scheme/ledger boundary. Operations’ past-due report grows; support and Finance investigate missing/duplicate settlement. | Scheduler heartbeat; count/age of `QUEUED` payments; alert on payments past `executionDate`; trace each dispatch attempt and detect repeated scheme/ledger references. | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Use a durable outbox/worker with row locking or a lease, idempotent dispatch state transitions, retries with the same scheme and ledger references, dead-letter/manual review, and a run-success metric. |
| F8 | Balance and daily-limit checks allow overspending or reject a valid payment. | The service uses a stale copy of the ledger balance; concurrent requests have no visible locking/version check; daily-limit aggregation is not an atomic reservation and includes all non-rejected/non-cancelled states. | Customers can overspend or be unfairly blocked. The ledger becomes the truth while the API shows a wrong balance; Finance, support, and complaints handle breaks. | Reconcile account balances and payment debits to the ledger; alert on negative/divergent balances and daily-limit breaches; concurrency/load tests. | 10 | 6 | 8 | **480** | Treat the ledger as authoritative for exact funds, add account optimistic locking/atomic debit reservation, make daily-limit reservation transactional, and reconcile after the 02:00 ledger refresh. |
| F9 | A payment is recorded or dispatched more than once from repeated client requests. | No customer idempotency key/unique constraint is enforced; every POST creates a new payment and therefore a new payment id and scheme reference. | Double payment and double debit are possible. The customer, payee, Finance reconciliation, and Operations all incur remediation. | Detect near-identical payments by account, amount, payee, and reference; reconcile duplicate scheme references/business references; inspect repeat POST rates. | 10 | 5 | 9 | **450** | Require and persist an idempotency key with a unique per-tenant constraint, return the original payment for a replay, and add duplicate-submit/concurrent-request tests. |
| F10 | Notification contains prohibited account data or its failure changes payment outcome. | Notification payload is currently last-four-only, but this is a high-impact invariant; integration behavior may change. ## Release priorities

Before release, the minimum risk-reduction set is F1–F4 (payment screening and ambiguous external outcomes), F7–F9
(durable/idempotent dispatch and balance reservation), F11 (tenant authorization regression tests), F13 (durable data
and restore evidence), and F16–F17 (closure and statement ownership/controls). F6 and F14 should be completed before
the next cut-off, bank-holiday, or DST transition. No payment should be considered operationally safe until scheme
timeouts and ledger timeouts can be reconciled without creating a new reference.

## Required operational evidence

At minimum, production readiness should include dashboards and alerts for API availability/5xx, authentication failures,
fraud screening outcomes and timeouts, scheme and ledger timeouts, `POSTING_FAILED`, `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, queued-past-date
payments, scheme/ledger reconciliation breaks, notification failures, statement completeness, closure SLA, and database
backup/restore status. Alerts should carry tenant/payment identifiers that are safe for operations and should not log
full account numbers, sort codes, or sensitive fraud rationale.
