# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API and its operational interactions with
identity, fraud screening, the payment scheme, the core ledger, notifications, the
database, and scheduled work. It is based on the current implementation and the
service context in `README.md`/`AGENTS.md`, including the fact that the account balance
is a customer-facing copy rather than the ledger's source of truth.

Scores use the usual FMEA convention:

- **Severity (S):** 1 = negligible inconvenience; 10 = material customer-money loss,
  serious data breach, or major regulatory impact.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 = rare and strongly controlled; 10 = frequent or inherent in
  normal operation.
- **Detection (D):** 1 = almost certain to be detected immediately; 10 = unlikely to
  be detected before customer, reconciliation, or regulatory impact.
- **RPN:** `S × O × D`. The scores are relative prioritisation aids, not estimates of
  financial loss. High RPN items should be addressed first; any S=10 item needs an
  explicit release decision even if its RPN is moderate.

## FMEA register

| ID | Failure mode | Cause / mechanism | Effect on customers and dependants | Current detection / controls | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |

| F-01 | Payment is sent without fraud screening | `FraudScreeningClient` exists, but `PaymentService` never calls it. `ACCEPT`, `REFER`, and `REJECT` are therefore not applied. | A fraudulent or sanctioned payment can be sent; referred payments are not held and rejected payments are not stopped. Fraud operations lose their review queue; the bank can breach FRD-7 and customers may lose money. | No application control or fraud decision is persisted. Scheme settlement and complaints may reveal it after the fact. | 10 | 6 | 10 | **600** | Make screening a mandatory, transactional gate before queueing or dispatch. Map `REFER` to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, `REJECT` to non-disclosing `REJECTED/FRAUD`, and all errors/timeouts to hold. Add metrics, alerts, and tests for every decision and the 2-second fail-closed rule. |
| F-02 | Scheme timeout loses the local payment or creates uncertainty about whether it was paid | The scheme timeout exception is not caught by `PaymentDispatcher`; the transaction rolls back. The scheme may have accepted `P` + payment id even though the API failed. | Customer sees an error and may retry; the original instruction may settle, creating duplicate payment. The service lacks a local record/status, and finance reconciliation and support cannot reliably match it. | Scheme settlement report and customer complaint can expose a mismatch; there is no status query or timeout-specific alert/reconciliation workflow. | 10 | 4 | 9 | **360** | Catch timeout, retain an immutable payment record with `SUBMITTED`/`UNKNOWN` (or an explicit exception status), and query `/v2/instructions/{reference}` before any retry. Reuse `P` + payment id; never generate a new scheme reference. Reconcile unknowns against the scheme report. |
| F-03 | Ledger posting is duplicated or permanently missed after timeout | A timeout may mean the ledger booked the entry. The dispatcher generates the ledger reference during dispatch and has no automatic or operational repost path in the service; a future retry could generate a new UUID. | Customer-facing balance may be debited while the ledger is not, or the customer is debited twice if operations retry with a new reference. Finance reconciliation breaks; operations must investigate exceptions. | `POSTING_FAILED` is logged and returned; daily exception reporting is described but not implemented here. The 07:00 reconciliation should detect settlement/posting mismatch. | 10 | 5 | 8 | **400** | Persist `ledgerReference` before posting, make it stable for the payment, and provide an idempotent retry/status workflow that always reuses it. Alert on `POSTING_FAILED`, reconcile scheme and ledger by payment reference, and record the ledger outcome separately from the customer status. |
| F-04 | Payment is queued for the wrong execution day | `WorkingDays` treats every Monday-Friday as working; it has no bank-holiday calendar. A payment on a bank holiday can be queued/sent on an invalid date or rejected by the scheme with `DT01`. | Payment is delayed or rejected unexpectedly. Customers see a promise the scheme cannot honour; operations and customer support handle avoidable exceptions. | Scheme rejection (`DT01`) and late queued-payment exception reports can reveal it; there is no bank-holiday-aware validation or alert before dispatch. | 7 | 5 | 7 | **245** | Use a centrally maintained UK bank-holiday calendar (with tested update/expiry monitoring), inject it into `WorkingDays`, and make execution-date selection and the 06:00 dispatch job use the same calendar. |
| F-05 | Queued payment is not dispatched, or is dispatched more than once | The single scheduled job may be disabled, fail, or run concurrently on multiple instances. It selects `QUEUED` rows without claiming/locking them; dispatch has no status/ownership guard. | Payment is late, or the same instruction is submitted repeatedly. Repeated scheme reference is safe only if every attempt retains `P` + payment id; downstream support sees stale or confusing status. | `QUEUED` payments past their date are intended for the operations report, but no metric/claiming mechanism is present. Scheme duplicate responses and settlement reconciliation are secondary detection. | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Make dispatch durable: use a claimed/lease state and database locking or an outbox/worker, make the job idempotent, expose backlog/age metrics, alert on missed runs, and ensure duplicate attempts retain the same scheme and ledger references. |
| F-06 | Account balance copy permits an incorrect payment decision or becomes inconsistent | Balance is checked and debited locally, while the ledger is authoritative and overwrites it at 02:00. Concurrent payment requests have no visible account locking/version check; a stale copy can pass insufficient-funds or limit checks. | Payment can be accepted when funds are unavailable, rejected when funds exist, or the copy can go negative/inaccurate. Customers see wrong balances; ledger reconciliation and operations absorb breaks. | Nightly ledger overwrite is the only documented correction. Payment/scheme/ledger reconciliation can detect some cases after settlement, not at authorisation time. | 9 | 7 | 8 | **504** | Use ledger-authoritative funds/holds for payment authorisation, or implement optimistic/pessimistic account concurrency control and a durable reservation. Reconcile after every posting and alert on negative or unexplained balance deltas. |
| F-07 | Duplicate customer submission creates duplicate payments | `POST /payments` has no client idempotency key or duplicate-request check. A retry after latency/error creates another payment; daily-limit and balance checks are not an atomic reservation. | Payee may receive two payments and the customer is debited twice. Support, finance, and complaints must unwind the duplicate; a scheme reference per payment does not protect against two payment rows. | Daily-limit checks, scheme settlement, and reconciliation may reveal duplicates; there is no request-id/idempotency metric or user-visible duplicate warning. | 9 | 6 | 8 | **432** | Require an idempotency key scoped to tenant/account, enforce a database uniqueness constraint, make the complete create/dispatch path safely retryable, and atomically reserve amount against balance and daily limit. |
| F-08 | Payment is marked/treated as sent when ledger posting failed | The dispatcher submits to the scheme, debits the local copy, marks `POSTING_FAILED`, then still calls `notifySent`; notification template is always `PAYMENT_SENT`. | Customer is told the payment was sent even though the money posting is unresolved. Operations may miss urgency; complaints and reconciliation are harder because customer communication contradicts the exception state. | `POSTING_FAILED` log/status and finance reconciliation; notification content itself is not correlated with ledger outcome. | 8 | 5 | 7 | **280** | Notify `PAYMENT_FAILED`/pending-posting for `POSTING_FAILED`, suppress “sent” until the defined completion condition, include payment id and outcome in structured events, and alert on notification/status mismatches. |
| F-09 | Payment request transaction rolls back after an external side effect | Scheme and ledger calls occur inside the Spring transaction. An unexpected runtime error, database failure, or notification-related path can roll back the local row after the external system has accepted/booked. | Customer and internal records lose the payment while an external debit or instruction remains. Reconciliation and customer support cannot explain the money movement. | External settlement/ledger reports may show an orphan; there is no transactional outbox or orphan-scan described. | 10 | 3 | 9 | **270** | Persist payment and durable references before external calls; use an outbox/state machine with recovery workers. Treat all external calls as at-least-once and reconcile orphan scheme/ledger records back to local payments. |
| F-10 | Notification failure causes missing or misleading customer communication | Notifications are best effort and caught, which is correct for payment completion, but there is no durable event/retry or delivery monitoring. For queued/review/rejected/failed paths, the corresponding templates are not implemented in the dispatcher. | Customer may not know a payment completed, is queued, or is held/rejected; support contact increases and customers may retry. | F-11 | Cross-tenant or unauthorised data/action access | Resource access relies on token `tenant`, role, scope, ownership, and assignment checks. | F-12 | Sensitive account/payment data is exposed in API or logs | `PaymentView` returns payee sort code and full payee account number; payment data is stored in the service. | F-13 | Account closure is approved/processed without an authoritative zero-balance check, or cannot be completed | The repository contains closure requests only; no approval/decision endpoint or close operation is present. | F-16 | Identity validation or key/configuration failure blocks valid users or admits invalid tokens | JWT verification depends on the configured public key; rotation, issuer/audience validation, clock skew, or misconfigured tenant/role/scope claims can fail. Payment, support, fraud review, and operations are disrupted. | Authentication tests cover anonymous and expired tokens; health checks and auth failure rates can detect outages, but claim/key rotation tests are not evident. | 9 | 3 | 7 | **189** | Validate issuer, audience, expiry and key rotation against the identity platform; monitor 401/403 spikes and key freshness, test rotation/failover, and fail closed on invalid tenant/role claims. |
| F-17 | Time-zone or clock error applies the wrong cut-off, date, or audit time | Payment logic uses the injected UK clock, but closure creation uses `Instant.now()` and scheduled execution depends on host/configured UK zone. DST boundary, clock drift, or inconsistent clocks can change working-day/cut-off decisions. | Payment is sent late/early or delayed to another day; audit chronology and closure decisions become unreliable. Customers and scheme operations receive incorrect execution promises. | Unit/API tests use `MutableClock` for payments; there is no visible NTP/DST monitoring or clock-use rule enforcement across all domains. | 7 | 4 | 7 | **196** | Centralise all business time behind the injected clock, monitor NTP drift, test DST/cut-off/bank-holiday boundaries, and include effective timestamp/time zone in operational events. |

## Release priorities

Before release, the minimum safety gate should include F-01, F-02, F-03, F-06, F-07,
F-13, and F-18. Together they cover unscreened money movement, ambiguous external
outcomes, ledger idempotency, stale-balance authorisation, duplicate customer retries,
unsafe closure, and concurrent writes. F-04/F-05 and F-14/F-15 then provide the
operational resilience needed to run the service reliably after launch.

The most important monitoring set is a reconciled payment state view keyed by payment
id, scheme reference, and ledger reference. It should expose unknown/time-out,
`POSTING_FAILED`, overdue `QUEUED`, duplicate, and balance-invariant events, with
alerts routed to operations and finance. Logs must contain correlation and reference
ids but no full account numbers, sort codes, credentials, or fraud details exposed to
customers.
