# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its payment dispatcher and queued-payment job, the account/closure and
statement data paths, authentication and tenant isolation, and the scheme, fraud, ledger and notification integrations.
It is based on the current implementation and the system rules in `README.md` and `AGENTS.md`, as at 4 November 2026.

Scores use the usual FMEA scale: severity (S) is the impact if the failure reaches a customer or dependent; occurrence
(O) is the expected likelihood under normal operation; and detection (D) is how unlikely the failure is to be detected
before harm (1 = very easy/near-certain detection, 10 = very difficult). RPN = S × O × D. These are relative release
triage scores, not actuarial probabilities. High priority is RPN >= 300 or any severity 10 item.

## Failure-mode analysis

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers and dependent systems/teams | Current detection | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| PM-01 | A payment is sent without fraud screening | `PaymentService.create` never calls `FraudScreeningClient`, although FRD-7 requires screening before dispatch | A fraudulent or sanctioned payment can reach the scheme and ledger. Fraud operations lose the review trail; the bank has a policy breach and customer remediation exposure | Scheme and ledger settlement reconciliation may show the payment, but there is no screening decision or alert | 10 | 8 | 9 | **720** | Wire screening before queue/dispatch as required by policy; enforce a 2-second timeout and fail closed. Persist the decision and audit event. Block dispatch unless an `ACCEPT` decision exists. |
| PM-02 | `REFER` is rejected, or an unavailable fraud service causes an unsafe outcome | No fraud decision handling exists in the payment flow; the client exception is not mapped to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` | Referred payments may be sent, or customers receive a generic failure and retry; fraud team has no held-payment work item. A timeout must never result in an unscreened send | Application error logs and customer 5xx responses; no held-payment metric or alert | 10 | 7 | 8 | **560** | Map `REFER`, timeout and all screening errors to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`; do not debit or submit; notify with `PAYMENT_UNDER_REVIEW`; create an operations/fraud queue with an SLA alert. Map `REJECT` to `REJECTED` with reason `FRAUD` without revealing the reason to the customer. |
| PM-03 | Client retry creates a duplicate payment | There is no request idempotency key or uniqueness constraint; a retry creates a new payment and a new scheme reference (`P` + new id) | Customers can pay the same bill twice; scheme settlement, ledger posting and reconciliation contain two valid payments | Customer complaint or daily scheme/ledger reconciliation; no immediate duplicate warning | 9 | 6 | 9 | **486** | Require an idempotency key scoped to tenant/customer/account, persist it with a unique constraint, and return the original result for a replay. Keep the scheme reference stable. |
| PM-04 | Scheme timeout loses the local payment record even though the scheme accepted it | `SchemeTimeoutException` is not caught; the enclosing transaction rolls back after a 10-second ambiguous external call | Customer sees an error and may retry; the scheme may pay an instruction with no local status or ledger posting. Finance sees a settlement without a matching local payment; complaints and operations cannot reliably remediate | Settlement reconciliation can find an unknown `P{id}` only if the scheme report is checked; current flow has no status-query or timeout queue | 10 | 6 | 10 | **600** | Catch timeout, commit a durable `SUBMITTED`/scheme-unknown state and original `P{id}`, then reconcile with the scheme status endpoint. Retry status queries and resubmit only with the same reference. Alert unresolved instructions. |
| PM-05 | A ledger timeout is retried with a different idempotency reference | `PaymentDispatcher` generates a new UUID every dispatch; the original `ledgerReference` is not reused | A timed-out ledger call may already have booked. An operations retry can debit twice; if it did not book, the payment may remain unpaid while the scheme has sent it. Finance reconciliation breaks | `POSTING_FAILED` status and daily exception report, but the report cannot prevent an unsafe manual retry; ledger reconciliation may expose a double debit | 10 | 5 | 9 | **450** | Generate and persist `ledgerReference` before the first post; reuse it for every retry. Add a ledger status/query or controlled replay operation and reconcile before retrying. |
| PM-06 | Scheme payment and ledger posting become inconsistent | Scheme submission succeeds, then ledger fails; the code still debits the local copy and marks `POSTING_FAILED`; scheme rejection is handled, but there is no compensating/reconciliation workflow | Payee may receive funds while the customer's core account is not debited, or the customer is debited locally but not in the ledger. Operations must investigate; customer balance/status is misleading | `POSTING_FAILED` is logged and persisted; 07:00 settlement reconciliation detects a missing ledger posting only if it compares both feeds | 10 | 6 | 6 | **360** | Treat this as a durable exception state with a daily report, ledger status/replay using the same reference, and alerting/escalation. Do not report `PAYMENT_SENT` as completed until the posting policy is satisfied; expose clear customer status. |
| PM-07 | Two payments overdraw the account or exceed the daily limit | Balance and `paidToday` are read from a stale copy without row locking/optimistic versioning; concurrent requests can both pass checks | Customer can overspend; ledger rejects or accepts an overdraft contrary to policy; support and finance see inconsistent balance and limit usage | Overnight ledger overwrite or reconciliation; no transaction-level concurrency alarm | 9 | 6 | 8 | **432** | Lock/version the account and make the balance/limit reservation atomic. Use the ledger as the authoritative funds check for exact decisions and reconcile rejected/overdrawn attempts. |
| PM-08 | Payment is queued for the wrong day or sent on a bank holiday | `WorkingDays` excludes only Saturday/Sunday; bank holidays are not configured. The 15:30 boundary uses `isAfter`, so exactly 15:30 is not queued | Scheme can reject with `DT01`, or a payment promised for the next working day is delayed/rejected. Customers, scheme operations and support receive avoidable exceptions | Scheme rejection code and customer complaint; no bank-holiday calendar validation or cut-off boundary alert | 8 | 5 | 7 | **280** | Use a versioned UK bank-holiday calendar shared with the scheme, define the cut-off inclusively, and test DST, holidays and boundary times. Queue rather than reject. |
| PM-09 | Queued payments are not dispatched, or are dispatched twice | The single 06:00 scheduler has no misfire/retry/lock control; it runs only Monday-Friday and has no job checkpoint. Multiple service instances can claim the same row | Payment misses its execution date; or concurrent dispatches produce duplicate/incorrect local state. Customers and scheme operations see late or contradictory statuses | Operations report is specified for overdue `QUEUED` items, but no implementation/metric is visible; scheme/ledger reconciliation is the fallback | 9 | 5 | 8 | **360** | Use a durable job/claim state with row locking or a distributed scheduler lock, make dispatch idempotent, retry with backoff, and alert on overdue queued payments and job heartbeat/misfire. |
| PM-10 | Customer is not told a payment was queued or held, or receives a misleading sent notice | Queue creation has no notification call; dispatcher always sends `PAYMENT_SENT`, including `POSTING_FAILED`; fraud-review notification is absent | Customer may think a payment was sent when it is pending/failed, miss a bill deadline, or retry and duplicate it. Contact centre and complaints workload increases | Customer reports and payment-status queries; notification failures are only WARN logs | 7 | 6 | 8 | **336** | Emit outbox-backed `PAYMENT_QUEUED`, `PAYMENT_UNDER_REVIEW` and `PAYMENT_FAILED` events. Keep notifications best effort and include only `accountEnding`; monitor delivery failures without coupling them to money movement. |
| PM-11 | A notification call stalls or fails during payment processing | HTTP notification client has no configured connect/read timeout; notification is synchronous, and only runtime failures after return are caught | A completed payment request can hang or time out at the API despite money movement; repeated client retries increase duplicate risk. Notification outage consumes request threads | Application latency and WARN logs; no notification timeout or delivery metric | 7 | 5 | 8 | **280** | Configure bounded timeouts/circuit breaking and an outbox/async sender. Make payment commit independent of notification delivery and provide delivery monitoring/replay. |
| PM-12 | Account is closed while funds remain, or closure cannot complete correctly | The code shown only creates and lists closure requests; there is no approval/close path, and no ledger balance check. The account copy is not authoritative | A customer’s funds can be stranded, or the closure process is permanently stuck. Back office, finance and complaints teams cannot complete the lifecycle safely | Pending closure list and manual operations review; no automated ledger-zero check or ageing alert | 10 | 4 | 9 | **360** | Implement a tenant-scoped approve/reject workflow with separation of duties, check the ledger balance (not the copied balance) immediately before closure, block new payments atomically, and alert on aged pending requests. |
| PM-13 | Account/payment data disappears or reverts after service restart | Production configuration uses an in-memory H2 database (`jdbc:h2:mem` with `create-drop`) and fixture initialization | All account, payment, case, closure and statement records can be lost on restart; customers see missing history and finance/regulatory records become incomplete | Restart smoke check or customer complaint; no durable-store health/continuity test in the service | 10 | 3 | 10 | **300** | Use the production durable database and migrations, backups/PITR, restore drills, and startup checks that prevent serving traffic with an empty/unmigrated store. Keep fixtures test-only. |
| PM-14 | Payment or account data is disclosed across tenants or to an unauthorized role | A future endpoint/query may trust IDs without applying `AccessPolicy`; payment visibility checks the account but payment data includes payee account details. Tenant isolation is application-enforced rather than database-enforced | Cross-bank disclosure of balances, transaction history or payee details; reportable data-protection incident and loss of trust | Some API tests cover another customer and tenant rules are centralized, but there is no stated automated query-level tenant invariant or audit alert | 10 | 3 | 8 | **240** | Add authorization tests for every endpoint and role/tenant combination, enforce tenant predicates/row-level controls where possible, audit sensitive reads, and alert on anomalous cross-tenant access. Keep notifications to last four digits only. |
| PM-15 | Authentication/access control is unavailable or misconfigured | Public-key rotation, issuer/claim validation, scope mapping or platform identity outage is not operationally covered in this service; all authorization depends on the token converter and config | Valid customers are locked out, or a bad trust/configuration could admit an invalid token. Payments and support operations stop or become unsafe | 401/403 rates, health endpoint and identity-provider monitoring; health is public but does not prove a valid authorization path | 9 | 3 | 7 | **189** | Validate issuer/audience/expiry and key rotation in configuration; alert on 401/403 spikes, test fail-closed behavior and key rollover, and include identity dependency status in readiness/operations runbooks. |
| PM-16 | Clock or time-zone error misdates payments and time-based controls | Most payment paths use the injected UK `Clock`, but closure/case timestamps use `Instant.now`; scheduler and cutoff depend on deployment/time-zone configuration | Payments can queue or execute on the wrong date; statements, audit trails and regulatory deadlines can be wrong. Support cannot reliably reconstruct events | Timestamp comparisons and reconciliation; no clock-skew/DST alert or complete injected-clock rule | 8 | 4 | 7 | **224** | Inject the clock into every timestamp-producing component, standardize UTC storage plus UK presentation, and test DST, cutoff, weekend and bank-holiday transitions. |
| PM-17 | Monthly statements or regulatory return are incomplete or wrong | Statement is only a data entity in the current code; no generation/scheduling path is visible, while regulatory reporting depends on ledger closing balances and the 5th-working-day deadline | Customers receive missing/incorrect statements; finance submits an incorrect or late regulatory return; complaints and regulator exposure follow | Finance/regulatory reconciliation and manual deadline checks; no statement-generation job, completeness metric or deadline alert is visible | 9 | 4 | 8 | **288** | Implement an idempotent statement close from authoritative ledger balances, validate every account/period, produce a reconciliation report, and alert well before the 5th working day. |
| PM-18 | Invalid or pathological payment input causes incorrect processing or denial of service | Validation checks format/amount but does not establish payee ownership/confirmation, and there are no explicit rate limits or payload/request controls at the API layer | Misdirected payments, excessive requests, resource exhaustion, and increased duplicate attempts. Support and fraud teams receive poor-quality signals | 400 responses and infrastructure metrics; no explicit rate-limit, anomaly or payee-change monitoring | 8 | 5 | 7 | **280** | Add rate limits and abuse detection, strict request-size/time limits, payee confirmation/allow-list controls appropriate to policy, and audit all payment submissions and retries. |

## Release priorities

Before release, the minimum safety gate should address PM-01/02 (fraud fail-closed), PM-04/05 (ambiguous external
outcomes and stable idempotency), PM-07 (atomic funds/limit control), and PM-13 (durable persistence). PM-08/09/10
should be completed or have explicit operational ownership and alerts before enabling scheduled payments. PM-12 and PM-17
need a verified business process and reconciliation evidence because their impact is high even though the affected code
paths are less frequently exercised.

The test suite should add failure-injection API/integration tests for scheme timeout after acceptance, ledger timeout
before and after booking, fraud `ACCEPT`/`REFER`/`REJECT`/timeout, duplicate request replay, concurrent payments,
bank holidays, scheduler misfires, notification outages, restart/restore, authorization across tenants, and DST/cutoff
boundaries. Production monitoring should at minimum expose payment counts by status, overdue queue age, scheme/ledger
reconciliation breaks, fraud decision latency, notification failures, job heartbeat, and 401/403/5xx rates.
