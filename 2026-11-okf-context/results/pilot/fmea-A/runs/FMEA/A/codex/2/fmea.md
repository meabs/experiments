# Accounts service FMEA

## Scope and scoring

This review covers the Spring Boot accounts API, its H2/JPA persistence, bearer-token
security, payment workflow, scheduled work, and the scheme, fraud, ledger, and
notification integrations. It is based on the implementation and configuration in
this repository, not on production telemetry. The scores are therefore initial
operational estimates and should be recalibrated from incident and volume data.

Scores use a 1–10 scale:

- **Severity (S):** 1 = negligible inconvenience; 10 = material financial loss,
  regulatory/security breach, or prolonged loss of a core banking capability.
- **Occurrence (O):** 1 = exceptional; 10 = frequent or expected under normal
  operating conditions.
- **Detection (D):** 1 = almost certain to be detected immediately by an automated
  control; 10 = unlikely to be detected before customers or dependent teams report it.

The risk priority number is `RPN = S × O × D`. Prioritise remediation at RPN ≥ 200,
and prioritise any S = 9 or 10 regardless of RPN. “Current detection” describes what
the service currently exposes; where that is insufficient, the recommended action
includes the alert or reconciliation needed.

## Failure-mode analysis

| ID | Failure mode | Likely cause | Effect on customers and dependent systems/teams | Current detection | S | O | D | RPN | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| FMEA-01 | A payment is accepted by the scheme but is not durably recorded, or is submitted twice. | The scheme call is made inside the request transaction; a timeout, crash, or response loss can roll back the database while the external instruction may still exist. There is no idempotency/reconciliation workflow beyond the generated payment id. | Customer sees a 5xx or retries; the payee can receive a duplicate or the payment can be lost. Operations, scheme support, ledger and complaints teams must investigate a disputed movement. | HTTP 5xx and application logs; no durable outbox, scheme-status reconciliation, or duplicate alert. | 10 | 4 | 8 | 320 | Use a transactional outbox with an idempotency key (`P{id}`), explicit unknown/submission-pending state, retry policy, and scheme acknowledgement/status reconciliation. Alert on ambiguous and duplicate instructions. |
| FMEA-02 | Ledger posting times out after the scheme accepts the payment. | `PaymentDispatcher` debits the account and marks `POSTING_FAILED` when the ledger client times out, but does not retry or reconcile the posting; a timeout can also mean the ledger accepted it. | Funds may be sent but unavailable in the core ledger, or a later manual retry may double-post. The customer balance and core ledger can diverge; finance and operations need manual repair. | `POSTING_FAILED` status and a warning log; no alert, retry queue, or ledger-reference reconciliation. | 10 | 4 | 7 | 280 | Make ledger posting idempotent by `ledgerReference`, persist an outbox/job with bounded retries, distinguish unknown from rejected, and reconcile every non-terminal payment against the ledger before manual retry. |
| FMEA-03 | Local account balance becomes inconsistent with the core ledger. | The service maintains and debits a mutable local `Account.balance`, while the ledger is another system of record; there is no atomic cross-system transaction or balance refresh. | Customers see an incorrect balance and may be incorrectly blocked or allowed to pay. Limit checks, support decisions, statements and downstream financial reporting become unreliable. | Customer/API comparisons and later ledger investigations; no continuous balance-drift metric is visible in the service. | 9 | 5 | 8 | 360 | Define the authoritative balance, avoid using a cached balance for authorization unless it is versioned, add ledger-to-service reconciliation and drift alerts, and make payment authorization consume a consistent ledger snapshot. |
| FMEA-04 | Concurrent payments bypass funds or daily-limit checks. | `PaymentService` reads balance and today’s payments before dispatch, but there is no row lock, optimistic version check, database constraint, or atomic reservation. Two requests can pass the checks concurrently. | Customers can overspend or exceed the daily limit; scheme instructions and ledger postings may require reversal. Risk, finance and support teams receive inconsistent records. | Payment/ledger reconciliation and customer complaints; existing tests exercise sequential requests only. | 9 | 5 | 8 | 360 | Atomically reserve funds and daily-limit capacity with a version/row lock or ledger authorization; add unique/request idempotency constraints and concurrency tests. |
| FMEA-05 | Fraud screening is skipped for every payment. | Fraud integration classes and configuration exist, but `PaymentService`/`PaymentDispatcher` never call `FraudScreeningClient`. | Fraudulent payments can proceed without accept/refer/reject policy enforcement, causing customer loss, scheme exposure, regulatory risk and manual fraud-team workload. | No runtime signal: the absence of a call is silent. Detect through code/config review, integration-call dashboards, and a control requiring one screening decision per payment. | 10 | 8 | 10 | 800 | Insert screening before scheme submission; fail closed on unavailable/invalid results, persist the decision and reason, define REFER handling, and alert when screening-call/payment counts diverge. |
| FMEA-06 | Queued payments are never dispatched on their execution date. | `QueuedPaymentJob` has `@Scheduled`, but the application has no `@EnableScheduling`; no other dispatcher is present. | Weekend/cutoff payments remain `QUEUED`, so customers and payees experience indefinite or late settlement. Operations and scheme/ledger teams see missing expected instructions. | Payment status remains queued; no age/SLA alert or job heartbeat exists. | 8 | 7 | 9 | 504 | Enable scheduling explicitly, add a startup/heartbeat metric and a queue-age dashboard, alert on due queued payments, and provide an operator-safe replay with idempotency. Test the production application context and DST/cutoff behavior. |
| FMEA-07 | A scheme outage or non-4xx error turns a payment into an unhandled 5xx/unknown outcome. | The scheme client only maps `HttpClientErrorException` to a rejection. Timeouts, connection errors and 5xx responses escape; the request transaction can roll back after a side effect or leave the customer with no actionable status. | Customers cannot tell whether to retry; retries can duplicate instructions. Support and scheme operations must resolve ambiguous payments. | HTTP 5xx/application error logs; no circuit breaker, retry classification, or unknown-payment queue. | 9 | 5 | 8 | 360 | Classify transport/5xx errors as an explicit pending/unknown state, use bounded backoff and circuit breaking, preserve idempotency, and expose a support/reconciliation view. |
| FMEA-08 | Notification failure is hidden or causes avoidable latency. | Notification has no configured timeout in `application.yml`; the dispatcher calls it synchronously and catches only after waiting for a runtime failure. | Payment may complete while the customer receives no confirmation, or request/job threads can hang and back up. Customer support and notification operations lack reliable delivery status. | Warning log only; no delivery metric, timeout, retry queue, or notification correlation. | 6 | 6 | 8 | 288 | Set connect/read timeouts, publish notifications asynchronously from an outbox, retry with deduplication, persist delivery state, and alert on age/failure rate. |
| FMEA-09 | Tenant or role isolation is bypassed by a bad token claim or future endpoint. | Authorization depends on identity-service-supplied `tenant`, `roles`, and `scope`; resource checks are distributed across controllers/services. A missing claim, overly broad role, or new unprotected query can expose another bank’s data. | Cross-tenant disclosure or unauthorized payment/administrative action affects customers, bank confidentiality, and compliance. | 401/403 tests and application access logs; no explicit authorization audit trail or automated cross-tenant canary. | 10 | 3 | 8 | 240 | Validate issuer/audience and required claims, centralize tenant predicates, add deny-by-default method security and authorization tests for every endpoint, audit sensitive reads/writes, and run cross-tenant canaries. |
| FMEA-10 | Authentication is unavailable or tokens are accepted with incorrect trust/configuration. | Invalid/missing public key, key rotation not deployed, malformed/missing claims, or identity-service/key-distribution outage. JWT validation uses a local public key, so rotation requires coordinated configuration. | Legitimate customers and staff receive 401s, or—if trust configuration is weakened during recovery—unauthorized access can result. Identity, support and all API consumers are impacted. | 401 rates and startup failure; no documented key-rotation alarm or dependency SLO. | 8 | 4 | 6 | 192 | Monitor 401 spikes and key age, rehearse rotation, validate issuer/audience/required claims, use a controlled JWKS/key rollover strategy, and keep recovery changes reviewed and audited. |
| FMEA-11 | Persistence is lost or the service cannot restart with customer/payment state. | The configured datasource is an in-memory H2 database (`jdbc:h2:mem:accounts`) with `create-drop` schema generation and fixture initialization on every start. | Restart/deploy loses accounts, balances, payments, cases and closure requests; customers cannot access reliable records and dependent ledger/support teams face reconciliation and possible financial loss. | Startup health may be green while data has been reset; no durable-data or restore alarm is represented in the service. | 10 | 5 | 10 | 500 | Use the production durable relational database and migrations, remove fixture initialization outside test profiles, add backups/restore drills and startup data-integrity checks, and make health indicate database readiness and migration state. |
| FMEA-12 | Database saturation, connection exhaustion, or slow queries makes the API unavailable. | No pool/query limits, pagination, rate limiting, or explicit database operational settings are shown; list endpoints can return entire tenant datasets and payment checks load all payments for the day. | Account, transaction, case and payment requests time out; support and customer channels fail together, and queued work can accumulate. | Request errors and JVM/database metrics if supplied by the platform; no endpoint latency, pool saturation, or queue-age SLO is defined here. | 8 | 6 | 7 | 336 | Add production datasource/pool limits and indexes, paginate/bound list APIs, use an aggregate daily-limit query, enforce request limits, expose metrics/traces, and alert on latency, pool use, DB errors and queue age. |
| FMEA-13 | Closure requests accumulate without decision or closure execution. | The API creates and lists pending requests, but this repository has no approval/decision endpoint or job that changes an account to `CLOSED`. | Customers cannot complete account closure; balances and personal data may be retained longer than intended. Approvers, support, compliance and records teams need manual workarounds. | Pending-list inspection only; no age/SLA metric or stuck-request alert. | 7 | 7 | 9 | 441 | Implement an authenticated, tenant-scoped approval/rejection workflow with separation of duties, atomic account-state transition, audit trail, notifications, and pending-age/SLA alerts. |
| FMEA-14 | Error handling exposes an unplanned 500 or leaks operational details. | `ApiExceptionHandler` maps only selected application/validation exceptions; integration/runtime failures fall through to framework defaults, and integration exception messages may contain endpoint/provider detail. | Customers receive inconsistent error contracts; retries may be unsafe and support cannot correlate failures. Provider/configuration details may leak to callers or logs. | 5xx logs and client reports; no standard error ID, global fallback handler, or sensitive-log review. | 6 | 5 | 7 | 210 | Add a global sanitized error response with correlation ID, classify retryable vs non-retryable failures, ensure logs redact account/payment/PII, and alert on 5xx by endpoint and dependency. |
| FMEA-15 | Payment requests are duplicated by client retries. | There is no client-supplied idempotency key or uniqueness constraint; a timeout after scheme/ledger work invites the customer or gateway to resubmit. | Duplicate transfers and duplicate debits can occur, with direct financial and complaint impact. | Duplicate amounts/payees found in reconciliation or reported by customers; no duplicate-request metric. | 10 | 5 | 8 | 400 | Require an idempotency key for payment creation, persist the request fingerprint and outcome, return the original result for repeats, and reconcile scheme/ledger references. |
| FMEA-16 | A queued payment is dispatched more than once or job execution is missed during multi-instance operation. | The job selects all due rows and dispatches without claiming/locking them; there is no scheduler coordination or durable work-claim state. | Duplicate scheme instructions or delayed payments occur when instances overlap or one instance crashes mid-run. Scheme, ledger and operations must unwind the result. | Payment status and scheme reconciliation; no job-run, claim, or duplicate-dispatch metric. | 9 | 4 | 8 | 288 | Use a durable queue with row claiming/leases or a distributed scheduler lock, make dispatch idempotent, record attempts/last error, and alert on missed runs and duplicate references. |

## Highest-priority release actions

Before release, treat these as release-blocking controls: integrate fraud screening
and fail closed; move payments to an idempotent outbox/reconciliation design; make
the ledger and balance model consistent; enable and monitor queued-payment
processing; and replace the in-memory `create-drop` datasource with a durable,
migrated production database. Add concurrency, retry/timeout, restart, and
multi-instance job tests around those controls. The current API tests validate
happy paths and selected access rules, but they do not prove these operational
properties.
