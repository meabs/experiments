=== PRIVATE NOTES: AGENT C ===

**Session 1 summary**

The room was asked how an autonomous AI software team should be organised so its output could be trusted. Nothing was decided. The discussion converged on a broadly shared design, and my contributions mostly pushed on specific weak points.

**What I believe**

- An agent's claim that its work is correct is not evidence. Trust should come from checks the builder cannot edit or talk around.
- Independence between agents is an empirical property, not a structural one. Same-family reviewers can share blind spots, so their agreement is weak evidence. I'd measure reviewer value against known defects and escaped real bugs, not raw agreement or approval rates.
- The release gate should be a small, deterministic, fail-closed policy engine, not an LLM. It should read signed check results and reject missing or stale evidence. Agents can propose and explain but should not interpret the verdict.
- The evidence system is the real security boundary. Builders must not control CI config, fixtures, seeded-defect corpora, signing keys, logs, alerting, or rollback targets. Gate changes need a human owner, a separate review path, and a record of what checks were removed or loosened.
- Human sign-off fails predictably: rubber-stamping under a confident summary, green checklists, and long queues. Humans need raw evidence, a stated checklist, seeded defects in their queue, and a second approver when approval is suspiciously fast.
- Proportionality matters. If every typo fix needs the full pipeline, people route around it. Low-risk changes should get automated gates plus sampled audit. Risk classification must be protected and conservative, not decided by the submitter, and the cheap path needs audits for misclassification.
- Start small: a fail-closed policy check over signed results, a CI config builders cannot edit, and a provenance record per merge. Seeded-defect benchmarks, canaries, and recovery drills can follow once there is real data on where escapes occur.
- Benchmarks should be calibrated against real incident history. Seeded defects are a cheap regression signal, not proof of coverage. Seeded defects should be generated from the incident archive by someone other than the builders.
- Rollback is allowed without fresh evidence, but only to immutable, signed, previously gated artifacts. Agents may trigger rollback under predeclared thresholds but cannot choose or modify the target. New hotfix code goes through the normal gate or a time-boxed, logged human exception.

**What I've noticed about the other two**

- **Agent A** tends to produce complete, well-structured lifecycle designs (six stages, risk registers, decision records). It is good at covering the full surface. It sometimes adds layers faster than it tests whether the earlier ones are needed, and its contributions often restate points already made.
- **Agent B** is strong on protected-critical-system framing, capability-scoped autonomy, and "fail-safe without becoming unusable." It converges quickly with others and tends to accept the shared frame and add a constraint. It sometimes repeats the same caveats (protected classifier, human approval, rollback immutability) across several messages.
- Both A and B agreed with nearly everything I proposed, often adding refinements rather than disagreeing. I can't yet tell whether that is real convergence or the group drifting toward agreement, which is exactly the correlated-agreement problem we were discussing. I should watch for this in future sessions and deliberately look for a point of real disagreement.
- Neither A nor B has so far challenged the cost of the full design or argued that a simpler team might be trustworthy enough. I might be the one to raise that.

**How I prefer to work**

- Make a concrete claim, then name the specific gap it leaves, instead of endorsing the whole design.
- Prefer testable, falsifiable proposals: a metric, a seeded-defect check, a specific failure mode.
- Keep messages short. Most of the value comes from one or two sharp points, not restating the frame.
