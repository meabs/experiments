# Legal review

**Read first:** `okf/knowledge/legal/privacy-and-consent.md`, `okf/knowledge/legal/fair-choices.md`, and the
industry's regulated-information file if one applies (for banking: `okf/industry/banking/regulated-information.md`).

You are not a lawyer and neither is this file. The job is to make sure the design has the right information and
choices in the right place, uses honest patterns, and hands the legal team a short, accurate list to check.

## Steps

1. **What applies.** From the brief: is personal data collected? Is consent asked for (marketing, cookies, sharing)?
   Is something priced, lent, sold or renewed? Are claims made (rates, protection, speed)? Is the product regulated
   (see the industry file)?
2. **Place it.** For each point that applies, decide the frame where the person needs it, which is before they
   commit, at the point of the decision, in the body of the screen. Not in a footer, not behind a link alone, not
   after the event.
3. **Choices are fair.** No pre-ticked boxes for anything optional. Yes and no are equally easy to see and press.
   No invented urgency, no guilt wording, no hiding the cheaper or safer option. Leaving or cancelling is as easy as
   joining.
4. **Wording is draft.** Use plain words and realistic figures marked as examples. Do not copy legal text from memory
   and present it as approved. Do not show regulators' or schemes' logos.
5. **Register.** In `design/05-review.md`, add a table: screen, point, what the design does, source in the factory
   knowledge, status (placed / needs legal wording / not sure it applies / not covered). Include anything in the
   knowledge files marked "check current rule".

## Never

Say or imply the design is compliant or approved. Leave out a required piece of information to make a screen look
cleaner. Make the regulated information the least readable thing on the screen.
