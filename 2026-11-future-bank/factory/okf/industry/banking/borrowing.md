# Borrowing

Borrowing journeys carry more risk of harm than any other. The design's job is to make the cost and the commitment
impossible to miss, and to make "no" and "not now" safe.

## What people want to know

How much could I borrow? What will it cost me in pounds? Will checking hurt my credit score? What happens if I can't
pay it back? How fast is the decision?

## Overdrafts

- **Say it is borrowing.** "An arranged overdraft is a way to borrow through your current account, up to a limit we
  agree. It's meant for short-term borrowing."
- **Price.** Overdrafts are priced as a single annual interest rate with no fixed daily or monthly fees. Show the
  rate as an **APR marked "representative"** with the variable or fixed status, and next to it a **cost in pounds**:
  "Borrowing £500 for 30 days would cost about £14.80" (example figures, marked as an example).
- **Calculator.** Offer a simple cost calculator: amount and number of days in, cost in pounds out.
- **Eligibility first.** An eligibility check that does not affect the credit score ("a soft search; lenders can't
  see it") before the full application. Say clearly when a step will leave a mark on the credit file.
- **Limit choice.** Let the customer choose a limit up to the amount offered; do not default to the maximum.
- **Arranged versus unarranged.** If the journey mentions going over the limit, explain that payments may be refused
  and what it costs.
- **Alerts.** Mention that the bank sends alerts when the overdraft is used, and where to manage them.

## Any credit application

1. Explain, eligibility check, result of check. 2. Income and outgoings (affordability): ask only what is needed,
explain why, allow estimates, be kind about the questions. 3. The offer: limit, rate, cost example, key terms.
4. Pre-contract information and the agreement are available to read and keep before agreeing. 5. Explicit agreement
(a clear action, not a pre-ticked box). 6. Result and what happens next.

- The offer screen shows the cost at least as prominently as the limit.
- No pressure: no "pre-approved, claim now", no countdowns, no celebratory confetti for getting into debt.
- People usually have 14 days to withdraw from a credit agreement (check current rule); say how to reduce or remove
  the overdraft later, as easily as it was arranged.

## Decisions

| Outcome | Design |
| --- | --- |
| Approved | the limit, rate and cost example again; when it is available; how to change or remove it |
| Lower amount offered | say so plainly, do not present it as full approval; accept or decline equally easy |
| Referred | we need longer; when and how they will hear; nothing to do now |
| Declined | say it kindly and plainly; that the bank used information from the application and credit reference agencies; that they can ask for the reason or a review; that this check has or has not affected their credit file; where to get free help |

## Money worries

Every borrowing journey has a visible route for people who are struggling: a line such as "Worried about money? We
can help, and it won't affect your application to ask", pointing to the bank's support team and to free, independent
debt advice (for example MoneyHelper). Do not hide it at the bottom of the decline screen only.
