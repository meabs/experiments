# Customers in vulnerable circumstances

Anyone can be vulnerable at some point. The FCA describes four drivers: **health** (conditions affecting the ability
to carry out tasks), **life events** (bereavement, job loss, relationship breakdown), **resilience** (low ability to
withstand financial or emotional shocks) and **capability** (low knowledge of money, low confidence, poor literacy,
numeracy or digital skills, English as a second language). Firms are expected to design for these customers, not
treat them as exceptions, and under the Consumer Duty to support customers' understanding and avoid causing
foreseeable harm.

## Design for the hardest moment

For each journey ask: who is doing this on their worst day?

| Journey | Likely circumstances | What helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paying a new payee | being coached by a scammer; in a rush; unfamiliar with bank transfers | specific warnings, a pause, an easy way to stop and talk to the bank |
| Borrowing | money worries, shame, optimism about repaying | cost in pounds, no pressure, visible support and free debt advice, kind decline |
| Lost or stolen card | panic, possibly a victim of crime, away from home | one-action safety, reassurance, a phone number |
| Savings | low confidence with rates and terms | plain explanations of AER, fixed and variable; examples in pounds |
| Any long form | cognitive load, fatigue, a screen reader, a small phone | short steps, save progress, no time pressure |

## Patterns

- **Plain language** at about the reading level of a 9 to 11 year old for key information; explain any term that
  must be kept (APR, AER, arranged overdraft) the first time it appears.
- **Chunking.** One decision per screen; key facts as short lists, not paragraphs.
- **No time pressure.** No countdowns; time-outs warned and extendable; progress saved.
- **A person, easily.** "Get help" on every screen of a journey: chat, phone (with opening hours), and other routes
  (relay services, branch). Do not make people finish or abandon the journey to find it.
- **Tell us once.** Where relevant, an optional, respectful way to say what support they need ("Is there anything
  that would make banking easier for you?") with examples and an assurance about how it is used. Never a mandatory
  disclosure.
- **Third-party help.** Mention trusted-person or power of attorney routes where a customer may not be acting alone.
- **Check understanding without testing.** A review screen in plain words; for risky choices, a specific
  confirmation ("I understand this payment may not be recoverable"), used sparingly.
- **Leaving is as easy as joining.** Cancelling, reducing a limit, closing or complaining take no more effort than
  opening.
- **Money worries.** Signpost the bank's support team and free independent help (MoneyHelper; for debt, organisations
  such as StepChange or Citizens Advice) wherever a customer could be in difficulty.
