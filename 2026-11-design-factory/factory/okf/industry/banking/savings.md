# Savings

## What people want to know, in order

1. What rate will I get, and is it fixed or can it change? 2. Can I get my money out, and does it cost me? 3. How
much do I need to put in, and is there a maximum? 4. Is my money safe? 5. How long does opening take?

Answer these on the product screen, before "Open account". Do not make people open the summary box to find the rate.

## Rates

- Show the **AER** (annual equivalent rate: what the rate would be if interest was paid and added once a year, so
  accounts can be compared) and the **gross** rate (before tax), and say whether the rate is **variable** or
  **fixed**, and for how long.
- If there is a bonus or introductory rate, show the rate after it ends and the date, with equal prominence.
- Say when interest is paid (monthly, yearly) and into which account.
- An example in pounds helps: what £1,000 would become after 12 months.

## The summary box

Savings accounts are presented with a standard summary box so they can be compared. It is a table with these
headings, in this order, and nothing else inside it:

1. Account name
2. What is the interest rate?
3. Can [the bank] change the interest rate?
4. What would the estimated balance be after 12 months based on a £1,000 deposit?
5. How do I open and manage my account?
6. Can I withdraw money?
7. Additional information

The rate itself must be in the box, not behind a link. Show the box in the journey before the customer commits, as
real text (a table or description list), not an image or a download only. On a phone, stack each question and
answer. (Headings 3 to 7 are from the author's knowledge of the standard form; confirm against BCOBS 2 Annex 1.)

## Protection

Eligible deposits with a UK-authorised bank are protected by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) up to
£120,000 per person, per authorised firm (from 1 December 2025; £240,000 for joint accounts). Say it in a line near
the decision. The limit covers all the person's accounts with that firm together. Do not use the FSCS logo in a
prototype, and do not say "100% safe" or "guaranteed".

## Opening

- For an existing customer: choose the account, read the summary, choose how much to move in and from where, review,
  open. Aim for under two minutes.
- Agreeing to terms is an explicit action; the terms and summary are available to keep (a link to save or email).
- Customers usually have 14 days to change their mind after opening without penalty (not for all fixed-rate
  accounts; check current rule). Say so on the review or result screen.
- Result: account name, new account details, the rate, when the first interest is paid, what to do next (add money,
  set up a regular payment).

## Withdrawals and limits

State plainly: instant access or notice period; any limit on the number of withdrawals and what happens to the rate
if exceeded; minimum and maximum balance. Penalties are shown in pounds for an example, not only as "90 days'
interest".

## Fair presentation

No countdowns or "rates may fall soon". Compare with the customer's current account rate honestly. Tax: one line
that interest may be taxable is enough; do not give tax advice.
