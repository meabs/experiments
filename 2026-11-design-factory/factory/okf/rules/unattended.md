# Working without a person

Normally a designer reads each stage's file and says "go on" or asks for changes. When you are run on your own
(a single request, no one answering), do this instead:

1. Do not stop to ask. Write each question in the brief file under "Open questions", choose the most reasonable
   answer, and record it under "Assumptions" with one line on why.
2. Still write every stage file, in order, before building. Keep them short: the brief, research, storyboard and
   direction files together should rarely pass 1,200 words.
3. At the end of each stage, run the stage's check from the workflow yourself and fix what fails before moving on.
4. Choose one visual direction and commit to it. Do not deliver several alternatives unless the brief asks.
5. Spend most of the effort on the prototype and the critique. A finished, polished prototype with short notes beats
   long notes and a rough prototype.
6. Finish with a short summary: what was built, the assumptions that most need a person's decision, and checks not
   run.
