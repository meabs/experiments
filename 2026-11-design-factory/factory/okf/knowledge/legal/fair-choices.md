# Fair choices, clear prices and equal access

Design guidance, not legal advice.

## Patterns to keep out

| Pattern | Looks like | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hidden or late costs (drip pricing) | a fee that first appears at the last step | show the full price, including unavoidable fees, as soon as a price is shown |
| Pre-selection | optional extras or consents already ticked | off by default |
| Unequal choices | a bright "Accept" and a grey text "No thanks" | equal weight for choices that are the person's to make |
| Guilt or pressure wording | "No, I don't care about my savings" | neutral labels: "Not now" |
| False urgency or scarcity | countdowns, "only 2 left" without basis | real deadlines only, stated plainly |
| Obstruction (sludge) | joining takes one tap, leaving takes a phone call | leaving, cancelling, downgrading and complaining are as easy as joining |
| Nagging | the same prompt on every visit | ask once, remember the answer |
| Disguised advertising | a promotion styled as a warning or a system message | promotions look like promotions |
| Buried information | key terms in grey small print or behind a link | key terms in the body, before the decision |
| Forced continuity | a free period that turns into charges silently | say the date and amount, remind before, cancel easily |

## Prices and commitments

- Before the person commits: what they get, the total cost and how it is calculated, how long it lasts, how to
  cancel, and any right to change their mind.
- The commit button makes the obligation clear ("Open account", "Pay £12.00").
- Afterwards: a confirmation they can keep (a reference, a summary, where to find it later).

## Claims

Anything the screen claims (rates, speed, "free", "protected", "instant") must be true in the way an ordinary
reader would understand it, with the conditions next to the claim, not in a footnote.

## Equal access

Service providers must make reasonable adjustments so disabled people are not put at a substantial disadvantage
(Equality Act 2010). For digital journeys, working to WCAG 2.2 AA and offering another way to complete the task
(phone, chat, branch, relay services) is the practical baseline. So: accessibility defects are legal risk, and every
journey needs a visible route to help from a person.
