# Content design

**Read first:** `okf/rules/house-rules.md` (rules 8 and 9);
Also `skills/impeccable/reference/clarify.md` for labels, errors and empty states; the industry file on money and trust if one applies.

## Steps

1. **Headings say the job.** "Who are you paying?" not "Payee details". Questions work well for steps.
2. **Buttons say what happens.** Verb plus object: "Send £250.00", "Freeze card", "Check details". Never "Submit",
   "OK" or "Continue" where a more exact word exists. The confirm button repeats the consequence.
3. **Labels and hints.** Labels are nouns people recognise ("Sort code"). Hints say the format or where to find it
   ("6 digits, on their card or statement"). Do not put the hint in the placeholder.
4. **Errors: what happened, why if useful, what to do.** "Enter a sort code with 6 digits", not "Invalid input". No
   blame, no jokes, no codes on their own. For system failures say whether anything was taken or changed.
5. **Results and waiting.** Say what happened, what happens next and when: "£250.00 sent to Sam Okafor. It usually
   arrives within 2 hours." For pending states say what the person should and should not do ("Don't send it again").
6. **Read every screen aloud**, top to bottom. Cut words that add nothing, replace jargon (see the industry
   glossary), split sentences over 20 words. Aim for a reading age of about 9 to 11 for anything important.
7. **Numbers.** Amounts with currency sign and two decimal places; dates in full; times with am or pm; never rely on
   "today" or "tomorrow" alone for money movements.

## Tone

Calm, exact, on the person's side. More careful as the stakes rise. No exclamation marks about money. No "Oops".
"We" for the organisation, "you" for the person.
