# Concept exploration

**Read first:** `design/01-brief.md` and `design/02-research.md`. Do **not** read the industry pack, the legal files or the
conventions in the knowledge files yet: they come after the concept is chosen (stages 3 to 6) and check it. Exploring
under every rule at once produces the category's average.
**Writes:** `design/02b-concepts.md` (about 300 words).

## Steps

1. **Opportunities.** From the research, list 3 to 5 opportunities: a need nobody serves well, or a shift that is coming.
   Each is one line: "People who ... need ..., and today ...".
2. **Three concepts.** Write three concepts that differ in *what the product is for*, not in colour or layout. For each:
   - the idea in one sentence;
   - who it is for and what changes for them;
   - **the thing no current product in the category does** (if you cannot name it, it is not a concept, it is a
     feature list);
   - the visual world it lives in: a few words, and one named real product or place outside the category that it
     should feel like.
3. **Choose one** and write why in two lines. Prefer the concept a reviewer would remember a week later, over the one
   with fewest risks. Risks are handled in the review (stage 6); do not pick the safe concept to avoid them.
4. **Name its risks** you can already see (safety, legal, trust, accessibility, feasibility) as a list for the review to
   check. Do not solve them by removing the concept; plan to solve them in how it is built.
5. **State the test.** One sentence: "If this concept is built well, a user could ... that they cannot in any current
   product." The review (stage 6) checks that the built design still passes this test.

## Avoid

- Three versions of the same idea in different colours.
- A concept that is "the usual screens plus an assistant".
- Dropping the distinctive part because a convention or rule seems to forbid it. Conventions are defaults. Legal and
  safety requirements are not, but they usually constrain how, not whether.

## Done when

Three concepts that differ in purpose, one chosen with a reason, the thing it does that others do not, its visible risks
for the review, and its test.
